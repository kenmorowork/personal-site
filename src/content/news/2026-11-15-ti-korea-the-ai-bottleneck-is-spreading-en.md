Texas Instruments Korea CEO Park Joong-seo says AI data-centre demand is tightening supply not only of memory and advanced nodes but also of power, embedded and cooling-control chips on mature 28–130nm nodes, which foundries are reluctant to expand because legacy wafers fetch $1,000–2,000 against $40,000–50,000 for advanced nodes (ChosunBiz interview, published 15 Nov 2026 via BigGo). TI's Q3 2026 revenue was $6.055bn, up 28% y/y, with data-centre revenue up about 95% y/y; TI cites 800V DC power delivery at 85.2% efficiency versus 84.6% for a 48V structure, seen reaching 86.7% after 2027, and in-house 300mm lines yielding 2.2–2.3x a 200mm wafer at roughly 30% lower chip cost.

**Why it matters:** The shortage narrative widens from HBM and leading-edge logic to the mature-node power and control chips every AI rack also needs — a read-across for Japanese power-semi and FA names levered to data-centre power chains.
