The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on Monday 16 Nov requiring licences for exports of advanced AI chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and requiring data centres to obtain a licence before giving companies headquartered in China or other restricted destinations remote access to advanced chips. Data centres already serving such companies have 90 days from the rule's effective date to obtain a licence; comments are due in 60 days. The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. A separate summary of the BIS rule text puts the remote-access threshold at more than 12 million aggregate TPP of compute — roughly 760 H100s or about 330 B200s. Sources: Nextgov, 16 Nov 2026; BIS rule summary via Beri, 16 Nov 2026.

**Why it matters:** The control now follows the compute, not just the chip: Southeast Asian data-centre build-outs — a core AIDC demand pool — face licensing friction, and the 90-day clock is the first dated checkpoint.
