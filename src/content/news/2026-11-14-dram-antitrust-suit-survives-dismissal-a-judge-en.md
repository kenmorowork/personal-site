US District Judge Noël Wise (Northern District of California) on 14 Nov denied in full Samsung, SK hynix and Micron's joint motion to dismiss a proposed class action alleging the three conspired from late 2022 to restrict conventional DRAM supply while shifting wafer capacity to HBM, in violation of Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The trio held over 91% of global DRAM revenue in Q3 2025; the complaint cites a 171.8% year-on-year rise in conventional DRAM prices by Q3 2025 and notes HBM uses roughly 3–4 times the wafer area per gigabyte of DDR5. The court accepted five factors as supporting a conspiracy inference, including long-term HBM lock-ups, refusals of conventional-DRAM long-term contracts, Micron's Crucial closure and near-identical customer vetting adopted in January 2026. Answers are due 7 Dec 2026 and a case-management conference is set for 12 Jan 2027; the allegations are unproven and were accepted as true only for the dismissal test.

**Why it matters:** Discovery is the dated risk: internal capacity and pricing documents from the HBM pivot now become disclosable, against the same three names that carry Korea's margin-debt concentration.
