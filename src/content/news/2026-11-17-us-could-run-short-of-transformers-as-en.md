A Johns Hopkins University study published 17 Nov in Cell Reports Sustainability finds the US could run short of copper as early as next year and of transformers and other grid equipment soon after. Under high power-demand growth, the US would be almost 15% short of transformers and about 30% short of other critical grid equipment by 2030, with as much as two-thirds of the expected shortages driven by data centres; trade disruption could deepen the transformer shortfall to about 25%. Under a baseline demand case, transformer supply would be sufficient by 2030 but other equipment would still be about 16% short. Source: Johns Hopkins University Hub, 17 Nov 2026.

**Why it matters:** A quantified read on the grid bottleneck: up to two-thirds of the expected transformer and grid-equipment shortfall is data-centre-driven, which supports the multi-year order visibility behind Hitachi Energy, GE Vernova and the transformer names tracked in this dashboard.
