Advantest is the world's largest maker of semiconductor test equipment, duopolist with Teradyne in SoC testers (V93000 platform) and the leader in memory testers, where HBM test demand has exploded. FY3/2026 (IFRS) sales were ¥1,128,610mn (+44.8%) with operating profit of ¥499,120mn — a 44.2% margin — and net income of ¥375,353mn, all records. The run has continued: H1 FY3/2027 sales were ¥845.2bn (+60.5%) at a 54.0% OP margin (calculated), and **FY3/2027 guidance has been raised twice — from sales ¥1,420,000mn / OP ¥627,500mn in April 2026 to ¥1,863,000mn / ¥967,000mn on 28 October 2026** — with management naming CPU test demand from agentic AI as a 'third layer' of growth on top of GPUs and custom ASICs. Strengths: **a company-estimated SoC tester share of 66% (CY2025, from 56% in CY2024)**, memory tester sales of ¥401.0bn in FY3/2026 (+141%) with HBM about half, Asia at 91.8% of sales by customer location, and a buyback of up to ¥280bn announced with the Q2 print. Weaknesses: H1's margin was flattered by pre-rise DRAM inventory and a one-time write-down reversal (unquantified), H2 OP margin is guided only to the low-50% range, the capacity target (10,000 SoC systems/year) is being pulled forward against a TAM the company itself sizes at $13.0–14.5bn for CY2026, orders/backlog are never disclosed, and the shares — ¥27,205 after an 11.1% fall on 16 November 2026 — still trade on 52.8x FY3/2026 diluted EPS (calculated). Rating and price target are Under Review; this report organises the reported record and the questions it raises — the investment judgement is the reader's.

## Key points

- **Guidance: +54% on OP in seven months.** FY3/2027 OP guidance went ¥627,500mn (27 Apr 2026) → ¥846,000mn (29 Jul 2026) → ¥967,000mn (28 Oct 2026), on sales of ¥1,863,000mn and net income of ¥741,500mn. **The October raise's biggest single factor, per the company, was CPU testing demand associated with agentic AI — a third demand layer on top of GPU and custom ASIC test**.
- **H1 margin of 54.0% is not the run-rate.** H1 FY3/2027 OP margin was 54.0% (calculated), helped by pre-rise DRAM inventory and a one-time inventory write-down reversal (company Q&A; size not disclosed). H2 is guided to a low-50% OP margin; **the implied H2 (calculated) is sales ¥1,017,800mn and OP ¥510,200mn at 50.1%**.
- **Share and capacity are the debate now.** **Company-estimated SoC tester share rose 56% (CY2024) → 66% (CY2025), with further gains expected**. SoC tester capacity above 5,000 systems/year is achieved; the 10,000/year target — originally end-FY2028 — 'must be brought forward significantly', and 20,000/year is under internal discussion (company Q&A). At 66% share, dual-sourcing pressure from hyperscaler customers is the standing counter-argument, and it is not addressed in disclosure.
- **MTP3 has been outrun; MTP4 lands ~May 2027.** The third mid-term plan (FY2024–26, revised Oct 2025) targeted three-year averages of sales ¥835–930bn, OP margin 33–36% and ROIC 34–39% (company plan). **The FY3/2027 guide alone is roughly double the plan's average sales range at a 51.9% implied OP margin (calculated)**. What MTP4 dares to promise for CY2028–29 is the next catalyst.
- **Disclosure gaps are structural.** **Advantest discloses no order intake or backlog total, no R&D total and no headcount in the tanshin (register S8)**; regional sales are annual-only and Asia is a single bucket (91.8% of FY3/2026 sales). The SoC/memory split of Test System sales is given in annual materials (FY3/2026 memory tester sales ¥401.0bn) but was not disclosed for H1 FY3/2027 in the sources read.

## 3. Business description

Advantest (founded 1954 as Takeda Riken; renamed Advantest in 1985) develops, manufactures and services semiconductor test equipment — the systems that verify every chip functions and meets specification before shipment. It reports two segments on the current basis (from Q1 FY3/2026): Test Systems and Services & Other (Chapter 5). Leadership is international: Douglas Lefever is Representative Director, Senior Executive Officer and Group CEO (Group CEO since April 2024), and Koichi Tsukui is Representative Director, Senior Executive Officer and President, Group COO (company appointments notice, February 2026; Q2 FY3/2027 call).

## Products — SoC and memory testers, handlers, interfaces

Test Systems covers: SoC test systems on the V93000 platform (the franchise — test for logic, HPC/AI accelerators, CPUs, APs and mixed-signal devices, increasingly described by the company as an industry standard in HPC); memory test systems for DRAM (including HBM) and non-volatile memory; test handlers that feed devices to testers; device interface boards and other peripherals; and system-level test. Services & Other covers support, maintenance, used-equipment and related services on the installed base. FY3/2026 memory tester sales were ¥401.0bn (+141% YoY) with HBM about half of the memory business (company IR) — implying SoC and other Test System lines of roughly ¥618bn (calculated against Test System sales of ¥1,019,380mn). New demand pools management cites: CPU test for agentic AI, HBM known-good-die/stacked-die insertions, and CPO (co-packaged optics) test, a market management itself sizes at ~$1bn growing rapidly (interview pack).

## Customers and demand drivers

Customers are foundries, OSATs, IDMs and fabless chip makers — in practice the AI-accelerator supply chain (GPU and custom-ASIC designers, their foundry and OSAT partners) and the HBM makers. Demand drivers: device complexity and test content per chip (rising with HPC/AI parts), HBM stack counts and test insertions, and wafer/test capacity additions in Taiwan, Korea and increasingly the US. Customer concentration is not disclosed in the tanshin (未取得). The company sizes the CY2026 tester market at $13.0–14.5bn in total, with the SoC tester market at ~$11.5bn (company Q&A, unchanged at the October print).

## Group structure and footprint

Production and development are centred in Japan with subsidiaries across the customer regions (Advantest America and Asian subsidiaries are material operating units; the Group CEO is US-based by background — President of Advantest America before group roles, per the company management page). The binding operational constraint management discusses is SoC tester production capacity: more than 5,000 systems/year achieved, 10,000/year being pulled forward from end-FY2028, 20,000/year under discussion. Headcount is not disclosed in the tanshin (register S8).

## Strategy as stated by the company

Stated strategy: (i) convert the V93000's HPC position into a durable platform standard and take SoC share from 66% toward 70%+; (ii) build capacity ahead of demand (10,000 systems/year early; 20,000 under discussion); (iii) grow memory test with HBM insertions and system-level test; (iv) defend margin against DRAM input-cost inflation through configuration, licensing and instrumentation rather than headline price increases; and (v) return cash — the ¥280bn buyback and a raised interim dividend (¥49) accompanied the October raise. The financial frame is MTP3 (Chapter 7), already outrun, with MTP4 due around May 2027.

## 4. Market and value chain

Test is the segment of WFE where Advantest and Teradyne (US) form a duopoly in SoC test, with Advantest also leading memory test against Teradyne and Korean suppliers (interview pack; company statements). The company's CY2026 tester TAM of $13.0–14.5bn (SoC ~$11.5bn) against its FY3/2027 sales guide of ¥1,863bn (~$12.4bn at the company's ¥150/$ assumption, calculated) implies Advantest expects to take a very large share of the incremental market — consistent with its claim that SoC share rose from 56% (CY2024) to 66% (CY2025) with further gains expected in CY2026–27. The interview pack records the two standing challenges: ==at 66%+ share, hyperscaler customers have every incentive to dual-source (no disclosure addresses qualification of a second platform at top accounts), and the TAM itself has compounded at ~50% a year for several years on management's account — the capacity plan is priced off that sentence==, and a quantitative CY2027 TAM outlook was promised only for the January 2027 Q3 briefing.

Below the tester duopoly, the adjacent test markets Advantest is expanding into are small but growing. The FIELDNOTES semiconductor test report compiles third-party estimates (all secondary, definitions varying): the broad system-level test market at about $6.68bn in 2025 growing to about $15.6bn by 2035 (SNS Insider, CAGR 8.85%), the narrower standalone SLT system market at about $1.79bn in 2025 (PW Consulting), and the test and burn-in socket market at about $1.8bn in 2025 rising to about $2.9bn by 2034 (Dataintelo, CAGR 5.7%). Set against the company's CY2026 tester TAM of $13.0-14.5bn, these pools explain why system-level test and interfaces feature in the growth narrative — and why they cannot yet move group numbers at FY3/26's ¥1,128,610mn sales scale.

## 5. Segments and regions

Segment presentation changed at Q1 FY3/2026: two segments (Test Systems / Services & Other) replace the three of the earlier era (Semiconductor & Component Test Systems / Mechatronics / Services, Support & Other). Segment profit is operating profit BEFORE stock-compensation expense on both bases (register S3/S12). The blocks are presented separately and never spliced. Regional sales (customer location) are annual-only in the tanshin; regional profit is not disclosed.

The old-basis history gives the current two-segment structure a measurable past. On the three-segment basis, Semiconductor & Component Test Systems earned a pre-stock-compensation margin of 16.4% in FY3/17, peaked at 30.8% in FY3/19, and reached 40.3% in FY3/23 (¥163,186mn on ¥404,213mn of sales) before the FY3/24 downturn that the draft's Exhibit 2 picks up. Mechatronics was loss-making in FY3/17-20 (segment losses of ¥1,529mn, ¥2,738mn, ¥712mn and ¥497mn) and only turned profitable in FY3/21 — the business now inside Test Systems was the weak leg of the old structure for four consecutive years. Regionally, Asia was already 73.2% of sales in FY3/17 (¥114,123mn of ¥155,916mn) and 85.6% by FY3/23, so the 91.8% Asia share of FY3/26 is the end of a decade-long drift, not a sudden concentration.
