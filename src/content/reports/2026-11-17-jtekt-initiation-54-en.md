JTEKT is the Toyota group's steering and driveline supplier (formed from Koyo Seiko and Toyoda Machine Works), reporting three segments — Automotive, Industrial & Bearings, and Machine Tools (Toyoda brand) — and managing itself on 'business profit' (revenue − cost of sales − SG&A), with structural-reform and other items sitting between business profit and IFRS operating profit. In FY3/2026 revenue was ¥1,924,950mn, business profit ¥75,367mn (3.9%, calculated as the segment sum), but operating profit only ¥24,847mn (1.3%) after ¥50,834mn of net other operating expenses — ¥41,786mn of it in Q4 alone — and owners' net income was ¥11,974mn (EPS ¥37.60). FY3/2027 guidance (revised 29 October 2026): revenue ¥1,920,000mn, business profit ¥95,000mn, OP ¥75,000mn, owners' NI ¥50,000mn, EPS ¥157.06, dividend ¥70. Strengths: (1) the underlying business is inflecting — **H1 FY3/2027 business profit rose 52.8% to ¥50.7bn (5.2% margin) on European and North American structural reform**, North American loss-cost reduction and cost improvement, and the revenue/business-profit guide was raised at H1; (2) Machine Tools is a genuinely good business inside the group — ¥17,440mn of business profit at an 8.3% margin in FY3/2026, H1 margin 8.8%, with the JTEKT Engineering absorption (1 April 2027) adding after-service weight; (3) Automotive business profit nearly doubled in H1 (+96.8% to ¥32.3bn) despite lower China sales; (4) steer-by-wire and Pairdriver™ have achieved first adoption in mass-produced vehicles (JTEKT Report 2026) — a real option on the post-2030 steering market; (5) cash generation is solid through the reform (operating cash flow ¥108,495mn in FY3/2026; debt down to ¥217,122mn against cash of ¥137,550mn); (6) the diluted share count fell ~6% in FY3/2026 to 318.5mn, so the guided EPS recovery (¥37.60 → ¥157.06) is partly a denominator story working in shareholders' favour. Weaknesses: (1) **the reform bill keeps growing — other expenses were ¥21.7bn cumulative in H1 FY3/2027 (vs ¥9.3bn a year earlier), the FY3/2026 charge was ¥50.8bn**, and the OP guide was held flat at the H1 revision explicitly because of updated structural-reform costs (a loss on the European automotive business transfer); (2) a lost decade on the IFRS OP line — OP margin has been 1.0–3.3% for seven straight years (FY3/2020–26) versus 5.6–5.9% in FY3/2017–18; (3) the Second Mid-Term Plan's final-year targets (business-profit ratio 5–6%, revenue ¥2.0tn, ROE 7–8%) will be missed on the guide (4.9% calculated; ¥1,920bn), with portfolio exits shrinking revenue by design; (4) Europe has been the chronic loss centre — the OEM automotive business (seven subsidiaries) was transferred only in August 2026 and a European return to profit is not expected until FY3/2028; (5) China revenue has fallen from ¥198,551mn (FY3/2022) to ¥150,435mn (FY3/2026), 7.8% of sales, with steering and driveline still declining there; (6) disclosure is thin where it matters — no orders/backlog, no R&D or employee figures in the tanshin, no steer-by-wire revenue, no Toyota-group concentration figure, and no Q&A was published at the Q2 print (slides only). Rating and price target are Under Review placeholders (see Basis of preparation).

## Key points

- **Two profit lines, two different companies** JTEKT's business profit (¥75,367mn in FY3/2026, segment sum) measures the trading business; IFRS operating profit (¥24,847mn) is what remains after ¥50,834mn of net other operating expenses — overwhelmingly European structural reform, ¥41,786mn of it booked in Q4 FY3/2026, which pushed Q4 standalone OP to a ¥12,955mn loss. The FY3/2027 guide embeds a further ¥20,000mn gap (BP ¥95,000mn vs OP ¥75,000mn). **Every JTEKT debate is really about when the gap closes**.
- **H1: the trading business delivered** H1 FY3/2027 revenue ¥970.8bn (+4.4%) despite the revenue-reducing effect of the European exits; **business profit ¥50.7bn (+52.8%) — Automotive ¥32.3bn (+96.8%, 4.7% margin), Industrial & Bearings ¥8.5bn (4.8%), Machine Tools ¥9.5bn (8.8%)**. Operating profit was ¥36.0bn (+38.4%) after cumulative other expenses of ¥21.7bn. Revenue and business-profit guidance were raised; OP, PBT and NI were deliberately not.
- **Why OP guidance stayed flat** The 29 October 2026 revision notice states it plainly: revenue and business profit will exceed the previous forecast on H1 results, revised FX assumptions and market trends, but **OP and below are unchanged because a loss on the transfer of the European automotive business was recorded**, among other factors. The transfer (seven consolidated subsidiaries, OEM-customer business) completed in August 2026; Europe is expected to return to profit in FY3/2028 (company, FY3/2026 Q&A).
- **The mid-term plan ends below its own band** The Second Mid-Term Plan (FY3/2025–27) targeted a 5–6% business-profit ratio, ¥2.0tn of revenue and 7–8% ROE in its final year. **The FY3/2027 guide implies 4.9% (calculated) and ¥1,920bn** — a miss the company attributes to the environment and to portfolio exits that shrink revenue by design. The 2030 Vision (ROE 10%, PBR 1.5×, business-profit ratio >8%, revenue >¥2.0tn) is the next anchor; the Third MT Plan is in preparation with steer-by-wire, North America FA and a profitable Europe as opening themes.
- **Steer-by-wire: adopted, unquantified** Steer-by-wire and Pairdriver™ have their first mass-production adoption (JTEKT Report 2026), and management expects the market to grow rapidly from 2030 with autonomous driving and BEVs. **No revenue, order value or per-vehicle content is disclosed (未取得） — the option is real but currently unpriceable from public disclosure**, and defending steering share as EPS commoditises is the franchise question for the Third MT Plan.

## H1 FY3/2027 results

H1 (April–September 2026): revenue ¥970.8bn (+4.4% YoY), business profit ¥50.7bn (+52.8%; 5.2% margin, calculated), operating profit ¥36.0bn (+38.4%), PBT ¥33.2bn (+40.3%), owners' net profit ¥21.8bn (+78.8%). **Cumulative other expenses rose to ¥21.7bn (from ¥9.3bn a year earlier)** — the net business-profit-to-OP gap is ¥14.7bn (calculated). ## 3. Business description

JTEKT (headquartered in Kariya, Aichi) was formed in 2006 from Koyo Seiko (bearings, steering) and Toyoda Machine Works (machine tools, driveline). It is a core Toyota-group supplier. President & CEO is Yoshihito Kondo (since June 2024; a Toyota production-engineering veteran, re-elected at the June 2026 AGM); Kazuyuki Kamiya is CFO. Segment profit is business profit — revenue less cost of sales and SG&A, after HQ cost allocation (register S3) — and the bridge from segment profit to IFRS operating profit is other operating income/expenses, which is where structural reform lives. Reportable segments were reorganised in FY3/2022 from the old two (Machine Parts & Components / Machine Tools) to the current three; the history block keeps the old basis for FY3/2017–21 and the two are never mapped into each other.

## Automotive

Steering systems (EPS, and now steer-by-wire) and driveline components (CVJs, hubs, pumps). FY3/2026 sales ¥1,367,005mn (71% of group), business profit ¥46,717mn (3.4%) — the margin recovered from 1.5% in FY3/2022 on reform and cost improvement, and H1 FY3/2027 printed 4.7%. Steering is the technology battleground: steer-by-wire and Pairdriver™ have achieved first adoption in mass-produced vehicles (JTEKT Report 2026); the company expects the market to grow rapidly from 2030. No steer-by-wire revenue is disclosed.

## Industrial & Bearings

Bearings and industrial components (the Koyo heritage). FY3/2026 sales ¥347,067mn, business profit ¥11,210mn (3.2%) — margin has compressed from 5.3% (FY3/2022) despite flat sales, absorbing the prior-year transfer of the European needle-roller-bearing business and, in H1 FY3/2027, a direct US tariff impact. The pack benchmarks the gap: NSK's industrial-machinery segment printed an 8.6% margin in the same H1 — mix, exits, tariffs and price are the candidate explanations, none yet decomposed by the company.

The margin gap against the closest domestic peer is now quantified in the company's own disclosure window. In H1 FY3/2027 Industrial & Bearings earned a 4.8% business-profit margin (¥8.5bn on ¥179.2bn), while NSK's industrial-machinery segment printed 8.6% in the same half. The candidate explanations are mix, the prior-year transfer of the European needle-roller-bearing business, the direct US tariff impact the segment absorbed in H1, and price — but the company has not decomposed the gap, and on the full-year history the segment's margin has halved from 5.3% in FY3/2022 (¥16,391mn) to 3.2% in FY3/2026 (¥11,210mn) on broadly flat sales, so the burden of the explanation sits with mix and price rather than volume.

## Machine Tools

Toyoda-brand machining centres, grinders and FA systems. The smallest segment and the best margin: FY3/2026 sales ¥210,877mn, business profit ¥17,440mn (8.3%); H1 FY3/2027 ¥107.0bn / ¥9.5bn (8.8%) with growth only in North America (Japan, Europe and China all down). The absorption of JTEKT Engineering (1 April 2027) consolidates after-service — the 'solution provider' mix shift on which the 2030 Vision's >8% group business-profit ratio partly depends. Service revenue and margin are not disclosed.

Machine Tools is the group's margin carrier on its smallest base, and its growth is geographically narrow. In H1 FY3/2027 segment revenue rose 7.8% to ¥107.0bn and business profit 5.9% to ¥9.5bn (an 8.8% margin), but the regional colour in the 4 November 2026 presentation is North America sharply up with Japan, Europe and China all down. The absorption of wholly-owned JTEKT Engineering on 1 April 2027, disclosed with the Q2 results, is intended to consolidate machine-tool after-service capability — the service and solutions mix on which the 2030 Vision's business-profit ratio above 8% partly depends — yet neither machine-tool service revenue nor its margin is disclosed, so the mix shift has no published baseline.

## 4. Market and value chain

JTEKT sits at the intersection of three markets. Steering: a global oligopoly (JTEKT, Bosch, Nexteer, NSK, ZF, thyssenkrupp) where the EPS-to-steer-by-wire transition resets share; JTEKT's first mass-production SbW adoption is its claim to a seat in the post-2030 market. Bearings: the NSK–NTN integration (basic agreement May 2026) makes JTEKT the standalone third pole in Japan — a scale/procurement question the pack raises directly, since JTEKT's tribology and elemental technologies are shared across its segments. Machine tools: Toyoda competes with DMG Mori, Yamazaki Mazak and Okuma, with North American (data-centre/FA) demand currently carrying the segment. ==Across all three, the Toyota relationship is both volume guarantee and pricing constraint — tariff and inflation cost recovery is negotiated with customers, not market-set==, and customer concentration is not disclosed (未取得）.

The NSK–NTN integration changes JTEKT's competitive frame in bearings directly. The basic agreement of 12 May 2026 would combine the second- and third-largest Japanese bearing makers into the world's largest bearing group by sales, leaving JTEKT as the standalone third pole in Japan; the interview pack records that no customer dual-sourcing or supply-security response had been disclosed as of November 2026, and that JTEKT's tribology and elemental technologies are shared across steering, driveline and bearings, so a procurement-scale disadvantage in bearings would not stay inside one segment. In steering, the technology reset is dated by the company itself: JTEKT expects the steer-by-wire market to grow rapidly from 2030 with autonomous driving and BEV adoption, and its first mass-production adoption of steer-by-wire and Pairdriver™ (JTEKT Report 2026) is its claim to share in that phase — with revenue, order value and per-vehicle content still undisclosed.

FY3/2026: Japan ¥732,640mn (38.1%), North America ¥525,921mn (27.3%; USA ¥428,912mn, other ¥97,009mn), Asia & Oceania ¥421,176mn (21.9%; China ¥150,435mn, other ¥270,741mn), Europe ¥199,850mn (10.4%), Other ¥45,361mn (2.4%).
