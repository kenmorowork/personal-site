The Swiss-type lathe specialist, earning record profit in China. Tsugami Corporation (6101, TSE Prime) is a precision machine-tool builder centred on CNC precision automatic lathes — sliding-headstock, or Swiss-type, lathes that turn small, slender, high-tolerance parts — supplemented by grinding machines, machining centres, thread-rolling and special-purpose machines. Revenue rose 20.2% YoY to ¥129,140mn in FY3/26 and **operating profit rose 54.9% to ¥36,102mn (28.0% margin)**, both records, and the company raised FY3/27 company guidance at Q2 (12 November 2026) to revenue ¥160,000mn and operating profit ¥48,000mn (Company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 12 November 2026). Strengths: a dominant, locally manufactured position in China — the China entity-location segment delivered ¥103,476mn of sales (80.1% of consolidated) at a 30.1% segment margin in FY3/26 and is anchored by Hong Kong-listed Precision Tsugami (China) Corporation Limited (HKEX: 1651), in which Tsugami held about 65.9% at 31 March 2026 — a balance sheet with ¥42,180mn of cash against ¥9,010mn of borrowings at end-FY3/26, and a dividend raised every year in the sheet period (¥40 in FY3/22 to ¥85 in FY3/26, ¥122 forecast for FY3/27). Weaknesses: extreme geographic concentration — **79.9% of FY3/26 sales were to customers in China** — a large non-controlling interest that absorbed ¥7,573mn (31.1%) of FY3/26 net income, a loss-making or near-breakeven record in Japan, India and Korea in several recent years, and disclosure that omits orders, backlog, R&D and unit volumes in the tanshin, limiting visibility.

## Key points

- **Record FY3/26, guidance raised again.** FY3/26 revenue ¥129,140mn (+20.2% YoY), operating profit ¥36,102mn (+54.9%), net income attributable to owners ¥16,745mn (+53.6%) and basic EPS ¥361.20. H1 FY3/27 revenue was ¥81,207mn (+35.2% YoY) and **operating profit ¥25,220mn (+65.8%)**, described by the company as all-time highs for a half year, and full-year company guidance was raised on 12 November 2026 to revenue ¥160,000mn / operating profit ¥48,000mn / owners' net income ¥22,500mn from ¥145,000mn / ¥36,500mn / ¥17,000mn initially (Company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 12 November 2026).
- **China is the business.** On an entity-location basis the China segment was **¥103,476mn of sales and ¥31,155mn of segment profit in FY3/26 (30.1% margin)**, versus Japan at ¥16,439mn and ¥2,328mn. On a customer-location basis China took ¥103,185mn (79.9%) of sales. Automatic lathes were ¥108,621mn (84.1%) of product-line revenue (Company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive), FY3/26).
- **India is the second engine, still small.** **India segment sales rose 46.0% to ¥6,819mn** in FY3/26 and segment profit turned positive at ¥347mn (5.1% margin) after a ¥310mn loss in FY3/25. The new Chennai (Oragadam) machining/assembly plant and foundry — inaugurated in December 2025, about ¥3.6bn / Rs300 crore invested, 3,000 machines a year of assembly capacity and 6,000 tonnes a year of foundry capacity — is the capacity behind the ramp (company integrated-report material and press reports, December 2025; segment data via PL/segment sheet).
- **Cash generation and returns stepped up.** **Operating cash flow was ¥28,602mn in FY3/26** against capex of ¥1,234mn in the sheet, cash reached ¥42,180mn and the owners' equity ratio was 52.0% at end-FY3/26. The FY3/27 dividend forecast was raised ¥24 to ¥122 per share on 12 November 2026, and 1,500,000 treasury shares (3.13% of issued shares before cancellation) were cancelled on 27 October 2026 (FY3/26 tanshin, 13 May 2026; Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 12 November 2026).
- **What to monitor.** (1) China customer-location sales and the China segment margin (30.1% in FY3/26; 30.0% on H1 FY3/27 segment totals); (2) India segment sales and margin as Chennai ramps; (3) the H2 FY3/27 implied run-rate versus guidance — **guidance implies H2 revenue of ¥78,793mn and operating profit of ¥22,780mn, below the H1 level**, so delivery against a strong H1 is the near-term test. Orders and backlog are not disclosed in the tanshin and are marked not disclosed throughout this report.

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Tsugami reports under IFRS with a March year-end. The ten-year history in Figure 1 is taken from the PL/segment sheet built from the company's tanshin (JPX archive) — Company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive) — supplemented by per-share and balance-sheet lines printed on the tanshin front pages.

The preceding five years were more volatile and much less profitable. Revenue rose from ¥41,050mn in FY3/17 to ¥68,486mn in FY3/19, then fell 28.0% to ¥49,310mn in FY3/20 before recovering to ¥61,662mn in FY3/21. Operating profit followed the same arc at far lower levels than in the recent window: ¥3,083mn in FY3/17 (a 7.5% margin), a then-peak of ¥10,215mn in FY3/19 (14.9%), a trough of ¥4,549mn in FY3/20 (9.2%), and ¥9,533mn in FY3/21, when the 15.5% operating margin was the highest of the five years. The first two of these years (FY3/17–FY3/18) are reported under Japanese GAAP and the last three under IFRS, as originally filed in each year's own tanshin, so the FY3/18–FY3/19 step in the extended table crosses a change of accounting standard as well as a change in the reported segment and regional bases.

Revenue troughed in FY3/24 at ¥83,928mn (−11.6% YoY) after the post-Covid China capex digestion, then **recovered +28.0% in FY3/25 to ¥107,411mn and +20.2% in FY3/26 to ¥129,140mn**. The five-year revenue CAGR (FY3/22–FY3/26) is 8.5%. Profit recovered far faster than sales: gross margin expanded from 28.2% in FY3/24 to 33.8% in FY3/25 and 37.1% in FY3/26, and operating margin from 15.6% to 21.7% and 28.0%, as the high-margin China mix rose and SG&A was held — SG&A fell as a share of sales from 14.0% in FY3/24 to 10.8% in FY3/26 even as absolute SG&A was broadly flat at ¥13,979mn in FY3/26 versus ¥14,302mn in FY3/25.

Returns followed: the tanshin-reported return on owners' equity was 22.6% in FY3/22, 15.9% in FY3/23, 10.0% in FY3/24, 18.2% in FY3/25 and 23.4% in FY3/26. Book value per share rose from ¥941.82 at end-FY3/22 to ¥1,719.40 at end-FY3/26. Total assets rose from ¥103,761mn to ¥154,055mn over the same period, with owners' equity of ¥80,102mn (52.0% of assets) at end-FY3/26.

Cash conversion was strong in FY3/26: operating cash flow of ¥28,602mn (FY3/25: ¥8,855mn) against sheet capex of ¥1,234mn, lifting cash and equivalents from ¥27,729mn to ¥42,180mn while borrowings fell from ¥11,524mn to ¥9,010mn. Receivables rose with sales, from ¥40,695mn to ¥50,321mn, and inventories were essentially flat at ¥32,724mn (FY3/25: ¥32,629mn) despite 20.2% sales growth. The dividend rose each year — ¥40 (FY3/22), ¥46, ¥48, ¥59 and ¥85 (FY3/26), a 23.5% payout on FY3/26 owners' net income — and the diluted share count declined steadily from 49.41mn in FY3/22 to 46.36mn in FY3/26 on buybacks, mechanically supporting EPS growth: basic EPS rose from ¥112.57 in FY3/24 to ¥361.20 in FY3/26.

A structural point runs through every year: non-controlling interests are large because the China business is separately listed. Non-controlling interests took ¥3,134mn of net income in FY3/22 and ¥7,573mn in FY3/26 — 31.1% of the FY3/26 total of ¥24,318mn. Consolidated operating profit therefore overstates the profit attributable to Tsugami shareholders by roughly one-third, and any per-share analysis must start from owners' net income (¥16,745mn in FY3/26), not from operating profit.

## 3. Business description

Tsugami manufactures and sells precision machine tools, organised as a single product business (machine tools) reported through five geographic, entity-location segments. Its core is the CNC precision automatic lathe: in particular the sliding-headstock (Swiss-type) lathe, in which bar stock is supported by a guide bushing at the cutting point and fed through the spindle, allowing long, slender, small-diameter parts to be turned to micron-level tolerances without deflection. Tsugami began production and sales of sliding-headstock automatic lathes in 1957 and launched its Mercury CNC composite automatic lathe line in 1978 (company history, Tsugami corporate materials, accessed November 2026). Typical workpieces are small precision components for smartphones and other electronics, automotive parts, medical devices, watches and connectors — parts typically below roughly 38mm in diameter, produced in high volumes.

The product range, as disclosed in the tanshin sales-results note, has four lines. (1) **Automatic lathes — ¥108,621mn in FY3/26, 84.1% of revenue** — covering Swiss-type sliding-headstock models, CNC lathes and turning centres. (2) Grinding machines — ¥5,505mn (4.3%) — including cylindrical grinders. (3) Machining centres, thread-rolling machines and special-purpose machines — ¥8,886mn (6.9%). (4) Other — ¥6,127mn (4.7%) — parts and services. Product-line profit is not disclosed. Automatic lathes have held an 84–85% revenue share in every year of the sheet period (FY3/22: ¥79,504mn of ¥93,174mn, 85.3%), so the group is, in substance, a single-product company with small adjacent lines (Company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive)).

Production is organised in three main bases. Japan (Nagaoka and other Niigata plants) develops the technology platform and makes higher-end machines; it is the group's R&D and mother-plant function, though as a segment it earned only a 1.1% margin in FY3/25 and 14.2% in FY3/26. China is the volume engine: Precision Tsugami (China) Corporation Limited, listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2017 (HKEX: 1651), manufactures in Pinghu, Zhejiang (a site the company describes as having grown from about 30,000 sq m at establishment in 2003 to about 140,000 sq m), with a further Anhui operation, manufacturing Tsugami machines in China for the Chinese market. India (Tsugami Precision Engineering India, Chennai area, established 2011) is the newest base — see Strategy below. Sales subsidiaries cover Korea, Germany, the United States and other markets; North American sales are small (USA customer-location sales were ¥4,274mn, 3.3% of FY3/26 revenue).

The business model is a conventional capital-goods model with one distinctive feature: because the China subsidiary is separately listed, roughly one-third of the group's profit accrues to outside shareholders of that subsidiary. Tsugami held 247,530,000 shares of Precision Tsugami (China), about 65.9% of its issued capital at 31 March 2026 (HKEX shareholder data, retrieved November 2026), and a separate disclosure in November 2026 described the holding as approximately 67.15% — the difference reflects source and date; either way, non-controlling interests of ¥26,374mn sat on the balance sheet at end-FY3/26 and took 31.1% of FY3/26 net income. Investors in 6101 therefore own a leveraged claim on the China profit pool, not the whole of it.

## 4. Market and value chain

Machine tools are the 'mother machines' of manufacturing: demand is derived from capex by electronics, automotive, medical, aerospace and general machinery producers, and is highly cyclical. The near-term backdrop at the date of this report is unusually strong. The Japan Machine Tool Builders' Association (JMTBA) reported **September 2026 orders of ¥223.2bn, up about 60% YoY** — a record month and the 15th consecutive month of growth — with overseas orders up 67% to ¥159.2bn and domestic orders up 46% to ¥63.9bn; the industry order backlog reached ¥1.06 trillion at end-August 2026, up 45% YoY, and trade press reported that Tsugami's own backlog had roughly doubled YoY (Nikkei, 10 October 2026, reporting JMTBA data). JMTBA's 2026 full-year orders forecast was revised up to ¥2 trillion (+24.7% YoY) on semiconductor, data-centre, aerospace and advanced-manufacturing demand (industry press reporting JMTBA, 2026). These are industry figures, not Tsugami disclosures: ==Tsugami does not publish its own orders or backlog in the tanshin (not disclosed)==, so the industry series is context, not a company KPI.

Within machine tools, Swiss-type lathes are a niche defined by small-part precision. Third-party market work cited in search results puts CNC machines at 62.5% of the Swiss-turn machine market in 2026 and automotive at 31.4% of demand (Fact.MR, updated 27 October 2026) — a vendor estimate, to be treated as indicative only. The competitive set is concentrated and mostly Japanese at the high end: Citizen Machinery (Citizen Watch group; Cincom sliding-headstock and Miyano fixed-headstock brands), Star Micronics (its machine-tool business built around Swiss-type automatic lathes; it launched the SB-12RII Swiss-type model at JIMTOF 2026, 26–31 October 2026), Tsugami itself, and, outside Japan, Tornos (Switzerland) and Hanwha (Korea), among others. Citizen Machinery commented at the JIMTOF-period industry briefings that small machine tools for semiconductor-related applications were strong, with demand from China, Japan and Southeast Asia and medical demand in Europe and the US (industry press, October–November 2026). No reliable, sourced market-share series for Swiss-type lathes was obtained for this report; a Frost & Sullivan-based claim of roughly 60% share for Precision Tsugami in China's high-precision small-lathe segment circulates in secondary research but could not be verified against a primary source and is therefore not used as a fact here.

Value chain: upstream, Tsugami buys castings (increasingly cast in its own foundries in China and, since April 2024, India), CNC controllers, spindles, ball screws, linear guides and bearings — components that industry executives flagged in October 2026 as in tight supply across the sector. Midstream, value is in machining accuracy, thermal stability, software and the installed application know-how for small-part turning. Downstream, machines are sold through subsidiaries and distributors (in India, the Proteck Machinery joint-venture partner) to component manufacturers, with parts and service a small but recurring tail (within the ¥6,127mn 'other' product line in FY3/26). The margin structure shows where the value currently sits: the entity that manufactures and sells in China earns a 30.1% segment margin, while the Japanese mother operation earned 14.2% in its best recent year — scale, local cost and local mix in China, not the Japanese plant, drive group profit.

## 5. Segments and regions

Reportable segments are geographic, by entity location — Japan, China, India, Korea and Other — for a single machine-tool business. Segment profit is defined by the company as sales less cost of sales and SG&A; it excludes other income/expenses and adjustments, which reconcile segment profit to operating profit (¥2,241mn of adjustments in FY3/26). Figure 2 sets out sales and profit by segment for FY3/22–FY3/26 (Company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive)).

China dominates and is becoming more dominant: its share of consolidated sales rose from 69.4% in FY3/22 (¥64,656mn) to 80.1% in FY3/26 (¥103,476mn), and its segment profit rose from ¥15,862mn to ¥31,155mn, with **margin expanding from 24.5% to 30.1%**. In FY3/26 **China alone generated segment profit equal to 86% of consolidated operating profit before adjustments**. Japan moved the other way: sales fell from ¥23,249mn (FY3/22) to ¥16,439mn (FY3/26) — the Japan segment total includes inter-segment sales of components and machines to overseas subsidiaries, so it is not a domestic-demand measure — and its profit swung from ¥2,146mn (FY3/22) to losses of ¥968mn in FY3/24, recovering to ¥2,328mn (14.2% margin) in FY3/26. India is small but inflecting: sales of ¥2,765mn (FY3/22), ¥4,691mn, ¥5,324mn, ¥4,671mn and ¥6,819mn (FY3/26), with profit of ¥347mn in FY3/26 after a ¥310mn loss in FY3/25. Korea (¥1,286mn sales, ¥0mn profit in FY3/26) and Other (¥1,118mn, ¥31mn) are immaterial.

The customer-location view (Figure 3) tells the demand story more directly. China took ¥103,185mn (79.9%) of FY3/26 sales, up 28.2% YoY; Asia ex-China ¥12,223mn (9.5%); the USA ¥4,274mn (3.3%); Europe ¥2,819mn (2.2%); and Japan, derived as consolidated sales less reported overseas sales, ¥6,639mn (5.1%). In other words, 94.9% of revenue is overseas by customer location and four-fifths is a single country. That concentration is the central fact of the equity story in both directions: it explains the margin (China scale in small lathes) and it is the principal exposure — to Chinese capex cycles, to CNY translation, and to any localisation or trade friction affecting a Japanese-controlled, Hong Kong-listed manufacturer selling in China. Europe has shrunk steadily (¥3,388mn in FY3/22 to ¥2,819mn in FY3/26, with a ¥5,435mn peak in FY3/24) and the US has been flat at ¥4–6bn, so neither currently offsets China.

## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan

No quantified medium-term management plan — with dated revenue, profit or ROE targets — was located in the company's tanshin, its Q2 FY3/27 materials or web sources searched for this report (searched 16 November 2026). Tsugami publishes a medium- and long-term management policy and growth strategy in its integrated report, but no numeric mid-term plan targets are stated in the sources obtained; accordingly this chapter describes the strategy as evidenced by actions and company statements, and flags the absence of a formal plan rather than inventing one.

The strategy in evidence has three legs. First, deepen China: keep manufacturing and selling in China through the listed subsidiary, expanding the Pinghu base and localising procurement, and upstream cash to the parent — in November 2026 the parent disclosed an expected dividend receipt from a China subsidiary (Tsugami Seimitsu Kikou (China)) of about ¥4,533mn (HKD 222,777 thousand), a standalone-accounts item with no consolidated P&L effect (company notice via Japan IR summary, 12 November 2026). Second, build India as the second production and demand base: the foundry near Chennai began operating in April 2024 and the machining and assembly plant followed, with an opening ceremony in December 2025; the company describes India as the second-largest manufacturing market after China, cites investment of about ¥3.6bn, and targets roughly doubling Indian production capacity to 200–300 units a month within two to three years (company integrated-report material, 2025). Independent inauguration reports put the Chennai assembly capacity at 3,000 machines a year and foundry capacity at 6,000 tonnes a year, with India revenue targeted at about Rs400 crore in the inauguration fiscal year from Rs279 crore the prior year and about 15% annual growth thereafter (Hindu BusinessLine, 4 December 2025) — partner/press figures, not Tsugami guidance. Third, allocate the resulting cash to growth investment and returns: Q2 FY3/27 presentation summaries describe using cash for additional production investment and supply-chain strengthening, continuing facility renewal and new-plant construction, keeping a stable dividend as the base and continuing to use the buyback authorisation (Japan IR summary of the Q2 FY3/27 earnings presentation, November 2026).

What would constitute a plan test: disclosure of India segment sales and margin as Chennai volumes build (FY3/26: ¥6,819mn at 5.1%), any capacity or capex figures for China beyond maintenance, and whether a formal mid-term plan with targets is published with FY3/27 results in May 2027. Until then, **FY3/27 company guidance (revenue ¥160,000mn, operating profit ¥48,000mn) is the only quantified forward statement by the company**.

## 8. Other information

History. Tsugami traces its origin to 1923, when block-gauge research began at Honjo, Tokyo — the company started in precision metrology, making the reference standards against which machining accuracy is measured. Tsugami Mfg. Co., Ltd. was established on 15 March 1937 in Nagaoka, Niigata Prefecture, and began block-gauge production. It started production and sales of sliding-headstock automatic lathes in 1957, was renamed Tsugami Corporation in 1970, and launched the Mercury CNC precision automatic lathe in 1978. Overseas production followed: Thailand in 1989, Precision Tsugami (China) in 2003 (Hong Kong listing in 2017), Germany in 2007, Korea in 2010 and India in 2011; the India foundry (2024) and Chennai assembly plant (2025) are the latest steps (company history in Tsugami corporate materials, accessed November 2026). Head office is in Nihonbashi, Tokyo; the Nagaoka factory in Niigata remains the mother plant.

Management. Representative Director and President is Nobuhiro Watabe, who is also Nagaoka Plant Manager; he became Representative Director and President in June 2023, having joined the company in 1993 and previously been seconded to the India subsidiary (company officer data; Q2 FY3/27 tanshin names Watabe as Representative Director and President, 12 November 2026). Other representative directors named in the FY3/26 business report include Mami Matsushita; the board chair (Director, Chairman) is Michiyo Yamamiya. The China listed subsidiary is chaired by Dr Wang Xiaokun (Chairman & CEO from November 2025), with Watabe and Matsushita as non-executive directors. Consolidated employees were 3,515 at 31 March 2026 (FY3/26 business report), of whom the China listed group employed about 2,458 at 30 September 2025 on its own reporting — headcount is not disclosed in the tanshin (not disclosed there) and the securities-report/business-report figures are used here.

Shareholders. Share capital ¥12,345mn; 10,238 shareholders at 31 March 2026 (FY3/26 business report). The shareholder register is dominated by trust banks and financial institutions, with the top 10 holding about 41.6% on third-party compilations (retrieved November 2026); no single controlling shareholder of Tsugami itself was identified in the sources obtained, and a full top-10 table could not be verified against a primary filing for this report — the detailed major-shareholder table is therefore marked not verified rather than reproduced from secondary data. Treasury shares were 1,412,422 at 31 March 2026 and 1,751,287 at 30 September 2026, before the 1,500,000-share cancellation on 27 October 2026.

Shareholder returns. **The dividend has risen for five consecutive years in the sheet period (¥40 → ¥85)** and the FY3/27 forecast of ¥122 would extend that to six; the FY3/26 payout ratio on owners' earnings was 23.5% (tanshin dividend table) and total dividends were ¥3,972mn. Buybacks reduced the diluted share count from 49.41mn (FY3/22) to 46.36mn (FY3/26), and the October 2026 cancellation retired 3.13% of issued shares. The company also pays substantial dividends to non-controlling interests (¥2,575mn in FY3/26), the cash counterpart of the China listing structure.
