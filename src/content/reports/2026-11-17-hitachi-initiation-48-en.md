A ¥12tn revenue guide built on an Energy order boom, with the mix story now carried by Lumada: Hitachi, Ltd. (6501, TSE Prime) is Japan's largest industrial conglomerate, reorganised since April 2025 into four sectors — Digital Systems & Services (DSS), Energy, Mobility and Connective Industries (CI) — under President & CEO Toshiaki Tokunaga. FY3/26 (IFRS) printed revenue of ¥10,586,781mn, adjusted operating profit of ¥1,199,275mn (11.3% margin), record profit before tax of ¥1,273,109mn and record net income attributable to owners of ¥802,368mn. The FY3/27 guide has been raised twice — revenue to ¥12,000,000mn, adjusted operating profit to ¥1,450,000mn, Adjusted EBITA to ¥1,560,000mn — on **an Energy order boom (H1 orders +66% YoY; Hitachi Energy backlog $66.9bn at September 2026)** and a Lumada digital layer that reached 44% of H1 revenue. The initiation question is not backlog size but backlog quality: **whether Energy's embedded margins, DSS productivity gains and continued portfolio exits can lift group Adjusted EBITA margin from 12.5% (H1 FY3/27) into the Inspire 2027 target band of 13–15%**. Strengths: (1) the Energy franchise — backlog coverage of roughly 2.8 years on the Hitachi Energy revenue plan, data-centre demand above 10% of Hitachi Energy revenue, and an H1 sector margin of 14.7% against a 15.0% full-year guide; (2) earnings momentum with discipline — two guidance raises in FY3/27, ~¥290bn of buybacks executed in H1, and an interim dividend raised to ¥32; (3) a decade of portfolio surgery (Hitachi Metals, Hitachi Chemical, Hitachi Construction Machinery, Astemo, home appliances) that has lifted the adjusted operating margin from 6.4% in FY3/17 to 11.3% in FY3/26. Weaknesses: (1) the margin evidence is not uniform — listed arm Hitachi Energy India printed a Q2 FY2027 EBITDA margin of 13.5%, down from 16.3%, on mix and materials, with H1 operating cash flow sharply lower on HVDC contract assets; (2) CI remains the margin laggard at 11.2% in H1 against an 11.8% full-year guide, and DSS absorbed a ¥9.0bn domestic project one-off in Q2; (3) disclosure breaks are frequent — the segment basis has been rebuilt four times in ten years and the Adjusted EBITA formula was revised in FY3/26, so long histories must be read basis by basis, as this report does.

## Key points

- **H1 FY3/27: a record half, profit up, owners' net income down on a high base.** For the six months to 30 September 2026, revenue was ¥5,614.0bn (+17% YoY, company presentation), **Adjusted EBITA ¥699.6bn (12.5% margin, +80bps YoY)**, adjusted operating income ¥643.3bn (+27%) and net income attributable to owners ¥416.6bn, down from ¥472.9bn a year earlier, when the result included the gain on sale of the air-conditioning joint venture. Q2 standalone revenue of ¥2,904.4bn was a record for a second quarter. All four sectors grew revenue and profit YoY in both Q2 and H1.
- **Guidance raised twice: revenue ¥12,000,000mn, Adjusted EBITA ¥1,560,000mn.** Initial FY3/27 guidance (27 April 2026) of revenue ¥11,100,000mn / adjusted operating profit ¥1,315,000mn / Adjusted EBITA ¥1,420,000mn / owners' net income ¥850,000mn was raised at Q1 (29 July 2026) to ¥11,700,000mn / ¥1,408,000mn / ¥1,520,000mn / ¥900,000mn and again at Q2 (28 October 2026) to ¥12,000,000mn / ¥1,450,000mn / ¥1,560,000mn / ¥920,000mn, with basic EPS guided at ¥206.41. **The Q2 raise was revenue-led by DSS and CI (+¥300,000mn) and profit-led by Energy and CI (+¥40,000mn of Adjusted EBITA), per the company's own revision summary**.
- **Energy is the engine: H1 orders +66%, backlog $66.9bn, margin 14.7%.** Energy took ¥3,472.5bn of orders in H1 FY3/27 on large European HVDC awards, data-centre orders and transmission-equipment demand; **Hitachi Energy's order backlog reached $66.9bn (¥10.5tn) at end-September 2026, up from $57.9bn at end-March**. Management attributes margin improvement mainly to productivity (ERP/AI) and large-project loss containment, with backlog gross margin 'continuing to improve' — the claim the next prints must evidence, against Hitachi Energy India's margin compression to 13.5%.
- **Lumada at 44% of H1 revenue; the '50–18' target needs a visible H2 step-up.** **Lumada revenue was ¥2,428bn in H1 FY3/27 (+31% YoY), 44% of consolidated revenue**, and the full-year Lumada and HMAX forecasts were raised at the print; HMAX stood at 47% of its ¥510bn FY3/27 forecast. Inspire 2027 targets a 50% Lumada revenue ratio at an 18% Adjusted EBITA margin. DSS absorbed a ¥9.0bn one-off cost increase on certain domestic projects in Q2; excluding one-offs, the domestic IT services margin was 17.2% in H1 (company Q&A).
- **Portfolio exits and returns continue in parallel.** In progress: the Hitachi Construction Machinery share transfer, a partial Astemo sale, an ATM-business JV in which Hitachi becomes the minority holder, and the transfer of 80.1% of the home-appliances new company to a Nojima-managed SPC for ~¥110bn, expected to complete in FY3/27. **H1 buybacks executed were ~¥290bn, and the interim dividend forecast was raised to ¥32 from ¥28**. The FY3/27 company plan carries core FCF of ¥920bn and ROIC of 13.5% (company guidance).

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Hitachi reports under IFRS with a March year-end. The history in this chapter and in Exhibit 1 is compiled in the PL/segment sheet for 6501 from each year's own tanshin (FY3/17–FY3/21 in the sheet's supplementary history block; FY3/22–FY3/26 in the main block, with 20 quarters; all template checks equal zero). Hitachi presents no plain IFRS operating-profit subtotal: its headline profit line is Adjusted Operating Profit (調整後営業利益 = revenue − cost of sales − SG&A), and that is the profit line used throughout this report unless a line is labelled Adjusted EBITA.

Revenue describes a decade of deliberate shrinkage followed by re-growth. Revenue was ¥9,162,264mn in FY3/17, rose to a peak of ¥10,881,150mn in FY3/23, then fell 10.6% in FY3/24 as Hitachi Construction Machinery (deconsolidated August 2022) and Hitachi Metals (deconsolidated January 2023) left the perimeter, before recovering to ¥9,783,370mn in FY3/25 and ¥10,586,781mn in FY3/26 — the second-highest in company history, behind only FY3/23. The FY3/27 company guide of ¥12,000,000mn would set a new record, driven by Energy and DSS.

Profitability has structurally improved. **Adjusted operating profit rose from ¥587,309mn (6.4% margin) in FY3/17 to ¥1,199,275mn (11.3%) in FY3/26**, with the step-changes in FY3/25 (¥971,606mn, 9.9%) and FY3/26 as the post-reorganisation portfolio — and grid-equipment pricing — came through. Profit before tax of ¥1,273,109mn and owners' net income of ¥802,368mn in FY3/26 were both record highs (company presentation). The weak years are instructive: FY3/20 owners' net income fell to ¥87,596mn on ¥518,643mn of net other investing expenses (restructuring and impairment era), and FY3/21 adjusted operating profit dipped to ¥495,180mn (5.7%) in the COVID year before the portfolio exits rebuilt the margin base.

Per-share history crosses two capital-structure breaks and is never spliced in this report: a 5-for-1 share consolidation on 1 October 2018 and a 5-for-1 share split on 1 July 2024. EPS in Exhibit 1 is calculated (owners' net income ÷ diluted weighted shares as reported in each year's EPS note): ¥47.9 (FY3/17, pre-consolidation basis), ¥230.3 (FY3/19, post-consolidation), ¥603.7 (FY3/22, pre-split), ¥133.9 (FY3/25, post-split) and ¥176.8 (FY3/26, post-split). The overseas revenue ratio has risen from 48.1% in FY3/17 to 63.0% in FY3/26 (calculated from the disclosed regional split), with Europe the fastest-growing region over the last three years on HVDC and grid awards.

## 3. Business description

Hitachi's organising idea is 'Social Innovation' through the combination of IT, OT (operational technology) and products, increasingly delivered as Lumada — the company's digital solutions layer, now being re-based on the stricter HMAX definition (solutions meeting defined criteria for AI/data-driven recurring value). Since the 'true One Hitachi' reorganisation at the start of FY3/26, the group reports four sectors plus Other: Digital Systems & Services, Energy, Mobility and Connective Industries. The former Green Energy & Mobility was split into Energy and Mobility, and Automotive Systems (Astemo) ceased to be a reportable segment. Sector profit is Adjusted EBITA; the definition was revised from FY3/26 (see Basis of preparation).

## DSS (Digital Systems & Services): the Lumada core

DSS combines domestic IT services (systems integration, cloud, managed services), Hitachi Digital (including GlobalLogic's engineering services and Hitachi Digital Services) and digital solutions for social infrastructure. FY3/26 revenue was ¥2,756,551mn with segment profit of ¥450,059mn (16.3% margin, sheet main block, restated basis). It is the principal carrier of the Lumada/HMAX story: management cites SI productivity of ~10% at end-FY3/25 from AI tooling, targeted at ~20% by end-FY3/26 and ~30% in FY3/27 (company Q&A), and a top-200-account strategy inside Hitachi Digital, where the top 200 accounts are ~80% of revenue growing at single digits — the reset management itself calls an 'acceleration year' problem. The Q2 FY3/27 ¥9.0bn domestic project one-off is the control question hanging over the margin bridge.

## Energy: Hitachi Energy and the grid supercycle

Energy is built around Hitachi Energy (power grids: HVDC, transformers, switchgear, grid automation and services), nuclear and other power systems. FY3/26 revenue was ¥3,200,844mn (+24.9% YoY) with segment profit of ¥416,015mn (13.0% margin, restated basis) — the group's largest profit growth engine. Hitachi Energy's order backlog was $66.9bn (¥10.5tn) at end-September 2026, up from $57.9bn at end-March 2026, roughly 2.8 years of cover on the FY3/26 Hitachi Energy revenue plan (calculation, FIELDNOTES Transformers report). Management states data-centre business is more than 10% of Hitachi Energy revenue and a low-teens percentage of its orders, with no reported delays or payment problems on secured projects, and that orders taken this fiscal year convert to revenue on average next fiscal year. **Capacity is being expanded 2–2.5×**. The counter-evidence sits inside the group: listed subsidiary Hitachi Energy India printed a Q2 FY2027 EBITDA margin of 13.5% (from 16.3% a year earlier) on mix and materials, with H1 operating cash flow sharply lower on HVDC contract assets.

## Mobility: rail systems and building systems

Mobility covers railway systems (rolling stock, signalling, turnkey rail, increasingly HMAX-based services) and building systems (elevators and escalators, centred on China exposure). FY3/26 revenue was ¥1,320,631mn with segment profit of ¥108,115mn — an 8.2% margin, the group's lowest, and the FY3/27 guide (¥1,450bn at 9.4%) still leaves it the margin laggard. In H1 FY3/27 Mobility's margin was 8.1%; the FY3/26 presentation noted profit held back by PMI-related costs even as Lumada revenue (including signalling) grew. The investment question is whether rail services mix and elevator modernisation can carry the margin toward double digits without a portfolio move.

## Connective Industries, Other, and the shrinking perimeter

CI is the industrial-products cluster: semiconductor manufacturing equipment (through the DSS-adjacent measurement/inspection franchises), industrial equipment, building-systems-adjacent products, materials-adjacent businesses and services. FY3/26 revenue was ¥3,000,760mn (−2.8% YoY) with segment profit of ¥367,396mn (12.2% margin) — the largest revenue block, but the H1 FY3/27 margin of 11.2% makes it the current debate: mix improvement (semiconductor equipment, measurement, services) versus portfolio action of the kind already applied elsewhere. Other (¥275,252mn revenue, ¥22,966mn profit in FY3/26) holds the remaining businesses. The perimeter keeps shrinking by design: home appliances (80.1% to a Nojima-managed SPC, ~¥110bn, completing FY3/27), the ATM business (JV, Hitachi to minority), a partial Astemo sale and the Hitachi Construction Machinery share transfer are all in progress.

## 4. Market and value chain

Hitachi's value chain position is unusual: it is simultaneously an equipment OEM (grid, rail, industrial), an IT services provider and a data/AI layer (Lumada/HMAX) over its own installed base. The demand cycle currently doing the work is the grid supercycle — transmission investment for renewables connection, grid stability and data-centre power — in which Hitachi Energy competes with Siemens Energy and GE Vernova for HVDC systems, large power transformers and switchgear, with industry lead times extended and pricing supported by component bottlenecks (GE Vernova's October 2026 agreement to acquire the Trench Group bushing business, per the FIELDNOTES Transformers report, is the latest example of rivals buying component security). **Hitachi's stated defence is backlog quality: index-linked large-project contracts, a shift of HVDC exposure toward engineering and procurement over construction, ERP-completed operations and AI-assisted project management**.

In digital, the value-chain question is different: whether Lumada/HMAX converts an installed-base service relationship into software-margin recurring revenue at the targeted 18% Adjusted EBITA margin, or whether the 50% revenue-ratio target is reached partly by re-registering existing businesses under the HMAX definition after customisation. Management's own framing — HMAX at 47% of its ¥510bn FY3/27 forecast at H1, with the forecast raised — keeps both readings open until the new-vs-reclassified split is disclosed (未取得 in the sources read). Rare-earth exposure in CI and Mobility (magnets, motors) is called 'currently limited' by management on strategic inventories; inventory cover in months was not disclosed in the Q&A (未取得).

The order book behind the Energy story is quantifiable at sector level for the latest half. In H1 FY3/27, sector orders were ¥1,822.0bn in DSS, ¥3,472.5bn in Energy, ¥973.1bn in Mobility and ¥1,945.8bn in Connective Industries, against H1 revenue of ¥1,533.1bn, ¥1,872.3bn, ¥692.9bn and ¥1,586.6bn respectively — Energy took in roughly 1.9 yen of orders for every yen of revenue recognised in the half. Hitachi Energy's backlog stood at $66.9bn (about ¥10.5tn) at end-September 2026, up from $57.9bn at end-March 2026, which the FIELDNOTES Transformers report calculates as roughly 2.8 years of cover on the FY3/26 Hitachi Energy revenue plan. Management has said data-centre business is more than 10% of Hitachi Energy revenue and a low-teens share of its orders, that it has seen no delays, postponements or payment problems on secured projects, and that orders taken this fiscal year convert to revenue on average next fiscal year.

## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan — Inspire 2027

The mid-term plan is Inspire 2027 (FY3/26–FY3/28; progress update 27 April 2026; all figures company plan targets, not forecasts of this report): revenue CAGR of 7–9% (FY3/25–FY3/28 as presented), Adjusted EBITA margin of 13–15%, cash-flow conversion above 90%, ROIC of 12–13%, and **the 'Lumada 50–18' target — a 50% Lumada revenue ratio at an 18% Adjusted EBITA margin** — with a long-term aspiration of Lumada 80% at 20%. The Energy sector's FY3/28 Adjusted EBITA margin target was raised at the April 2026 update from above 12% to above 14% on backlog visibility; the FY3/27 sector guide of 15.0% already sits above that raised target. FY3/27 guidance (Chapter 2) is consistent with the plan's trajectory: a 13.0% guided Adjusted EBITA margin at the bottom of the target band, ROIC guided at 13.5%.

Execution levers, in management's own order: (1) Energy capacity and backlog conversion — capacity up 2–2.5×, backlog gross margin 'continuing to improve', price rises assumed only around inflation; (2) DSS productivity — SI productivity ~10% → ~20% (end-FY3/26) → ~30% (FY3/27) from AI tooling, plus the Hitachi Digital reset (GlobalLogic vertical narrowing, top-200-account focus); (3) HMAX scaling — ¥510bn FY3/27 forecast, 47% achieved at H1, target raised; (4) portfolio — the exits listed in Chapter 3, with proceeds funding returns (~¥290bn H1 buybacks; interim dividend raised to ¥32) under a D/E discipline of ~0.5× cited in the April 2026 Q&A. The plan's soft spot is definitional: how much of the Lumada ratio step-up from 44% (H1) to 50% is new recurring revenue versus re-registration of existing businesses under HMAX criteria is not disclosed (未取得).
