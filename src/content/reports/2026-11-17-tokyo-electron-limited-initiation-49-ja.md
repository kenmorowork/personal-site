Tokyo Electron (TEL) is Japan's largest semiconductor production equipment (SPE) maker and a top-three global WFE supplier, with a near-monopoly in coater/developers and leading positions in etch, deposition, cleaning and wafer probers. FY3/2026 (J-GAAP) sales were ¥2,443,533mn (+0.5%) with operating profit of ¥624,936mn (25.6%, −10.4% YoY) — but net income rose to a record ¥574,454mn because ¥115,494mn of gains on sales of policy-held shares sat in extraordinary income. The cycle has since inflected violently: **H1 FY3/2027 sales were ¥1,667.4bn (+41.3%) and OP ¥506.7bn (30.4%)**, and on 30 October 2026 the company published its first full-year FY3/2027 guidance — sales ¥3,700,000mn (+51.4%), OP ¥1,169,000mn (+87.1%), net income ¥865,000mn — built on its view that CY2027 WFE exceeds $210bn. Strengths: **a >90% share in coater/developers**, a debt-free balance sheet (cash ¥505,414mn, zero borrowings), FY3/26 operating cash flow of ¥539,732mn, and a ¥150bn buyback running to March 2027. Weaknesses: the medium-term plan expiring this year targeted an OP margin of 35% or more — the FY3/2027 guide implies 31.6% (calculated) — China was 36.4% of Q2 sales against a low-30% full-year guide, segment disclosure collapsed to a single segment from FY3/2024 (no regional split in the tanshin since), and the shares at ¥8,794 trade on 35.0x FY3/2026 earnings (calculated) after a 5-for-1 split in October 2026. Rating and price target are Under Review; this report organises the reported record and the questions it raises — the investment judgement is the reader's.

## Key points

- **Guidance arrived late and large.** FY3/2027 full-year guidance was 未定 (undetermined) at both the April results and Q1; only H1 was guided (raised in July to sales ¥1,620,000mn / OP ¥458,000mn). The full-year guide first published on 30 Oct 2026 — sales ¥3,700,000mn, OP ¥1,169,000mn, EPS ¥381.69 (post-split) — **implies an H2 of ¥2,032.5bn sales and ¥662.2bn OP at a 32.6% margin (calculated)**.
- **The mid-term plan scores 2 of 3.** The June 2022 plan targeted FY3/2027 sales of ¥3tn or more, OP margin 35% or more and ROE 30% or more (company plan). **The guide clears revenue (¥3.70tn) but implies a 31.6% OP margin**; FY3/2026 ROE was about 29.6% (secondary source). Whether the next plan rebases the margin ambition or bridges to it is the central strategy question.
- **China mix has to fall while sales rise 51%.** **China was 36.4% of Q2 FY3/2027 sales (presentation basis) against a low-30% full-year guide**, so H2 requires leading-edge logic and DRAM outside China to outgrow China materially. The within-China split between leading-edge-capable and mature-node tools is not disclosed.
- **FY3/2026 profit quality needs care.** Operating profit fell 10.4% in FY3/2026 while net income rose 5.6% to a record — **the bridge is ¥120,726mn of extraordinary income, mainly ¥115,494mn of gains on sales of policy-held shares (Integrated Report 2026)**. Underlying OP margin compressed from 28.7% to 25.6% as SG&A rose to 19.8% of sales.
- **Disclosure has thinned as the cycle turned.** TEL moved to a single segment in FY3/2024 and dropped the tanshin regional reference table; **orders, backlog, headcount and units are not disclosed in the tanshin at all (register S5/S13)**. The finest current splits — application mix, China share, new-equipment vs field solutions — exist only in briefing presentations.

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Tokyo Electron reports on Japanese GAAP with a March year-end. Exhibit 1 (page 1) sets out the ten-year record, FY3/2017–FY3/2026, from the PL/segment sheet (main window FY3/2022–26, register S1/S2; history block FY3/2017–21 as originally reported, S14).

The decade in one line: sales grew from ¥799,719mn (FY3/2017) to ¥2,443,533mn (FY3/2026), a 13.2% CAGR (calculated), through two full WFE cycles — the FY3/2019 peak (¥1,278,240mn), the FY3/2020 dip, the FY3/2022–23 AI/memory upcycle (¥2,209,025mn), the FY3/2024 correction (−17.1% to ¥1,830,527mn) and the FY3/2025 recovery (¥2,431,568mn). **Operating profit grew faster, from ¥155,697mn to ¥624,936mn (16.7% CAGR, calculated)**, but the margin path is cyclical, not linear: 19.5% (FY3/2017) → 24.3% (FY3/2019) → 22.9% (FY3/2021) → 29.9% (FY3/2022, the decade peak) → 24.9% in the FY3/2024 trough → 28.7% (FY3/2025) → 25.6% (FY3/2026). Gross margin has been the swing factor (45.5% in FY3/2022, 47.1% at the FY3/2025 peak, 45.3% in FY3/2026), while SG&A rose from 15.6% of sales (FY3/2022) to 19.8% (FY3/2026) as R&D climbed from ¥158,256mn to ¥277,866mn (11.4% of sales).

Net income attributable to owners reached a record ¥574,454mn in FY3/2026 — but the composition matters: operating profit fell ¥72,383mn YoY while extraordinary income of ¥120,726mn (mainly ¥115,494mn of gains on sales of investment securities from partial sales of policy-held shares; Integrated Report 2026) lifted pre-tax profit to ¥748,180mn. EPS is shown in Exhibit 1 on each period's own share basis and is never spliced: FY3/2017–21 are calculated on pre-split basic shares, FY3/2023 on the post-3-for-1-split restated count (467.9mn shares, register S11), and FY3/2026 is the reported ¥1,254.57 (pre-split; ¥250.91 post-split equivalent, calculated). EPS for FY3/2022, FY3/2024 and FY3/2025 is not shown: the sheet's annual share-count cells for those years are misaligned (see Chapter 9), and this report does not substitute unverified counts.

Cash generation is consistently strong: operating cash flow was positive in all ten years (¥136,948mn in FY3/2017 to ¥539,732mn in FY3/2026, peaking at ¥582,174mn in FY3/2025), and the company has carried zero borrowings at every period-end in the window (register S6).

## 2. Latest update — record H1 FY3/2027 and the first full-year guide

The most recent results are Q2 FY3/2027 (30 October 2026; Q&A summary 2 November 2026). This chapter summarises the half, then the guidance chain in full.

## Results — H1 FY3/2027 (April–September 2026)

H1 FY3/2027: sales ¥1,667.4bn (+41.3% YoY), operating profit ¥506.7bn (30.4% margin, +67.2%), net income (owners) ¥376.9bn (+56.0%) (company Q2 FY3/2027 materials, 30 October 2026, via the interview pack). Q1 standalone was sales ¥732.4bn (+33.3%) and OP ¥211.4bn (tanshin, 30 July 2026); **Q2 standalone was a record quarter — sales ¥935.0bn (+48.4%), OP ¥295.3bn (+86.4%), OP margin 31.6%, gross margin ~48% (H1: 47.4%)**.

Composition (briefing presentation, 30 October 2026 — presentation basis, not the tanshin): new equipment was ~79% of Q2 sales; by application, non-memory (logic/foundry and others) 56%, DRAM 34%, non-volatile memory 10%. By region, China took ¥339.9bn — 36.4% of Q2 sales, up from 30.4% in Q1 — with South Korea at 20.4% and Taiwan at 19.3%. Product-level FY sales growth rates by system were not disclosed this time, and etch/deposition share figures were not disclosed (未取得, Q&A of 2 November 2026). No order intake or backlog total is disclosed; demand colour is qualitative — coater/developer inquiries "from around the world" with share over 90%, prober inquiries for CY2027 and CY2028 "very large", and 3D-integration/advanced-packaging demand expected to grow substantially from CY2027 (company Q&A).

## Guidance — H1-only for two quarters, then a full-year step-change

Exhibit 3 (this section) sets out the chain. At the FY3/2026 results (30 April 2026) the company published H1 FY3/2027 guidance only — sales ¥1,570,000mn, OP ¥431,000mn — with the full year 未定 (undetermined). At Q1 (30 July 2026) H1 was raised to sales ¥1,620,000mn / OP ¥458,000mn; the full year remained undetermined. The guide rests on the company's WFE view (Q&A, 2 November 2026): CY2026 WFE of ~$160bn (logic/foundry high-$80bn range, DRAM high-$40bn, NAND mid-$10bn) and CY2027 of $210bn or more (logic/foundry ~$100bn, DRAM low-$70bn range), explicitly a build-up of customer capex plans received rather than a supply-constrained number, "with possible upside". Dividend guidance for FY3/2027 was not captured in the sources used (未取得); the FY3/2026 dividend was ¥628 per share (pre-split; secondary source).

## 3. Business description

Tokyo Electron (founded 1963) develops, manufactures, sells and services semiconductor production equipment. Since FY3/2024 it reports a single segment — semiconductor production equipment — having previously reported SPE, FPD (flat panel display equipment) and Other (Chapter 5). The group's manufacturing and sales/service subsidiaries in Japan and overseas execute production, installation and field service.

The finest product split the company discloses shows what the single segment contains in a strong quarter. In Q2 FY3/2027 (July-September 2026), new equipment was about ¥734.4bn, roughly 79% of the quarter's ¥935.0bn of sales, with field solutions (parts, used equipment, modification and service) making up the balance. Within new equipment, non-memory applications (logic and foundry) were 56% of sales, DRAM 34% and non-volatile memory 10%. Geographically, the same quarter's presentation-basis sales were ¥339.9bn to China (36.4% of sales, up from 30.4% in Q1), ¥190.7bn to South Korea (20.4%) and ¥180.4bn to Taiwan (19.3%); management guides the full-year China share to the low-30% range, below the Q2 print.

## Products — the SPE portfolio

The portfolio spans the main front-end process steps: coater/developers for lithography (share over 90% by the company's own statement, Q&A 2 November 2026 — the franchise product), plasma etch systems (the largest single WFE market), deposition (thermal CVD/ALD and related), surface preparation/cleaning systems, wafer probers for test, and wafer bonders/debonders and other advanced-packaging tools for 3D integration. Field solutions — parts, maintenance, modifications and used equipment — provide the recurring remainder (~21% of Q2 FY3/2027 sales, calculated as the complement of the ~79% new-equipment share). FPD equipment, once a reported segment (¥111,261mn of sales at its FY3/2019 peak), is no longer separately disclosed and is immaterial to the current mix. Product-level sales growth by system, disclosed in some earlier briefings, was not disclosed at the Q2 FY3/2027 print.

## Customers and demand drivers

Customers are the world's logic foundries, logic IDMs and memory makers; the company's Integrated Report names Samsung Electronics and TSMC among major customers. Demand is a derivative of WFE spending, which the company sizes at ~$160bn for CY2026 and $210bn or more for CY2027 (Chapter 2). Mix is shifting within that total: the company's CY2027 build-up has logic/foundry and memory at roughly 5:5 (from 6:4 in CY2026), with leading-edge nodes at 35% to just under 40% of logic/foundry spending. Customer concentration percentages are not disclosed in the tanshin (未取得).

## Group structure and footprint

Production is concentrated in Japan (including the Miyagi etch operations), with sales and service subsidiaries across the customer regions. Capacity additions named in current materials are the Tohoku Production & Logistics Center and the Miyagi etch centre completing in summer 2027 (interview pack, from company materials). Headcount is not disclosed in the tanshin (register S13); the FY3/2026 securities-report-based figure is 20,236 employees (secondary source). President & CEO is Toshiki Kawai (Representative Director, in the role since January 2016; company leadership page and FY3/2027 filings).

## Strategy as stated by the company

Stated strategy centres on (i) holding or extending share in the process steps where TEL is strongest — coater/developer, etch, deposition, cleaning, probers — as WFE grows; (ii) advanced packaging and 3D integration as a new growth pool from CY2027; (iii) price revisions becoming structural rather than one-off, with the full gross-margin effect "in earnest from FY2028" (Q&A); and (iv) capacity built ahead of the CY2027 demand build-up. The financial frame for that strategy is the medium-term plan in Chapter 7.

## 4. Market and value chain

WFE is the largest pool in the semiconductor equipment value chain. TEL's company view — ~$160bn in CY2026 growing to $210bn or more in CY2027 (+30% or more) — is a sum of customer capex plans, and the interview pack records the obvious challenge: a customer-plan build-up is procyclical by construction, and management answered the CY2028 payback question only with "no slowdown signals in customer dialogue". Global competitors are Applied Materials and Lam Research (US), ASML (Netherlands, lithography), and in Japan SCREEN Holdings and Kokusai Electric; Chinese domestic makers are advancing in mature-node etch and deposition, which bears directly on the 36.4% China mix. ==TEL's claimed defensible ground is coater/developer (>90% share), its installed-base field-solutions annuity, and process co-optimisation with leading-edge customers==; its etch and deposition shares versus Lam and Applied Materials are not disclosed in the print (未取得), so the share trajectory in the largest WFE pool cannot be verified from company disclosure — a standing gap for follow-up.

Two WFE frames now coexist and should not be mixed. SEMI's forecast, as compiled in the FIELDNOTES semiconductor equipment report, puts WFE at $116.9bn in 2025, rising 23.1% to $143.9bn in 2026 and to roughly $200bn by 2028, with 2026 foundry/logic at $78.0bn, DRAM at $38.8bn and NAND at $13.9bn. The company's own view, built up from customer capex plans, is higher — about $160bn for CY2026 and $210bn or more for CY2027. The gap between an industry-association forecast and a customer-plan build-up is itself information: the company number is procyclical by construction, and management answered the CY2028 payback question only by saying it sees no slowdown signals in customer dialogue.
