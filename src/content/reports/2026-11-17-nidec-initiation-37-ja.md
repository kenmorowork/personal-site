All for motors, nothing yet for the audit: the franchise is intact, the numbers are not yet reliable: Nidec Corporation (6594, TSE Prime) is the Kyoto-based group founded in 1973 by Shigenobu Nagamori — described in the interview pack as the world's largest motor maker — spanning precision small motors (HDD spindle motors, brushless and fan motors, and water-cooling modules for AI data centres), automotive motors and traction-motor systems, appliance, commercial and industrial motors, machinery (presses, reducers, machine tools, inspection equipment) and electronic and optical components. FY3/26 revenue was ¥2,708,705mn (year ended 31 March 2026, as filed in the FY3/26 tanshin of 30 September 2026; FY3/26 figures carry a disclaimer of opinion from the auditor — the second consecutive year — and are labelled as such at every use in this report). The initiation question is not demand but reliability. A third-party committee (established 3 September 2025; reports received 27 February and 17 April 2026) found numerous improper accounting treatments at many group sites, carried out with management involvement or awareness — premature revenue recognition, under-provisioning, overstated inventories, impairment avoidance and cost capitalisation — and found that executives had provided the auditor with inaccurate or misleading information and concealed or falsified records (FY3/26 tanshin, note 8). **Prior years were retrospectively restated: FY3/25 operating profit as originally filed (¥240,200mn) became ¥128,222mn as restated in the FY3/26 tanshin**. FY3/26 itself printed an operating loss of ¥518,978mn (disclaimer of opinion) after ¥632,135mn of non-financial-asset impairment losses, the shares have been designated Securities on Special Alert (特別注意銘柄) since 28 October 2025, and as of this report's date, 17 November 2026, **no FY3/27 quarterly results have been filed at all** — the Q1 FY3/27 tanshin was never disclosed and the Q2 tanshin is postponed, with the H1 FY3/27 interim-report deadline extended to 16 December 2026. Strengths: (1) the underlying franchise is real — SPMS segment profit rose to ¥62,844mn in FY3/26 (disclaimer of opinion) at a 15.6% margin, HDD motor revenue grew 9.8% to ¥110,152mn on nearline and AI-related demand, and generators and battery energy storage systems (BESS) for data centres drove the appliance/commercial/industrial product group; (2) liquidity held through the crisis year — cash of ¥852,204mn against interest-bearing debt of ¥1,113,404mn at March 2026 (disclaimer of opinion), operating cash flow of ¥234,218mn (disclaimer of opinion) and a ¥600bn commitment line renewed on 5 November 2026; (3) governance is being rebuilt under duress — the founder has left the board, an independent outside director chairs it, President Michio Kaida (from 29 September 2026) leads a transformation plan, and the company resolved on 27 October 2026 to sue its founder and eight other former executives. Weaknesses: (1) the audit situation itself — disclaimers of opinion on two consecutive securities reports (FY3/25 and FY3/26), a tanshin note stating that some personnel involved in the improper accounting still hold responsible positions in the financial-reporting process and that undiscovered misstatements could be material and pervasive, and corrected filings whose targeted 28 October 2026 submission was missed with audit completion timing undetermined (interview pack); (2) pre-crisis earnings were overstated — the cumulative impact of the improper accounting is put at about ¥160.7bn in the committee process (interview pack, press compilation) and FY3/25 as-filed operating profit was cut 46.6% on restatement; (3) the FY3/27 plan (revenue ¥2,800,000mn, operating profit ¥200,000mn) is an unaudited management assertion issued alongside disclaimed statements, untested by any published H1 actuals against its ¥120,000mn H1 operating-profit plan — it is labelled exactly that way wherever it appears in this report, and never as guidance of the usual standing. All historical figures are as reported by the company via the PL/segment sheet built from tanshin in the JPX disclosure archive, on the bases labelled in each chapter.

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Nidec reports under IFRS with a March year-end. The history in this chapter and in Exhibit 1 covers ten years, FY3/17–FY3/26, as reported in each year's own annual tanshin and compiled in the PL/segment sheet for 6594 (FY3/22–FY3/26 main block plus the FY3/17–FY3/21 history block added in the history-deepening programme; all template and history-block checks equal zero). Two audit labels sit inside the ten years and are not removable: the FY3/25 securities report received a disclaimer of opinion and the FY3/26 securities report received a disclaimer of opinion — the second consecutive year (sheet register S1) — and the FY3/26 tanshin retrospectively restates FY3/25. This chapter's history therefore uses each year's own tanshin as filed (the sheet's standard method), with FY3/25 shown as originally filed and the restated FY3/25 figures given separately in Chapters 2, 5 and 9 wherever the comparison matters. FY3/27 has no filed figures at all and appears nowhere in this history. Figures are ¥mn unless stated.

Revenue grew from ¥1,199,311mn in FY3/17 to a peak of ¥2,708,705mn in FY3/26 (disclaimer of opinion) — a 9.5% ten-year CAGR (calculated) built by the FY3/18–FY3/19 appliance and automotive consolidation wave, the COVID-era small-motor surge (FY3/21–FY3/22, +5.4% and +18.5%) and the FY3/23 peak growth year (+16.9% to ¥2,242,824mn), followed by three slower years (+4.7% / +11.0% / +3.9% on the as-filed series). Operating profit tells a different story: ¥140,331mn (11.7% margin) in FY3/17 and a high of ¥167,637mn in FY3/18 gave way to the FY3/20 dip (¥110,326mn), recovery to ¥171,487mn in FY3/22, then a halving to ¥100,081mn in FY3/23 (4.5% margin) as China demand, EV traction-motor pricing and inventory corrections hit simultaneously and income tax expense of ¥74,889mn (a 63.1% effective rate on the sheet's control definition) cut owners' net income to ¥45,003mn. ## 2. Latest update — FY3/26 results filed on 30 September 2026 (disclaimer of opinion) and the missing FY3/27 interims

The latest actually filed period is the full year FY3/26 (April 2025–March 2026), published on 30 September 2026 — roughly five months late, one day after the president's resignation, and accompanied by a disclaimer of opinion on the securities report for the second consecutive year. There is no more recent filed period: FY3/27 Q1 and Q2 results had not been filed as of 17 November 2026 (section 2b). ## FY3/26 results (as filed; disclaimer of opinion — second consecutive year)

Revenue was ¥2,708,705mn (+3.9% YoY against the restated FY3/25 revenue of ¥2,606,347mn), gross profit ¥526,635mn (19.4% margin), operating profit a loss of ¥518,978mn, profit before tax a loss of ¥496,214mn (tanshin header, continuing basis; the sheet's control PBT including the −¥51mn discontinued result is −¥496,265mn), net income a loss of ¥622,961mn in total and ¥564,624mn attributable to owners, and basic EPS −¥492.55 (basic and diluted identical; no dilutive securities). All FY3/26 figures in this paragraph carry a disclaimer of opinion. The anatomy matters more than the headline: **the company recognised non-financial-asset impairment losses of ¥632,135mn in the year (qualitative section), booked overwhelmingly in Q4** — standalone Q4 operating profit was −¥634,713mn against +¥33,339mn / +¥53,969mn / +¥28,427mn in Q1–Q3 on the restated quarterly trend table (Exhibit 3) — and by segment the disclosed impairments were ACIM ¥298,800mn, MOEN ¥116,287mn and AMEC ¥18,387mn (segment note), with the balance in Machinery, Group Companies and SPMS; by management product group, the appliance/commercial/industrial group took ¥335,139mn and the automotive group ¥231,085mn. FX added about ¥14,682mn of gains below operating profit, and income tax expense of ¥126,696mn was still booked against the pre-tax loss (including ¥74,850mn of deferred-tax expense in the cash-flow presentation), which is why the total net loss exceeds the pre-tax loss. The FY3/26 dividend is nil (interim and year-end both no dividend, decided 23 October 2025 and 3 March 2026 respectively), and the FY3/27 dividend is undecided.

The balance sheet at 31 March 2026 (disclaimer of opinion): total assets ¥3,107,621mn, total equity ¥764,726mn, owners' equity ¥794,164mn (owners' equity exceeds total equity because non-controlling interests are negative at −¥29,438mn after subsidiary losses), owners' equity ratio 25.6% (restated FY3/25: 42.5%), book value per share ¥692.80 (restated FY3/25: ¥1,081.57). Interest-bearing debt (the company's definition: short-term borrowings + current portion of long-term debt + long-term debt, excluding leases) jumped to ¥1,113,404mn on the sheet (¥1,113,405mn in the tanshin's qualitative text — a ¥1mn rounding difference, Chapter 9) from a restated ¥639,192mn, as short-term borrowings rose by a net ¥492,904mn in the year (cash-flow statement) while ¥131,040mn of bonds were redeemed; cash rose ¥605,950mn to ¥852,204mn, so net interest-bearing debt actually fell to ¥261,201mn and the net D/E ratio was 0.33x (gross D/E 1.40x) — the company pre-funded liquidity ahead of the filing crisis, a fact pattern that matters for the listing-risk debate. Operating cash flow was ¥234,218mn, investing outflow ¥136,238mn (capex on tangible assets ¥130,482mn), free cash flow ¥97,979mn (company definition) and financing inflow ¥418,246mn (all FY3/26, disclaimer of opinion). One non-consolidated figure deserves prominence: the parent company's own net assets fell to ¥18,976mn at March 2026 (equity ratio 1.0%), from ¥202,984mn restated a year earlier (FY3/26 tanshin, individual summary) — the holding-company balance sheet, not the consolidated one, is where the loss landed hardest.

Why the disclaimer — in the company's own disclosure (FY3/26 tanshin, note 8, summarised here, not verbatim): the third-party committee (established 3 September 2025 under the Japan Federation of Bar Associations guidelines; reports 27 February 2026 and, as the final supplementary report, 17 April 2026) identified, at the company and across many group sites, improper accounting committed with management involvement or awareness — premature revenue recognition, under-provisioning, overstated inventories, impairment avoidance and cost capitalisation — and confirmed that management and accounting staff had given the auditor inaccurate or misleading information and concealed or falsified records. Separate internal investigations found quality-related misconduct (falsified test and inspection records and unauthorised use of nonconforming materials at multiple production sites) and customs under-declaration on import transactions; the identified items were corrected in the FY3/26 statements, which restate FY3/25 retrospectively. However, personnel measures for some involved officers and employees were still incomplete at filing, some of those individuals still held responsible positions in the financial-reporting process, successors' performance of those responsibilities could not be sufficiently confirmed, and the company therefore states that undiscovered misstatements could have a material and pervasive effect that is not reflected because the affected items and amounts are not clear. PwC Japan accordingly disclaimed an opinion on the FY3/26 securities report — the second consecutive disclaimer, after FY3/25 — and the shares remain designated Securities on Special Alert (designated 28 October 2025). ## No FY3/27 interims: postponement, the 16 December 2026 deadline, and the FY3/27 plan as an unaudited management assertion

**Nothing about FY3/27 performance is knowable from company disclosure as of 17 November 2026**, and this report presents no FY3/27 interim figures because none have been filed. The chronology, from the sheet register (S1) and the interview pack: the FY3/27 Q1 tanshin was never disclosed (a delay notice was issued on 5 August 2026); the Q2 FY3/27 tanshin was formally postponed (company notices on 11–12 November 2026); and on 16 November 2026 the company applied to extend the H1 FY3/27 interim-report filing deadline by one month to 16 December 2026, citing delayed restatement work at some overseas sites (Reuters / Newsweek Japan reports via the interview pack; the register records the Q2 delay notice under 16 November 2026 — the dating difference between the two sources is flagged in Chapter 9). In parallel, the audited corrected filings for prior years that the company had targeted for 28 October 2026 were not submitted: additional procedures — including checks on 252 signatory candidates at the auditor's request — pushed the company's own additional work into November 2026 with audit completion timing undetermined, although corrected-audit and FY3/27-audit contracts were signed on 23–24 October 2026, and the internal-control confirmation document was submitted to the TSE on 30 October 2026, starting the listing-maintenance review (interview pack, citing company disclosure via Tokyo Brief, 30 October 2026).

The only FY3/27 numbers in existence are the plan figures published in section 3 of the FY3/26 tanshin (30 September 2026): full-year revenue ¥2,800,000mn, operating profit ¥200,000mn, profit before tax ¥180,000mn, owners' net income ¥100,000mn and basic EPS ¥87.23, on FX assumptions of USD/JPY 150 and EUR/JPY 180, with an H1 (cumulative Q2) plan of revenue ¥1,400,000mn, operating profit ¥120,000mn, profit before tax ¥110,000mn and owners' net income ¥60,000mn (EPS ¥52.34). In this report these figures are labelled, at every use, as an unaudited management assertion only: they were issued alongside financial statements carrying a disclaimer of opinion, they have never been revised or confirmed since (guidance tracker, 17 November 2026: unchanged, status 未検証), and roughly 60% of the planned full-year operating profit was scheduled to land in an H1 for which no results have been published — so there is no disclosed basis on which to treat the ¥200,000mn as guidance of the usual standing, and no H1 progress rate can be computed. The FY3/27 dividend forecast is undecided (interim and year-end both 未定). Two post-period liquidity facts are on the record: on 5 November 2026 the company renewed commitment-line agreements with MUFG Bank and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation totalling ¥600bn (company release via Business Wire), against cash of ¥852,204mn at March 2026 (disclaimer of opinion). The next hard disclosure event is therefore not an earnings print but a deadline: the H1 FY3/27 interim report by 16 December 2026, and whether the Q1 and Q2 FY3/27 tanshin appear with it. ## The portfolio frame: breadth built by fifty years of M&A, now being cut back

Nidec's breadth is an acquisition artefact: from the 1973 founding as a precision small-motor maker, the group bought its way into appliance motors (Emerson's motor business, Leroy-Somer, Control Techniques), automotive (Elesys, and the OMRON automotive-components business that became Nidec Mobility), compressors (Embraco), machinery (Shimpo, OKK, Takisawa, Pama) and components — reaching 353 group entities and 248 sites by March 2026 (FY3/26 tanshin, Re-Definition plan section). The common technology thread is the motor and its control: rotating and moving things, from a 10mm HDD spindle motor to a traction-motor system and a generator, with power electronics and gearing attached. The crisis-era reversal of that build-out is now official policy on two documents: the 'Re-Definition' five-year reform plan (basic policy announced 13 May 2026; Chapter 7) targets consolidation to 160–180 sites and 160–180 entities, and the Transformation plan of 1 October 2026 (interview pack) names five growth areas — water-cooling modules/CDU for AI data centres, generators, BESS/power-conversion systems, HDD precision small motors and one further data-centre-adjacent line — with selection and concentration in automotive and appliance motors. The first signed exit is Nidec Components, to be sold to Carlyle for ¥103.0bn with closing on 1 December 2026 (interview pack). Whether the rump portfolio — SPMS plus data-centre power — can carry a group of ¥2.7tn of revenue is the business question that sits underneath the disclosure question. Source: FY3/26 tanshin (Re-Definition section), 30 September 2026; interview pack, 16 November 2026.

## 4. Market and value chain

Nidec sells components, not systems, into four value chains that share one competence — **designing and mass-producing motors and their drive electronics at a cost and quality level customers cannot replicate in-house**. In precision small motors, the chain runs from rare-earth magnets, bearings and semiconductor drivers through Nidec's motor plants (heavily in Thailand, China and the Philippines, on the entity-location disclosure) to HDD makers, fan and module makers and, increasingly, server and data-centre equipment makers: the current cycle is AI-led, with nearline HDD demand (HDD motor revenue +9.8% in FY3/26) and liquid-cooling modules (CDU) as the growth edge the company itself identifies, against a structurally declining client-HDD base. Competition is against MinebeaMitsumi and specialist Asian motor makers on cost and customisation; the barrier is co-development depth and capacity, and the substitute risk is technology shift (solid-state storage against HDDs, direct liquid-cooling architectures against module suppliers) — no company-disclosed market-size or share figure is carried in the sources used here, and none is substituted.

In automotive, Nidec supplies motors and components (EPS motors, brake and pump motors, BSG) and complete traction-motor systems (e-axle) to global OEMs and tier-1s; this is the chain where the model broke — Chinese EV price competition and over-built traction capacity produced sustained losses, the FY3/25–FY3/26 impairments (automotive product-group impairments of ¥231,085mn in FY3/26 alone) and the current order-review and fixed-cost programme. In appliance, commercial and industrial motors, the chain runs to appliance OEMs, HVAC makers and — the growth pole — data-centre developers and power-equipment channels buying generators and BESS, where the FY3/26 tanshin reports demand still rising steeply and capacity investment under way in India, France and North/Central America; appliance-motor utilisation of only about 50–60% (Transformation plan materials via the interview pack) marks the legacy end of the same chain. In machinery, Nidec sells presses, reducers, machine tools and inspection equipment into factory capex, competing with the domestic machine-tool industry on installed base and service. Entry barriers across the group are manufacturing know-how at scale, customer qualification cycles (longest in automotive and HDD) and a global plant footprint; the governance overlay is now itself a value-chain variable — the interview pack's central commercial question is whether hyperscaler and OEM customers will qualify and keep sole- or dual-sourcing critical motors and CDU from a supplier whose financial reporting carries a disclaimer of opinion, and no quantified customer attrition has been disclosed (未取得). The two sensitivities that follow: first, overseas sales are 90.4% of the total by customer location (Chapter 5), so FX and the China/EV complex dominate the demand mix; second, the growth the transformation plan depends on — AI data-centre physical infrastructure — is also the demand most exposed to a single capex cycle, and with no order or backlog disclosure in the tanshin, the earliest observable of a change is the quarterly print itself, which currently does not exist for FY3/27. Sources: FY3/26 tanshin qualitative section, 30 September 2026; PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); interview pack, 16 November 2026.

## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan — 'Re-Definition', the Transformation plan, and what is not yet a plan

Nidec has had three strategic frames in eighteen months, and this report keeps them separate and dated. The pre-crisis frame, Conversion2027, was abandoned with the scandal. The second frame is the 'Re-Definition' Nidec management-reform five-year plan (basic policy announced 13 May 2026, covering FY3/27–FY3/31), documented in the FY3/26 tanshin: governance and culture reform first ('必ず正しく' — 'be absolutely correct' — as the stated top management priority), a reorganised board with strengthened supervision, and a business rebuild around five pillars — Better Life; Electrification & Energy Infrastructure; Digital Society Solutions; Manufacturing Solutions; Mobility Innovation — with portfolio review, group restructuring (248 sites to 160–180; 353 entities to 160–180, as targeted in the May 2026 policy) and a group IT rebuild, aimed at raising ROIC. Critically, the tanshin states that the mid-term management plan containing business strategies and numerical plans is still being formulated on the basis of that policy: **audited or published multi-year numerical mid-term targets are therefore 未取得 (not disclosed) in the sources used, and none are presented here**. The third frame is the 'Transformation for Revival' plan (再生に向けての変革) announced by the new management on 1 October 2026 (interview pack; company plan materials via press): concentrate resources on five high-profit/growth areas — water-cooling modules/CDU for AI data centres, generators, BESS/power-conversion systems, HDD precision small motors, and one further data-centre-adjacent line, with machine tools redefined as an enabler of that infrastructure — apply selection and concentration to automotive and appliance motors (appliance capacity utilisation described as only about 50–60%), and fund the pivot partly from disposals, of which the first is signed: Nidec Components to a Carlyle entity for ¥103.0bn, closing 1 December 2026. Growth claims in the plan materials (water-cooling, generator and BESS sales each growing about 40%) carry no stated base period in the materials and are treated here as unquantified plan colour, not data (interview pack flag).

Shareholder-return policy as stated in the FY3/26 tanshin is a total return ratio target of 50%, combining stable and continuous dividends with opportunistic buybacks — a policy currently in abeyance in fact: the FY3/26 dividend was nil and the FY3/27 dividend is undecided, and no buyback is recorded in the periods covered here. The monitoring set that substitutes for a fuller plan, in the order the evidence arrives: (1) the H1 FY3/27 interim report by the extended deadline of 16 December 2026, and publication of the Q1 and Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, with or without an auditor review conclusion; (2) H1 FY3/27 operating profit against the ¥120,000mn H1 plan (unaudited management assertion) — the first hard test of the FY3/27 ¥200,000mn plan figure (unaudited management assertion); (3) the corrected filings: the audit opinion type on the restated accounts and any further change to cumulative profit or equity beyond the about ¥160.7bn already identified; (4) the TSE's listing-maintenance review outcome on the internal-control confirmation submitted 30 October 2026; (5) execution markers — the Components disposal closing (1 December 2026), further disposals, and the site/entity consolidation counts against the Re-Definition targets.
