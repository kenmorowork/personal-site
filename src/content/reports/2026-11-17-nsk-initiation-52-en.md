NSK is one of the world's largest bearing makers — Industrial Machinery (bearings and precision products for factory automation, machine tools and semiconductor equipment), Automotive (bearings and components) and, since September 2025, a re-consolidated Steering segment — and it is now pursuing a business integration with NTN, announced in May 2026. In FY3/2026 revenue was ¥911,644mn and operating profit ¥38,812mn (4.3% margin), owners' net income ¥22,867mn. FY3/2027 guidance has been raised twice — to revenue ¥1,050,000mn, reported OP ¥56,000mn, PBT ¥53,500mn and owners' net income ¥34,000mn (EPS ¥69.41, dividend ¥34) — on AI-related precision demand; **the company's adjusted OP guide is ¥71,000mn, the ~¥15,000mn difference being structural-reform and integration-related costs** (briefing materials, via secondary summary; the split is 未取得）. Strengths: (1) genuine operating leverage to the AI capex chain — **H1 FY3/27 Industrial Machinery OP rose to ¥18.5bn (8.6% margin) on semiconductor-equipment, machine-tool, aerospace and defence demand**, and management guides the environment to hold at least through H1 FY3/28; (2) guidance momentum — two raises by Q2, with H2 production volumes planned +10–20% vs H1 as the personnel bottleneck is fixed; (3) cash generation is strong and consistent (operating cash flow ¥97,806mn in FY3/26; positive in all ten years shown); (4) China at 21% of sales is a bearing-market position competitors cannot easily replicate, and China auto held up better than demand in H1 on electric-brake ball screws and local-OEM expansion (company Q&A); (5) the NTN integration, if executed, would create a domestic bearing champion with real synergy scope. Weaknesses: (1) a decade of value destruction in the P&L — OP margin peaked at 9.6% in FY3/2018 and has not exceeded 4.3% since FY3/2021; owners' net income in FY3/2021 was ¥355mn on ¥747,559mn of sales; (2) Automotive earned a −2.9% margin in FY3/2022 (a ¥13,220mn third-quarter segment loss) and only 4.3% in FY3/2026 — structurally the weakest leg; (3) comparability is poor: steering was inside Automotive (FY22–23), a discontinued operation (FY24–25, post-tax losses of ¥4,986mn and ¥1,875mn) and a consolidated segment again from September 2025 (FY26), and FY2027 filings restate FY2026 comparatives — almost no clean YoY line exists; (4) the reported-to-adjusted OP gap (~¥15,000mn guided for FY3/27) is large relative to reported OP and its split between reform and integration is undisclosed; (5) the NTN integration's definitive-agreement target date (12 November 2026, six months after the basic agreement) has passed — the interview pack records no commitment even to disclose a delay — and Steering, outside the mid-term-plan perimeter, is simultaneously being shopped for a strategic partner; (6) leverage is real: borrowings of ¥320,084mn against cash of ¥142,123mn at FY3/26-end (net debt ¥177,961mn, calculated). Rating and price target are Under Review placeholders (see Basis of preparation).

## Key points
- **Guidance raised twice by Q2** FY3/2027 guidance: initial 12 May 2026 revenue ¥1,000,000mn / OP ¥42,000mn / owners' NI ¥24,000mn; Rev.1 30 July 2026 ¥1,040,000mn / ¥50,000mn / ¥29,000mn; Rev.2 29 October 2026 ¥1,050,000mn / OP ¥56,000mn / PBT ¥53,500mn / NI ¥34,000mn / EPS ¥69.41. The company attributes the raises to Industrial Machinery demand for semiconductor production equipment and machine tools on expanding AI investment, plus a weaker yen (revision notices). **The adjusted OP guide of ¥71,000mn implies ~¥15,000mn of reform/integration costs in the reported line**.
- **H1 FY3/27: precision profit nearly quadrupled** H1 revenue ¥520.4bn (+26.2%), OP ¥30.1bn (+69.8%), owners' NI ¥18.8bn (EPS ¥38.5). **Industrial Machinery OP was ¥18.5bn (8.6% margin, +278% YoY)**; FX added +¥28.4bn to revenue and +¥7.1bn to OP (company). Two flatterers: Steering was consolidated for six months vs one month in the prior H1, and management concedes some orders are advance ordering. The binding constraint was personnel, not equipment — H2 volumes are planned +10–20% vs H1.
- **NTN integration: the timetable is the question** NSK and NTN announced a business-integration basic agreement in May 2026 (register S14) — the largest event in NSK's history, with no effect on reported figures through Q2 FY3/27. The six-month definitive-agreement target date (12 November 2026) has now passed; the interview pack records no disclosed commitment on next steps. **Execution, remedy and synergy disclosure from here is the single largest driver of the equity story — and of the ~¥15bn cost gap in the FY3/27 guide**.
- **Steering: out, in, and now shopped** The steering business sat inside Automotive through FY3/2023, was a discontinued operation in FY3/2024–25 (post-tax losses ¥4,986mn / ¥1,875mn), and returned as a consolidated reportable segment on 1 September 2025 when NSK consolidated NSK Steering Systems (now NSK Steering & Control): FY3/2026 Steering sales ¥100,554mn / profit ¥7,730mn on seven months. H1 FY3/27 printed ¥88.7bn / ¥3.1bn — against ¥4.3bn of OP in the single consolidated month a year earlier — and **the company is seeking a strategic partner for the business (briefing coverage)**. Clean run-rate disclosure is a gap.
- **A decade the P&L has not recovered** **FY3/2018: sales ¥1,020,338mn, OP ¥97,875mn (9.6%). FY3/2026: sales ¥911,644mn, OP ¥38,812mn (4.3%)**. The mid-term plan's successor framework targets a sustained 10% Industrial Machinery margin (briefing materials); H1 printed 8.6% at full demand stretch with 2–3-month lead times — pricing power, not volume, is the constraint on the plan.

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Exhibit 1 (page 1) summarises ten fiscal years, FY3/2017–FY3/2026, all IFRS. Three comparability breaks run through the window and are flagged wherever they bite: (i) FY3/2022–23 are as originally reported with the steering business inside continuing operations (mainly Automotive); (ii) FY3/2024–25 are continuing operations with steering a discontinued operation (post-tax results −¥4,986mn and −¥1,875mn, register S3); (iii) FY3/2026 includes NSK Steering Systems consolidated from 1 September 2025, with Steering a reportable segment. FY3/2022 segment figures are on the post-reorganisation basis (CMS moved from Other to Industrial Machinery; FY2023 filings' restated comparatives, register S6).

The arc is a long de-rating and a sharp recent turn. **Sales peaked at ¥1,020,338mn in FY3/2018 with OP of ¥97,875mn (9.6%); by FY3/2021 sales were ¥747,559mn and OP ¥6,364mn (0.9%), and owners' net income was ¥355mn** — total net income was negative (−¥748mn) with non-controlling interests absorbing the profit. The FY3/2022–23 recovery stalled at a 3.5% OP margin; the continuing-operations years FY3/2024–25 printed ¥27,391mn and ¥28,457mn. FY3/2026 OP of ¥38,812mn (4.3%) is the best since FY3/2019, and the FY3/2027 guide (¥56,000mn reported; ¥71,000mn adjusted) would still leave reported margin below the FY3/2018 level. Cash flow tells a better story than the P&L throughout: operating cash flow was positive in all ten years and reached ¥99,818mn in FY3/2024. NSK does not disclose order intake or backlog in its tanshin (ND in both blocks of the sheet); the demand gauge in the current cycle is lead times (2–3 months on precision products, company Q&A) rather than a backlog number.

## 3. Business description

NSK (founded 1916; headquartered in Shinagawa, Tokyo) is one of the world's largest manufacturers of bearings, with three reportable segments since September 2025: Industrial Machinery (industrial bearings, ball screws, linear guides, megatorque motors and condition-monitoring solutions), Automotive (bearings and automotive components including electric-brake ball screws) and Steering (electric power steering and steering columns through NSK Steering & Control), plus a small Other category. President & CEO is Akitoshi Ichii (Representative Executive Officer, in the role since April 2021); Keita Suzuki is CFO. NSK is a company with a nominating committee. In May 2026 NSK and NTN announced a basic agreement on a business integration (Chapter 8).

## Industrial Machinery — the profit engine of this cycle

Sales ¥377,491mn and profit ¥12,565mn in FY3/2026 (3.3% margin) — a weak full-year print that conceals the turn: H1 FY3/2027 delivered ¥214.5bn / ¥18.5bn at an 8.6% margin as semiconductor-production-equipment, machine-tool, aerospace and defence demand surged on AI-related investment. The segment's history is volatile: profit of ¥32,887mn on ¥269,974mn in FY3/2019, ¥7,697mn in FY3/2021, ¥35,541mn in FY3/2023, then ¥8,007mn in FY3/2024. The CMS (condition monitoring) business sits here from FY3/2023 (moved from Other). The full-year segment guide is OP ¥37.5bn reported / ¥44.0bn adjusted (10.1%).

## Automotive — scale without margin

The largest segment by sales for most of the window (¥696,271mn in FY3/2017; ¥403,304mn in FY3/2026 on the continuing basis) and the weakest earner: **profit of ¥64,577mn in FY3/2017 gave way to −¥4,018mn in FY3/2021 and −¥13,762mn in FY3/2022 (including a −¥13,220mn third quarter), recovering to ¥17,366mn (4.3%) in FY3/2026**. The FY3/2027 guide is revenue ¥403.0bn / OP ¥17.5bn — flat sales, ~5% adjusted margin ambition. In China, H1 sales held up better than demand on electric-brake ball screws and local-OEM expansion, and management says no plant-closure structural reform is needed there at this time, though small lines are being transferred or downsized and space reallocated to Industrial Machinery (company Q&A).

## Steering — the returning segment

Electric power steering systems, consolidated from 1 September 2025 when NSK took control of its steering JV (NSK Steering Systems, now NSK Steering & Control). FY3/2026 contribution (seven months): sales ¥100,554mn, profit ¥7,730mn (7.7%). H1 FY3/2027: ¥88.7bn / ¥3.1bn (3.5%) — margin well below the single-month print a year earlier (¥4.3bn on ¥15.2bn), and the clean half-year run-rate is not disclosed. The FY3/2027 guide is revenue ¥180.0bn / OP ¥6.0bn. The business sits outside the mid-term-plan perimeter and the company is seeking a strategic partner for it (briefing coverage); until it is partnered or sold it consumes capital and management attention inside a group simultaneously integrating with NTN.

## Other

Non-reportable businesses: sales ¥54,936mn and profit ¥484mn in FY3/2026 (FY3/2017: ¥52,858mn / ¥4,415mn). Note the label history: the sheet's third slot held 'Other' for FY2022–25 (sales ¥53,303mn–¥67,654mn) and holds Steering from FY2026, with Other moving to a fourth slot — the two are never spliced in this report.

## 4. Market and value chain

NSK competes in a global bearing oligopoly — SKF, Schaeffler, Timken, NTN, JTEKT and NSK — where ==the profit pool splits between commoditised automotive bearings (price-taker economics, EV-transition mix risk) and precision products for factory automation, where tolerances, installed-base trust and lead times defend margin==. The current cycle is an AI-capex derivative: semiconductor production equipment and machine tools pull high-precision bearings, ball screws and linear guides; aerospace/defence adds a second leg. NSK's own demand read (Q&A) is unusually specific — strength 'at least' through H1 FY3/28 with an advance-ordering caveat — and its constraint is labour, not plant. The NTN integration would combine Japan's #2 and #3 bearing makers; competition-authority remedies and the overlap map (automotive bearings above all) are unquantified in the sources read.

Market-size claims for bearings need their definitions attached, because the published range is unusually wide. The FIELDNOTES bearings report (16 Nov 2026) compiles agency forecasts side by side: a narrow rolling-bearings definition at $58.6bn in 2024 growing to $143.6bn by 2034 (Global Market Insights basis, CAGR 9.0%), a ball-and-roller-only definition at $29.5bn in 2024 growing to $38.7bn by 2035 (WiseGuy Reports, CAGR 2.5%), and a broad definition including plain and mounted bearings at $154.4bn in 2025 (Reports and Data, CAGR 4.5%). The common ground across definitions is narrower: automotive is the largest end-market (about 40% on the broad basis) and Asia-Pacific the largest region (51.2% on the narrow basis). The one segment with consistent high growth across sources is EV bearings — about $2.94bn in 2025 to about $5.87bn by 2030 (CAGR 14.9%) — where electrical-erosion protection (insulated, coated and ceramic-hybrid designs) is becoming a qualification requirement rather than a differentiator.

## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan

MTP2026 (FY2022–FY2026) has run its course; the successor framework reflected in FY2027 briefing materials targets, among other things, a sustained 10% Industrial Machinery operating margin (adjusted basis) — **the FY3/2027 segment guide already prints 10.1% adjusted, but only ¥37.5bn of reported OP against ¥44.0bn adjusted**, with the difference inside the ~¥15bn group reform/integration load. The strategic agenda is therefore threefold and simultaneous: (i) capture the precision cycle without repeating the FY2019–21 capacity hangover (the H2 +10–20% volume plan is hiring-led, not capex-led — capex was ¥51,894mn in FY3/2026, below the FY3/2023 peak of ¥62,085mn); (ii) execute the NTN integration — basic agreement May 2026, definitive agreement pending past its six-month target date; (iii) resolve Steering's perimeter (strategic partner sought) while it is consolidated on the balance sheet. DOE/dividend: the FY3/2027 dividend guide is ¥34 per share. Buyback and capital-policy specifics for the integration period are 未取得 in the sources read.
