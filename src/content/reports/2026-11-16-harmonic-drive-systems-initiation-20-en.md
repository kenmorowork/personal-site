A global niche monopoly whose earnings are a factory-utilisation story: Harmonic Drive Systems Inc. (6324, TSE Prime) is the world's leading maker of strain-wave gearing — the HarmonicDrive® precision speed reducers that sit in the joints of industrial robots, semiconductor production equipment and, increasingly, humanoid and medical robots — together with planetary reducers (Harmonic Planetary®, AccuDrive®) and mechatronics products (actuators, motors and controllers) sold under a 'Total Motion Control' proposition. No competitor mass-produces strain-wave gears of equivalent precision, and Shared Research (report of 11 November 2026) estimates the company's standalone marginal profit ratio at about 55%, which is why earnings swing so violently with volume: **operating profit was ¥16,624mn in FY3/19, ¥124mn in FY3/24 and ¥2,567mn in FY3/26** (revenue ¥59,557mn, +7.0% YoY), and company guidance for FY3/27 — raised twice, most recently on 11 November 2026 — is revenue of ¥78,000mn and operating profit of ¥10,200mn after **H1 order intake rose about 71% YoY**. Strengths: (1) a defensible technology moat in strain-wave gearing, built on the flexspline principle, proprietary materials and machining know-how, and decades of qualification history with robot and equipment makers; (2) exceptional operating leverage and disclosure — orders and backlog are published with the results materials, and H1 FY3/27 converted a 29.8% revenue increase into an 803.5% operating-profit increase; (3) a fortress balance sheet (equity ratio 72.2% at March 2026, 73.2% at September 2026) funding capacity built ahead of demand at the Hotaka/Ariake plants in Nagano. Weaknesses: (1) profitability is still far below both history and plan — the FY3/26 operating margin was 4.3% against 24.5% in FY3/19, ROE was 2.0%, and the previous mid-term plan's FY3/26 targets (revenue ¥90bn, operating margin 15–20%, ROE 10%+) were missed by wide margins; (2) the bottom line has been repeatedly distorted below the operating line — FY3/24 recorded a net loss to parent of ¥24,806mn despite a small operating profit, and FY3/25 net profit of ¥3,473mn rested on a ¥5,865mn gain on sales of investment securities while operating profit was ¥7mn; (3) demand concentration and cyclicality — industrial robots were 41.4% of the FY3/26 application mix on the Shared Research reading, China segment sales fell 28.0% in FY3/26, and revenue halved once before (FY3/19 ¥67,809mn to FY3/20 ¥37,487mn) when the robot capex cycle turned. Historical figures are as reported by the company (J-GAAP) from its tanshin and its published 11-year financial data, cross-checked against the Shared Research report of 11 November 2026; the only forward figures are company guidance and company plan targets, labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Harmonic Drive Systems reports under Japanese GAAP with a March year-end. The history is a study in operating leverage on a lumpy capex cycle. Revenue was ¥30,069mn in FY3/17 — a year in which total assets roughly doubled, from ¥48,749mn to ¥101,999mn, as the German business (today Harmonic Drive SE) was fully consolidated, and net profit to parent spiked to ¥19,732mn on one-off gains associated with that consolidation, well above ordinary profit of ¥7,958mn. The robot and semiconductor capex boom then took revenue to ¥54,339mn in FY3/18 (+80.7%) and **a peak of ¥67,809mn in FY3/19, with operating profit of ¥16,624mn (24.5% margin)** and ROE of 11.2%. The cycle broke in FY3/20: revenue fell 44.7% to ¥37,487mn, gross margin compressed from 44.9% to 32.0% as fixed costs were absorbed over half the volume, and the company printed an operating loss of ¥195mn and a net loss to parent of ¥1,095mn. FY3/21 was flat (revenue ¥37,034mn, operating profit ¥865mn) before the next upcycle: ¥57,087mn in FY3/22 (operating profit ¥8,739mn) and ¥71,527mn in FY3/23 (operating profit ¥10,224mn, 14.3% margin) — still the revenue record until the FY3/27 guidance year.

The FY3/24–FY3/26 trough is the second lesson. Revenue fell 22.0% to ¥55,796mn in FY3/24 as robot makers destocked; gross margin fell to 28.0%, operating profit to ¥124mn (0.2%), and below-the-line charges drove a net loss to parent of ¥24,806mn and ROE of −27.1% (company 11-year data). FY3/25 revenue was ¥55,645mn (−0.3%) with operating profit of ¥7mn on the detailed statement (¥6mn in the tanshin header, which truncates; see Basis of preparation) and ordinary profit of ¥151mn, yet net profit to parent was ¥3,473mn because extraordinary income included a ¥5,865mn gain on sales of investment securities, partly offset by ¥1,189mn of impairment losses. The balance sheet has carried the group through both troughs. ## 3. Business description

Harmonic Drive Systems is a precision-reducer specialist headquartered in Shinagawa, Tokyo, with its main plants at Hotaka and Ariake in Azumino, Nagano Prefecture. It operates a single business segment — precision speed reducers and mechatronics built around them — and sells worldwide through its own subsidiaries, principally Harmonic Drive SE in Germany (Europe), Harmonic Drive LLC and Harmonic AD in the United States, and Harmonic Drive Systems (Shanghai) in China. Consolidated subsidiaries' December year-ends (Europe, North America, China) mean quarterly geographic disclosure runs one quarter out of phase with the Japanese parent, a presentational point the company flags in its results materials.

## Product portfolio: reducers are 78% of sales

Two product categories are disclosed in the results materials. Speed reducers — principally HarmonicDrive® strain-wave gears, plus Harmonic Planetary® and AccuDrive® planetary reducers — were ¥46,333mn in FY3/26, 77.8% of consolidated revenue (+9.5% YoY, per the FY3/26 results presentation as compiled by third-party research summaries). Mechatronics products — rotary, linear and swing actuators, combined modules integrating a reducer with a motor and encoder, servo drivers/controllers and related systems — were the residual about ¥13,224mn (22.2%). The strategic direction is to raise content per joint: an integrated actuator (reducer + motor + encoder + driver) sells for a multiple of the bare reducer and embeds the company deeper in the customer's design, which is also the company's stated response to lower-priced Chinese strain-wave entrants. By application (FY3/26, Shared Research reading of the briefing materials), **industrial robots were 41.4% of the mix**, automotive parts 16.2% and semiconductor production equipment 14.8%, with the balance across machine tools, medical equipment, aerospace and other FA uses; the securities report itself does not disclose an application split, and robot-only revenue is not a reported line. Source: Shared Research report on 6324, 11 November 2026; FY3/26 results presentation, 21 May 2026; FY3/26 Annual Securities Report (filed 16 June 2026) as summarised in third-party research.

## Strain-wave gearing: principle, advantages and why it is hard to copy

A HarmonicDrive® reducer has three elements: a rigid circular spline (an outer ring with internal teeth), a flexspline (a thin-walled flexible cup with external teeth, typically two fewer than the circular spline) and a wave generator (an elliptical cam and bearing) that deforms the flexspline into mesh with the circular spline at two points. Each rotation of the wave generator advances the flexspline by the two-tooth difference, giving single-stage reduction ratios of roughly 50:1 to 160:1 in a coaxial, hollow-shaft package with near-zero backlash, high torsional stiffness and high positioning accuracy — the combination robot joints, wafer-transfer mechanisms and surgical devices require, and one that conventional multi-stage planetary gearboxes cannot match at the same size and weight. The principle was invented by C. W. Musser in the United States in the 1950s; Harmonic Drive Systems built its business on industrialising it in Japan.

The moat is manufacturing, not the expired principle. The flexspline must flex millions of cycles without fatigue failure while holding tooth-profile accuracy; that requires proprietary steel treatment, thin-wall machining and grinding, and inspection know-how accumulated over decades, plus the installed base's qualification history — a robot maker that has qualified a reducer for a joint design does not re-qualify a cheaper substitute lightly, because field failure costs dwarf the component price. Shared Research's assessment, adopted here as the benchmark view, is that no competitor mass-produces strain-wave gears of equivalent precision. The credible competitive pressure is at the edges: Chinese makers (for example Leaderdrive) competing on price in lower-precision and domestic-China applications, and alternative reduction technologies (cycloidal reducers, where Nabtesco is the leader in heavier robot joints) bounding the addressable joint set. Source: company technology materials (hds.co.jp, accessed November 2026); Shared Research report on 6324, 11 November 2026.

## Mechatronics: from component to Total Motion Control

The mechatronics line integrates the reducer with motors, encoders, drivers and control software into rotary actuators, linear actuators, swing actuators and combined modules, sold under the company's 'Total Motion Control' concept. The Q1 FY3/27 results illustrated the line's torque: **North American mechatronics orders rose 336.2% YoY to ¥4,416mn in the quarter**, with the backlog up 176.2% to ¥5,330mn, driven by a large order for surgical-robot applications in North America (company Q1 results materials, 7 August 2026, as summarised from the filing). Mechatronics is also where the newer demand fields appear first — medical robots, humanoid/AI-robot development programmes and semiconductor equipment subsystems are typically bought as integrated actuators rather than bare gears. The financial significance is mix: mechatronics carries more bought-in content (motors, electronics) than the reducer line, so its gross margin structure differs, but it raises switching costs and the revenue captured per robot. Product-level profit is not disclosed, so this mix effect cannot be quantified from public filings. Source: Q1 FY3/27 results materials, 7 August 2026; company product materials, accessed November 2026.

## Production system: Nagano capacity built ahead of the cycle

Production is concentrated in Azumino, Nagano — the Hotaka plant and the Ariake plant — with Harmonic Drive SE manufacturing in Germany and US operations in Massachusetts. The defining capital decision of the last cycle was the Ariake capacity expansion announced in 2021 (about ¥6.5bn to lift strain-wave capacity from 150,000 to 220,000 units per month, completed in August 2022, split between industrial-machinery and automotive applications, per the company's capacity notice of 13 October 2021). That capacity arrived just as the FY3/24 destocking hit, which is the proximate cause of the margin collapse in Chapter 1 — and, symmetrically, of the speed of the FY3/27 recovery, because the guided volume increase requires little incremental fixed cost. Company commentary reported in October 2026 put **the revenue capacity of the Hotaka and Ariake plants at about ¥80bn at full utilisation** — essentially the FY3/27 guided revenue level — which frames the next capital question: growth beyond about ¥80bn of revenue requires either the new mid-term plan's further capacity steps or a step-up in overseas production (third-party reports in November 2026 described a North American capacity expansion; the company filing itself was not verified for this report and the item is flagged as third-party). Capital investment was ¥4,955mn in FY3/24 (company 11-year data; FY3/25–FY3/26 capex is in the securities report and was not separately verified here) against depreciation of ¥7,361mn in FY3/26, so the group is currently harvesting the earlier investment phase. Source: company capacity notice, 13 October 2021; company 11-year financial data; FY3/26 tanshin cash-flow statement, 13 May 2026.

## 4. Market and value chain

Harmonic Drive Systems sells into the capex of three big equipment cycles — industrial robots, semiconductor production equipment and factory automation generally — plus automotive parts production, medical devices and emerging humanoid/AI-robot programmes. The value chain runs from specialty steels and bought-in motors/encoders, through the reducer/actuator maker, to robot OEMs (the Japanese, European and Chinese articulated-robot makers), semiconductor-equipment makers, and system integrators, and finally to end-users in automotive, electronics, logistics and healthcare. The reducer is a small share of the end product's cost but a large share of its precision and reliability, which is the structural source of the company's pricing power in upcycles and of its order volatility in downcycles: customers double-order and build inventory when lead times stretch, then destock for quarters, as in FY3/24.

Market structure favours the incumbent at the precision end. In strain-wave gearing, Harmonic Drive Systems is the global niche leader and, on the Shared Research assessment, has no mass-production rival at equivalent precision; the realistic competitive set is (1) Chinese strain-wave makers gaining share in mid-range and China-local applications on price, (2) cycloidal reducers (Nabtesco's RV reducers) which dominate the large, high-torque base and shoulder joints of industrial robots and bound the strain-wave addressable market to the lighter, precision joints, (3) planetary reducers for lower-precision axes, and (4) direct-drive and quasi-direct-drive architectures in some humanoid designs, which would bypass reducers entirely if torque density and cost allow — a genuine long-term substitute risk for the humanoid use case specifically. Entry barriers are the flexspline materials/machining know-how, fatigue-life validation data, and OEM qualification cycles measured in years; the same barriers mean share shifts are slow in both directions.

Demand context at initiation is strongly positive but late-cycle in character. Readers should therefore underwrite the robot and semiconductor-equipment recovery that is in the filings, and treat humanoid volume production as unpriced optionality rather than as a forecast input. ## 5. Segments, applications and orders

The company reports one business segment (precision reducers) and geographic segment information for Japan, China, North America and Europe based on the location of the selling/manufacturing entity. Two features of this disclosure need stating plainly. First, segment profit is measured on an ordinary-profit basis (the tanshin narrative reports 'segment profit (ordinary profit)'), not the operating-profit basis most Japanese peers use, and segment totals therefore reconcile to consolidated ordinary profit after adjustments, including the amortisation of the customer-related and technical intangible assets recognised on the Harmonic Drive SE acquisition (¥1,008mn in Europe in FY3/26). Second, overseas subsidiaries close their books in December, so their contribution to each Japanese fiscal period is offset by one quarter.

## Geographic segments: Japan and Europe carry the revenue; Japan carries the profit

FY3/26 external sales and segment profit (ordinary-profit basis) were: Japan ¥26,626mn (+22.5% YoY) and ¥3,693mn (+66.1%); China ¥4,046mn (−28.0%) and ¥382mn (+26.5%); North America ¥12,107mn (+4.1%) with segment profit not verified in the sources accessed for this report (a third-party research summary put North American profit at about US$3.5mn, down about 5%, citing IT-system upgrade costs — flagged as third-party and not used in Exhibit 2); Europe ¥16,776mn (+0.7%) and ¥644mn (a profit, against a ¥52mn loss in FY3/25), despite absorbing ¥1,008mn of SE-acquisition intangible amortisation. Exhibit 2 sets out the verified figures. On the customer-location view summarised by Shared Research, the FY3/26 regional mix was Asia including Japan 47.7%, Europe 28.2% and North America 20.3% (the balance other regions) — a different cut from the entity-location segment sales above, and the two should not be mixed.

The patterns to monitor are three. **Japan is the manufacturing hub: its 13.9% segment margin in FY3/26** (¥3,693mn on ¥26,626mn) reflects parent-company production for the group, so Japan's margin is the cleanest read on Nagano utilisation. China is the swing segment: sales fell 28.0% in FY3/26 on weak industrial-robot demand even as segment profit rose on mix, and China is where local strain-wave competition is most direct. Europe is structurally diluted by the SE intangible amortisation (customer-related assets of ¥9,187mn and technical assets of ¥2,453mn remained on the March 2026 balance sheet), so Europe's reported margin understates its cash contribution until that amortisation rolls off. In H1 FY3/27 the tanshin reports sales growth in all four segments, led by Japan, Europe and North America, with a rising overseas sales ratio supporting profit. ## Applications and the order book

Orders are the leading indicator this company is managed by, and the disclosure (in the results presentation, not the tanshin) is unusually granular by region, product and application. Total order intake was ¥61,613mn in FY3/26 (company results materials, as compiled in third-party summaries of the filings) against revenue of ¥59,557mn — **a book-to-bill of about 1.03x** in a year that still felt like a trough. Q1 FY3/27 intake was ¥24,128mn (+55.7% YoY) and the backlog reached ¥29,457mn (+45.6%) at June 2026; H1 intake was about ¥50,390mn (+70.7%), implying the backlog built further through Q2 (the September backlog figure itself was not verified in the sources accessed and is not stated here). By application, the FY3/26 sales mix on the Shared Research reading was industrial robots 41.4%, automotive parts 16.2% and semiconductor equipment 14.8%; in Q1 FY3/27, industrial-robot orders were about ¥6,100mn (about a quarter of the quarter's intake) and robot sales about ¥4,900mn (29.5% of quarterly sales) on the presentation charts, while the growth rates were led by North American mechatronics (surgical robots) and semiconductor equipment.

The investment-relevant distinction is between the two order engines. The semiconductor-equipment engine is tied to AI-driven wafer-fab capex and is growing fastest (Q2 orders reportedly +178% YoY) but is the most cyclical and the most prone to the double-ordering that produced the FY3/24 air pocket. The robot engine — articulated robots restocking after destocking, collaborative robots, medical robots and, prospectively, humanoids — is broader but slower: industrial-robot orders grew about 26.5% in Q1 and about 51% in Q2 on the reported summaries, from a depressed base. Automotive parts (16.2% of the FY3/26 mix) was the declining leg in FY3/26, with Japan-segment automotive sales down on the year. ## 7. Strategy, mid-term plan and long-term vision

The operative plan is the new mid-term management plan announced with the FY3/26 results in May 2026, covering FY3/27–FY3/31 (the plan period as summarised by Shared Research from the 13 May 2026 announcement; a June 2026 securities-report summary describes the same plan as drawn up with the 38th Annual Securities Report — see Basis of preparation). Its FY3/31 targets are consolidated revenue of ¥100,000mn or more, an operating margin of 15% or more, ROE and ROIC of 10% or more, and GHG emissions down 30% versus FY2022, with AI robots, aerospace/defence and e-mobility as the focus growth fields alongside the core industrial-robot and semiconductor-equipment businesses. All figures in this paragraph are company plan targets, not J-Core Fundamental Research estimates.

The plan must be read against the scorecard of its predecessor. The FY3/24–FY3/26 plan (announced 20 May 2024) targeted FY3/26 revenue of ¥90,000mn, an operating margin of 15–20%, ROE of 10% or more and ROIC of 10% or more, under a long-term vision of revenue above ¥100bn by FY3/30 (in the plan's labelling). **Actual FY3/26 delivery was revenue ¥59,557mn (66% of target), an operating margin of 4.3%, ROE of 2.0%** and ordinary profit to total assets of 2.3% — a miss the company attributes to the robot-destocking cycle and to AI-robot demand starting more slowly than assumed, and which it has answered by pushing the ¥100bn revenue ambition out to FY3/31 and adding aerospace/defence and e-mobility as nearer-term fields. The FY3/27 guidance of ¥78,000mn (Chapter 2) is the new plan's first-year marker: it requires the Nagano plants to run at close to their reported full-utilisation revenue level of about ¥80bn, so the plan's outer years implicitly require capacity and/or overseas-production decisions that have not yet been fully disclosed. The monitoring variables are therefore the quarterly order intake and backlog (the plan's demand proof), the Japan segment margin (the utilisation proof) and any capacity announcement (the supply proof). Source: mid-term plan materials, 20 May 2024 and May 2026 (company, via hds.co.jp); Shared Research report on 6324, 11 November 2026; FY3/26 tanshin, 13 May 2026.
