The order book, not the cycle, is now the story: Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Ltd. (7011, TSE Prime) is Japan's largest heavy-industry group — gas turbine combined cycle (GTCC) and nuclear power in Energy Systems; metals machinery, engineering and commercial ships in Plants & Infrastructure; thermal, engine and drive products (now Industrial Solutions); and aircraft, defence and space systems. The initiation question is whether the earnings step-change of the last three years is a backlog-conversion story with years to run, or a coinciding peak of GTCC, defence and nuclear demand. The disclosed evidence leans to conversion: order intake has risen from ¥3,336,300mn in FY3/21 to ¥7,653,600mn in FY3/26 (continuing basis), **the order backlog stood at ¥14,225,600mn at September 2026 (briefing materials — about 2.4x guided FY3/27 revenue)**, and on 6 November 2026 the company raised FY3/27 guidance to revenue of ¥6,000,000mn and business profit of ¥600,000mn (company guidance), above the 2024 Medium-Term Business Plan's final-year targets (business profit of ¥450,000mn or higher; ROE of 12%) in the plan's last year. Business profit — MHI's headline profit measure (revenue − cost of sales − SG&A − other expenses + share of profit of associates + other income) — has risen every year since the FY3/20 loss of ¥29,538mn, printing a record ¥432,218mn in FY3/26 at an 8.7% margin, with gross margin expanding from 15.8% in FY3/21 to 21.8% in FY3/26. Strengths: (1) backlog quality and mix — H1 FY3/27 orders of ¥3,612,500mn were led by GTCC (¥1,568,700mn; 19 large-frame units), 94% of 2024–25 GT orders came from utilities and IPPs, and management reports no order cancellations in the past one to two years (company Q&A, 6 November 2026); (2) a second, policy-funded leg — defence orders under Japan's defence build-up programme and the April 2026 Australian frigate contract sit inside an Aircraft, Defense & Space segment whose revenue rose 35.3% in FY3/26; (3) capital discipline arriving with the cash — **a buyback of up to ¥300,000mn (up to 120mn shares, 3.6% of shares ex-treasury, all to be cancelled)** was resolved on 6 November 2026 alongside guided FY3/27 free cash flow of ¥800,000mn (company guidance). Weaknesses: (1) execution risk inside the boom — H1 FY3/27 absorbed a product-warranty provision of about ¥67,500mn for parts replacement at the Nakoso and Hirono IGCC plants delivered in 2021, plus a ¥20,000mn risk buffer in guidance; Q2 standalone business profit fell to ¥89,659mn (calculated), a 6.7% margin; (2) the H2 FY3/27 order ask is concentrated — Defence & Space orders of ¥153,800mn in H1 (interview pack, company briefing) must recover sharply against a ¥1,800,000mn segment orders guide, and GTCC must roughly repeat H1's ¥1,568,700mn to reach its ¥3,000,000mn guide; (3) comparability breaks — Mitsubishi Logisnext was classified as a discontinued operation in FY3/26 and deconsolidated on 1 May 2026, the segment structure was reorganised on 1 April 2026 (Logistics, Thermal & Drive Systems became Industrial Solutions), and the ten-year history in this report crosses the FY3/19 move from J-GAAP to IFRS, so no single clean series spans the decade. All historical figures are as reported by the company via the PL/segment sheet built from tanshin in the JPX disclosure archive; the only forward figures are company guidance and company plan targets, labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## Key points

- **H1 FY3/27: record orders, revenue and profit — with a ¥67.5bn charge inside.** For the six months to 30 September 2026 (IFRS, continuing operations), revenue was ¥2,523,269mn (+19.4% YoY), business profit ¥249,304mn (+45.3%; margin 9.9% vs 8.1%), profit before tax ¥263,214mn and net income attributable to owners ¥203,291mn (+76.9%); basic EPS was ¥60.49. Orders received were ¥3,612,500mn (+9.0%, a first-half record) and the order backlog reached ¥14,225,600mn, up ¥988,000mn from March 2026 (briefing materials). The half absorbed a product-warranty provision of about ¥67,500mn in Energy for parts replacement at the Nakoso and Hirono IGCC plants; **Q2 standalone business profit (derived as H1 minus Q1, both as reported) was ¥89,659mn, a 6.7% margin, against Q1's ¥159,645mn (13.4%)**. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin [IFRS], 6 November 2026; Q1 FY3/27 tanshin, 2026; company Q&A summary, 6 November 2026.
- **Guidance raised hard — revenue ¥6,000,000mn, business profit ¥600,000mn (company guidance).** Full-year FY3/27 company guidance (会社計画) was raised on 6 November 2026 — the only revision of the year — to orders of ¥8,000,000mn (from ¥7,000,000mn), revenue ¥6,000,000mn (from ¥5,400,000mn at the initial May 2026 plan), business profit ¥600,000mn (from ¥540,000mn), profit before tax ¥600,000mn (from ¥530,000mn) and owners' net income ¥440,000mn (from ¥380,000mn), with basic EPS of ¥130.91, guided ROE of 14% and guided free cash flow of ¥800,000mn; FX assumptions are USD/JPY 150 and EUR/JPY 180. The stated reason (revision notice, 6 November 2026) is smooth construction progress and profitability improvement in GTCC and other lines in Energy and in Aircraft, Defense & Space. H1 delivered 42.1% of guided revenue and 41.6% of guided business profit, so the guide implies H2 business profit of ¥350,696mn (calculated) — and the ¥600,000mn already carries the ¥20,000mn risk buffer; **the CFO told the briefing that underlying earnings power is about ¥690,000mn (the guide plus the ~¥70,000mn IGCC provision and the ¥20,000mn buffer)** and that this is the starting point for the 2027 Business Plan. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 6 November 2026; guidance revision notice, 6 November 2026; company Q&A summary.
- **Capital return steps up: ¥300,000mn buyback, all shares to be cancelled.** The board resolved on 6 November 2026 (disclosed as a subsequent event in the Q2 tanshin) to repurchase up to 120,000,000 shares (3.6% of shares outstanding excluding treasury) for up to ¥300,000mn by market purchase on the TSE between 9 November 2026 and 8 November 2027, and to cancel all shares acquired. The dividend forecast was left unchanged at ¥29 per share (interim ¥14 decided), +¥4 YoY. The CFO's stated logic (Q&A summary) is that the scale and timing of future large investments have become visible enough, and equity has accumulated enough, to optimise the capital level ahead of the 2027 Business Plan without hindering growth investment; DOE remains the basic shareholder-return framework and ROE the profitability target. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin (subsequent-events note), 6 November 2026; company Q&A summary.
- **Strength — the backlog is the earnings bridge: ¥14.2tn, GTCC about ¥6tn of it.** Order intake (continuing basis, as reported) rose from ¥4,067,700mn in FY3/22 to ¥4,501,300mn in FY3/23, ¥6,684,000mn in FY3/24, ¥7,071,200mn in FY3/25 and ¥7,653,600mn in FY3/26, and the backlog at September 2026 was ¥14,225,600mn (briefing materials), of which GTCC-related backlog is about ¥6,000,000mn and the GTCC backlog excluding long-term service agreements about ¥4,000,000mn, converting to revenue through FY3/31 (company Q&A). Energy segment orders in FY3/26 were ¥3,936,700mn, +50% YoY (company results briefing, May 2026). Management's demand frame (Q&A): global gas-turbine demand averaging over 70GW per year for five years, possibly about 100GW in 2026; 94% of 2024–25 orders from utilities/IPPs and over 80% advanced-class (J-series) frames; the J-series fleet in operation roughly doubles between FY3/26 and FY3/31, compounding high-temperature-parts after-sales demand. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 briefing materials and Q&A summary, 6 November 2026.
- **Weakness — the guide's H2 ask is concentrated in defence orders and GTCC repetition.** Within H1 orders of ¥3,612,500mn, **Defence & Space orders were ¥153,800mn against ¥427,600mn in H1 FY3/26** (interview pack, from company briefing materials) — the prior year's large orders rolling off — while the full-year Aircraft, Defense & Space orders guide is ¥1,800,000mn, of which H1 segment orders of ¥296,800mn are only 16%. GTCC must add roughly ¥1,430,000mn in H2 to reach its ¥3,000,000mn orders guide (company guidance). The interview pack's three verification points for Q3 FY3/27 (expected early February 2027) are cumulative GTCC orders against the ¥3,000,000mn guide, the Defence & Space order recovery, and any addition to the IGCC provision beyond the ~¥90,000mn full-year burden (provision plus buffer). Defence & Space standalone profit and the Australian frigate contract value are not disclosed (未取得). Source: interview pack 7011 (2026-11-16); Q2 FY3/27 briefing materials, 6 November 2026.

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

MHI reports under IFRS with a March year-end; FY3/17 and FY3/18 in the ten-year history are J-GAAP as originally reported, and IFRS applies from FY3/19 (the basis of each year is flagged in Exhibit 1). The history in this chapter and in Exhibit 1 (page 1) is compiled in the PL/segment sheet for 7011 (FY3/22–FY3/26 in the template block plus 20 quarters, and FY3/17–FY3/21 in the sheet's supplementary history block; all template checks equal zero). MHI's headline profit measure is business profit (事業利益): revenue − cost of sales − SG&A − other expenses + share of profit of associates + other income, where other income/expenses include dividends received, gains/losses on fixed-asset sales and impairment. Figures are ¥mn unless stated.

The decade divides into three acts. Act one (FY3/17–FY3/18, J-GAAP): revenue of ¥3,914,018mn and ¥4,110,816mn with operating profit of ¥150,543mn and ¥126,530mn — a mid-single-digit-margin heavy-industry profile carrying the Mitsubishi Regional Jet (SpaceJet) development burden in the aircraft business. Act two (FY3/19–FY3/21, IFRS): revenue stalled at ¥4,078,344mn / ¥4,041,376mn / ¥3,699,946mn and business profit printed ¥186,724mn, then a loss of ¥29,538mn in FY3/20 — other expenses ballooned to ¥236,350mn in FY3/20 on SpaceJet and other programme charges, partly masked below the line by a ¥139,945mn tax credit that left net income positive at ¥107,284mn — and a recovery to only ¥54,081mn in FY3/21, when Aircraft, Defense & Space lost ¥94,841mn at segment level (sheet history block, FY3/21 segment basis). Operating cash flow was negative ¥94,948mn in FY3/21. Act three (FY3/22–FY3/26): the order cycle turned. Order intake — disclosed as an annual total in the tanshin narrative and treated in this report as a first-class KPI — rose from ¥4,067,700mn (FY3/22) through ¥4,501,300mn (FY3/23) and ¥6,684,000mn (FY3/24, +48.5%) to ¥7,071,200mn (FY3/25) and ¥7,653,600mn (FY3/26, continuing basis), driven first by defence (Japan's build-up programme) and then by GTCC as North American and Asian power demand — data-centre load growth on top of utility replacement demand — hit a supply-constrained gas-turbine market. Revenue followed with a lag: ¥3,860,283mn / ¥4,202,797mn / ¥4,657,147mn / ¥5,027,176mn (FY3/25 as originally reported, including Mitsubishi Logisnext) / ¥4,974,168mn (FY3/26, continuing operations only; +14.1% against the restated continuing FY3/25 figure of ¥4,361,127mn).

Profitability compounded rather than merely recovered. Gross profit rose from ¥583,482mn in FY3/21 (15.8% margin) to ¥1,082,674mn in FY3/26 (21.8%), on mix (GTCC after-sales, defence volume), price improvement at order intake — management states profitability at the time of order for new gas turbines has been improving for about two years (Q&A, 6 November 2026) — and the exit of low-margin volume. Business profit rose from ¥160,240mn (FY3/22, 4.2% margin) to ¥193,324mn, ¥282,541mn, ¥383,198mn (FY3/25 as reported; ¥354,965mn on the restated continuing basis) and a record ¥432,218mn in FY3/26 (8.7% margin, continuing). Profit before tax (continuing) was ¥474,694mn in FY3/26 and net income attributable to owners a record ¥332,129mn (+35.3%), giving basic EPS of ¥98.86 and ROE of 12.2% (tanshin header) — against ROE of 10.7% in FY3/25. The balance sheet swung to net cash on the sheet's definitions: cash and equivalents of ¥1,334,874mn at March 2026 against bonds, borrowings and other financial liabilities of ¥876,240mn as presented (a definition that includes lease liabilities and therefore exceeds the company-defined interest-bearing debt — see Basis of preparation), helped by operating cash flow of ¥942,619mn in FY3/26 and the Logisnext transaction proceeds. Owners' equity was ¥3,088,566mn (equity ratio 37.3%; book value per share ¥919.16). The order backlog — disclosed in briefing materials, not in the tanshin — exceeded ¥13,000,000mn at March 2026 (¥13,237,600mn derived from the September 2026 figure and its stated increase) and reached ¥14,225,600mn at September 2026. ## 2. Latest update — Q2 FY3/27 results, guidance raise and ¥300bn buyback

MHI reported Q2 FY3/27 (H1, 1 April–30 September 2026) results on 6 November 2026, with the Logisnext business deconsolidated (transaction completed 1 May 2026) and presented as a discontinued operation, and with the reportable segments reorganised from 1 April 2026: the former Logistics, Thermal & Drive Systems segment — minus Logisnext — became Industrial Solutions, and the Data Center & Energy Management business was transferred into it; prior-period segment figures are restated onto the new basis in the Q2 tanshin. President and CEO Eisaku Ito and CFO Hiroshi Nishio presented the results; the company publishes a Q&A summary of the briefing (an edited company record, not a verbatim transcript), which is in the programme's transcript library and is used for the colour in this chapter. Three decisions landed the same day: the FY3/27 guidance raise, a buyback of up to ¥300,000mn with cancellation, and an unchanged ¥29 dividend forecast. This chapter summarises the release; segment detail is in Chapter 5.

## H1 FY3/27 results (cumulative, as reported, IFRS, continuing operations)

Revenue was ¥2,523,269mn (+19.4% YoY), business profit ¥249,304mn (+45.3%; margin 9.9% against 8.1% in H1 FY3/26), profit before tax ¥263,214mn (+51.5%), interim net income ¥207,255mn and net income attributable to owners ¥203,291mn (+76.9%); basic EPS was ¥60.49. Orders received were ¥3,612,500mn (+9.0% YoY, a first-half record; briefing materials) and the order backlog ¥14,225,600mn at 30 September 2026 (+¥988,000mn from March 2026; briefing materials). The quarterly split is the analytical centre of the print: Q1 FY3/27 standalone printed revenue of ¥1,194,203mn (+15.5%) and business profit of ¥159,645mn (+65.1%, a 13.4% margin), so Q2 standalone (derived as H1 minus Q1, both as reported) contributed revenue of ¥1,329,066mn and business profit of ¥89,659mn — a 6.7% margin — because Q2 absorbed the IGCC product-warranty provision of about ¥67,500mn (the briefing materials state the exact amount as ¥67,500mn; the Q&A record rounds it to about ¥70,000mn) for periodic parts replacement at the Nakoso and Hirono IGCC plants delivered in 2021. **Excluding that provision, the interview pack calculates H1 business-profit margin at about 12.7% and Energy's Q2 standalone margin at just under 20%**. The CFO stated in the Q&A that the parts in question are used only at those two plants, that no spillover to other plants is expected, that the provision is the current best estimate of future periodic replacement costs rather than a single construction outlay, and that the ¥20,000mn risk buffer held in guidance is not earmarked to an IGCC shortfall or any other specific risk.

By segment (new basis; total revenue including internal sales / business profit, Q2 tanshin segment note): Energy ¥1,122,525mn / ¥145,129mn (external revenue ¥1,119,458mn; +29.0% / +79.6% YoY on the restated prior half); Plants & Infrastructure ¥429,801mn / ¥41,452mn (external ¥410,765mn; profit −7.1% YoY despite revenue growth); Industrial Solutions ¥352,693mn / ¥14,788mn (external ¥349,001mn; profit +120.0% YoY off a low base); Aircraft, Defense & Space ¥633,456mn / ¥76,361mn (external ¥633,308mn; +17.6% / +26.5%). Other businesses contributed ¥14,939mn of profit and corporate/eliminations −¥43,367mn. Within Energy (briefing materials): GTCC orders ¥1,568,700mn on 19 large-frame units (8GW), GTCC revenue ¥587,100mn; steam power orders ¥239,600mn / revenue ¥175,900mn; aero engines orders ¥182,100mn / revenue ¥171,300mn; nuclear orders ¥278,400mn / revenue ¥152,100mn. Within Aircraft, Defense & Space, Defence & Space revenue was ¥489,600mn (H1 FY3/26: ¥419,300mn) on smooth programme progress, but Defence & Space orders fell to ¥153,800mn from ¥427,600mn as the prior year's large orders rolled off (interview pack, from briefing materials; Defence & Space standalone profit is not disclosed). H1 free cash flow was ¥468,300mn (operating cash flow ¥394,500mn; investing cash flow +¥73,800mn, including Logisnext share-transfer proceeds; briefing materials). The discontinued Logisnext business recorded H1 revenue of ¥49,904mn and a pre-tax loss of ¥5,665mn, including a ¥34,806mn share-transfer loss partly offset by a ¥24,535mn realised foreign-exchange translation gain, and a post-tax profit of ¥5,321mn after a ¥10,986mn tax credit (Q2 tanshin discontinued-operations note). The balance sheet at 30 September 2026: total assets ¥8,314,295mn, owners' equity ¥3,217,056mn (ratio 38.7%), cash and equivalents ¥1,706,718mn and contract liabilities of ¥2,573,339mn (from ¥2,161,881mn at March 2026) — the advance-payment engine of the backlog model. Market reaction is part of the record: the shares fell 4.6% on 6 November 2026 itself and 7.4% on 13 November 2026, closing at ¥2,900 on 16 November 2026, 44% below the 2 March 2026 high of ¥5,208 (TipRanks / Finnhub via search results) — the raise was not enough for a market that had priced the boom earlier in the year. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin [IFRS], 6 November 2026; Q1 FY3/27 tanshin [IFRS], 2026; Q2 FY3/27 briefing materials and Q&A summary, 6 November 2026.

## 3. Business description

MHI is headquartered in Marunouchi, Tokyo, and organises itself into four reportable segments. The segment definitions below are the company's own (Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, segment note, on the structure effective 1 April 2026): Energy Systems (thermal power systems — GTCC and steam power — nuclear power systems, aero engines, compressors, marine machinery); Plants & Infrastructure Systems (metals machinery, commercial ships, engineering — transportation systems and chemical plants — environmental systems, and machinery systems); Industrial Solutions (thermal products, engines, turbochargers, car air-conditioners, and data centre & energy management — the continuing remainder of the former Logistics, Thermal & Drive Systems after the Logisnext exit); and Aircraft, Defense & Space (commercial aircraft, defence aircraft, missiles, naval ships, special vehicles including torpedoes, and space systems). Segment profit is business profit. In FY3/26 (continuing operations, external revenue basis) the mix was Energy 41.3%, Plants & Infrastructure 16.4%, Logistics/Thermal/Drive (continuing parts) 12.6% and Aircraft, Defense & Space 28.0%, with ¥74,467mn of other-business external revenue. The group's common thread is long-cycle, engineer-to-order heavy equipment sold to utilities, governments and industrial customers, with a growing after-sales and service annuity on the installed base — the GTCC long-term service model is the clearest example.

## Energy Systems: GTCC at the centre, nuclear behind it, aero engines attached

Energy is the group's largest and most profitable segment: external revenue of ¥1,643,374mn / ¥1,731,444mn / ¥1,752,569mn / ¥1,803,878mn / ¥2,053,956mn across FY3/22–FY3/26 (as reported in each year's own tanshin; FY3/26 continuing) with segment business profit of ¥86,268mn / ¥85,160mn / ¥141,570mn / ¥205,356mn / ¥267,272mn — **margins of 5.2% / 4.9% / 8.1% / 11.4% / 13.0%**. The engine is GTCC: MHI's J-series (and JAC) large-frame gas turbines are one of three global large-frame franchises, sold into a market where management estimates demand at over 70GW per year on a five-year average and possibly about 100GW in 2026 (Q&A, 6 November 2026). The order evidence: GTCC orders of ¥1,568,700mn in H1 FY3/27 alone (19 large-frame units, 8GW), a GTCC backlog of about ¥6,000,000mn (about ¥4,000,000mn excluding LTSA), and a guided ¥3,000,000mn of GTCC orders for FY3/27 (company guidance). The business model compounds: each installed J-series unit generates decades of high-temperature-parts replacement and LTSA service revenue — the fleet in operation is expected roughly to double by FY3/31 (company statement) — and management is investing about ¥500,000mn in GTCC growth capacity, concentrated on high-temperature parts, while doubling new-unit output through lead-time shortening and productivity rather than large greenfield capex (Q&A). Q2 FY3/27's after-sales ratio was 42% of GTCC revenue (interview pack) — temporarily low as new-unit revenue surges — and Energy's Q2 standalone margin excluding the IGCC provision was just under 20%. The IGCC provision is the segment's open wound: the Nakoso and Hirono IGCC plants (delivered 2021) need periodic parts replacement to operate stably, provided at about ¥67,500mn in Q2 FY3/27; the CFO states the parts are unique to those two plants. Nuclear is the second leg: light-water reactors (restarts, maintenance and fuel), the nuclear fuel cycle, and the SRZ-1200 next-generation reactor in development — H1 FY3/27 nuclear orders were ¥278,400mn and revenue ¥152,100mn, and FY3/26 orders included fuel-cycle facility construction work (company briefing). Aero engines (commercial engine programmes and defence engines) printed H1 orders of ¥182,100mn and revenue of ¥171,300mn. Steam power is in structural decline as a new-build market but carries a large service base; compressors and marine machinery round out the segment. ## 4. Market and value chain

MHI sells into four distinct value chains that share one trait: ==the customer buys availability and lifecycle cost, not a commodity unit==. In large-frame gas turbines, the chain runs from forged and cast high-temperature components (a concentrated, capacity-constrained supplier tier that MHI partly internalises at Takasago) through the OEM — a global oligopoly of MHI, GE Vernova and Siemens Energy — to utilities and IPPs, which were 94% of MHI's 2024–25 GT orders (company Q&A), with data-centre demand arriving mostly indirectly, through utility load growth (management estimates data centres at a little over 10% of US power demand and a few percent globally). Entry barriers are thermodynamic and reputational: firing-temperature materials science, a proven operating fleet, and the service network that makes a 30-year LTSA bankable; the substitute risks are renewables-plus-storage at the margin of new capacity and, in the current squeeze, delivery-slot scarcity itself — which is why MHI can double output by lead-time reduction and still sell forward to FY3/31. No company-disclosed market-share figure for GTCC is carried in the sources used here; the verifiable demand evidence is MHI's own order intake and the company's stated demand frame (over 70GW/yr average, possibly about 100GW in 2026 — company statement, not independently verified here).

In defence, the customer is effectively the Japanese state (plus, newly, export governments under Japan's eased transfer rules): demand is set by the Defence Build-up Programme's budget path, competition is by programme allocation among domestic primes rather than by price, and the barriers are security clearance, programme heritage and systems-integration capability; the risks are budget politics, programme slippage and the cost discipline of first-of-type exports such as the Australian frigates. In nuclear, MHI is Japan's incumbent PWR vendor: the value chain is restarts, maintenance, fuel and fuel-cycle facilities today, with new-build (SRZ-1200) a 2030s revenue question — the interview pack's framing is to separate the existing-fleet revenue base from the new-build story. In the industrial businesses (metals machinery, environmental plant, thermal products), competition is global and price-real — Primetals and European plant makers in metals, Chinese makers rising in environmental and machinery — and margins (Plants & Infrastructure at 10.3% in FY3/26, Industrial Solutions at about 5%) price that in. Across the group, the two sensitivities that matter for the equity are, first, backlog conversion: ¥14,225,600mn of backlog (September 2026) at margins locked in at order intake over the past two improving years, exposed to component capacity and cost escalation over four-to-five-year conversion windows; and second, the yen — guidance assumes USD/JPY 150 — since Energy and defence-adjacent export revenue is dollar-linked while the cost base is substantially domestic. ## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan — the 2024 Business Plan's final year, and what replaces it

The 2024 Medium-Term Business Plan (2024 中期経営計画, FY3/25–FY3/27) is in its final year. Its published FY3/27 targets, as restated by the company in its FY3/26 results briefing (May 2026), are business profit of ¥450,000mn or higher and ROE of 12% — plan targets, not forecasts. **Delivery against them is no longer in doubt: FY3/27 company guidance is business profit of ¥600,000mn and ROE of 14%**, and even the initial May 2026 plan (¥540,000mn) cleared the profit target, because the plan was written before the GTCC order supercycle (FY3/26 Energy orders ¥3,936,700mn, +50% YoY) and the full effect of Japan's defence build-up. The plan's operating agenda — the ITO (Innovative Total Optimization) management methodology cited in the Q&A, growth investment concentrated in GTCC high-temperature parts (about ¥500,000mn), doubling gas-turbine output through lead-time shortening rather than greenfield capacity, and portfolio pruning (the Logisnext exit is the largest single step) — is the bridge between the FY3/20 trough and the current guide.

The successor 2027 Business Plan (from FY3/28) has not been published as of this report; what exists on the record is the CFO's framing at the Q2 FY3/27 briefing (Q&A summary): underlying business-profit earnings power of about ¥690,000mn — the ¥600,000mn guide plus the ~¥70,000mn IGCC provision and the ¥20,000mn risk buffer — 'will be the starting point for the 2027 Business Plan'; GTCC and defence revenue in FY3/28 are expected to exceed FY3/27; shareholder returns will keep ROE as the profitability target and DOE as the basic framework, with buybacks remaining a flexible option; and for Energy's margin, management has previously indicated 20% as an aimable level for the segment over time while cautioning that 25% is not realistic as new-unit (lower-margin) revenue grows faster than after-sales. None of these statements is a published plan target, and this report does not present them as such.

The monitoring set, in the order the data arrives: Q3 FY3/27 cumulative GTCC orders against the ¥3,000,000mn full-year guide (H1: ¥1,568,700mn) and large-frame unit count against H1's 19; Defence & Space cumulative orders against the recovery implied by the ¥1,800,000mn segment orders guide (H1 segment orders ¥296,800mn); any addition to the IGCC provision beyond the ~¥90,000mn full-year burden; buyback execution pace against the ¥300,000mn / 120mn-share authorisation (from 9 November 2026); backlog and contract liabilities as the conversion gauge (¥14,225,600mn and ¥2,573,339mn at September 2026); and publication of the 2027 Business Plan, expected with the FY3/27 results cycle in spring 2027 (timing not confirmed by the company in the sources used). ## 8. Other information

History. MHI's roots are the Nagasaki shipyard leased from the Meiji government in 1884 (Mitsubishi's shipbuilding business); the modern company was formed in 1964 by the merger of three Mitsubishi heavy-industry companies, and it listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange's first section (now TSE Prime). The group's defining programmes across the decades — thermal and nuclear power plant, shipbuilding, aircraft (including the SpaceJet regional-jet programme, terminated in 2023 after the FY3/20–21 charges visible in Chapter 6) and defence equipment — map directly onto today's four segments.
