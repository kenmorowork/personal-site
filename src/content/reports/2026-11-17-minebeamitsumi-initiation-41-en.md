A ¥1.7tn-revenue components conglomerate whose profit pool is concentrating in one small, precise place — miniature ball bearings for AI servers: MinebeaMitsumi Inc. (6479, TSE Prime) is the Nagano-based precision-components group formed by the 2017 integration of Minebea and Mitsumi Electric, extended by the U-Shin (2019) and ABLIC (2020) acquisitions. It reports four segments: Precision Technologies (PT — ball bearings, aircraft rod-end bearings, HDD pivot assemblies), Motor, Lighting & Sensing (MLS — motors, backlights/electro devices, sensors), Semiconductors & Electronics (SE — analogue semiconductors, optical devices, mechanical components, power supplies) and Access Solutions (AS — automotive access products, built from U-Shin and the former Honda Lock). The initiation question is whether the AI-server cycle converts a decade of ¥50–100bn operating-profit oscillation into a structurally higher earnings base. The evidence so far is genuinely strong at the top: H1 FY3/27 (April–September 2026) printed record revenue of ¥890,751mn (+14.4% YoY) and record operating profit of ¥58,065mn (+30.8%), **PT earned ¥40.1bn of operating profit in the half on data-centre bearings and aircraft recovery**, and full-year company guidance was raised on 6 November 2026 to revenue of ¥1,730,000mn and operating profit of ¥128,000mn (company guidance). Strengths: (1) PT's economics — a 22.1% segment margin in FY3/26 (¥62,245mn on ¥281,151mn) and roughly 24% in H1 FY3/27 — rest on a capacity-constrained niche: ball-bearing production capacity is 404mn units/month at maximum utilisation, October 2026 sales volume was 374mn units, and management guides PT operating profit to ¥80,000mn in FY3/27, the level its medium-term plan had targeted for FY3/29 (company plan, briefing 6 November 2026); (2) a second AI leg is forming — AI server-related sales are planned at ¥148bn in FY3/27 rising to ¥230bn by FY3/29 (company plan), spanning bearings, HDD spindle motors, fan motors and power semiconductors; (3) the balance sheet carries the cycle: owners' equity ratio 49.5% at March 2026 and operating cash flow has exceeded ¥85bn in nine of the ten years in Exhibit 1. Weaknesses: (1) the group margin is still thin and the mix is dilutive — consolidated operating margin was 6.2% in FY3/26, SE's margin has fallen from 9.8% (FY3/22) to 4.5% (FY3/26) and AS earned just ¥3.4bn in H1 FY3/27 against a ¥16,500mn full-year guide, so **delivery of the ¥128,000mn guide requires H2 operating profit of ¥69,935mn (calculated) at an 8.3% margin versus 6.5% in H1**; (2) earnings quality below the operating line is noisy — FY3/26 profit before tax of ¥133,779mn was flattered by a ¥29,800mn net finance gain including fair-value gains on financial assets held, and H1 FY3/27 owners' profit includes a c. ¥8.4bn fair-value gain on Toshiba shares, which is why guidance EPS of ¥249.01 barely grows (+1.0%) despite guided operating profit growth of +23.1%; (3) disclosure limits the debate — the tanshin discloses no regional sales, no order intake or backlog, no R&D and no employee figures (all 未取得 / not disclosed in this report), so the AI exposure cannot be sized from company disclosure beyond the plan figures management volunteers in briefings. All historical figures are as reported by the company via the PL/segment sheet built from tanshin in the JPX disclosure archive (FY3/17–FY3/18 J-GAAP, FY3/19 onward IFRS, each year as originally filed); the only forward figures are company guidance and company plan targets, labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## Key points
- **H1 FY3/27: records across the P&L — with a one-off inside the bottom line.** For the six months to 30 September 2026 (IFRS), revenue was ¥890,751mn (+14.4% YoY), operating profit ¥58,065mn (+30.8%; 6.5% margin), profit before tax ¥71,805mn (+77.9%) and net income attributable to owners ¥51,005mn (+78.4%); basic EPS was ¥127.01. The company called revenue, operating profit and net income first-half records. Q2 standalone set quarterly records (revenue ¥464.2bn, operating profit ¥31.8bn). Earnings quality: **Q2 owners' profit of ¥29.7bn includes a c. ¥8.4bn gain from fair-value measurement of financial assets held (Toshiba shares); excluding it, Q2 owners' profit was ¥21.3bn (company briefing)**. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin [IFRS], 6 November 2026; company-published Q2 briefing summary, 6 November 2026.
- **Guidance raised at Q2 — and it is back-loaded into H2.** Full-year FY3/27 company guidance (会社計画) was revised on 6 November 2026 to revenue ¥1,730,000mn (initial ¥1,690,000mn, 12 May 2026), operating profit ¥128,000mn (initial ¥120,000mn), profit before tax ¥138,000mn and owners' net income ¥100,000mn (initial ¥83,000mn; raised to ¥87,000mn at Q1 on 5 August 2026 before the Q2 raise), with basic EPS of ¥249.01. H1 delivered 51.5% of guided revenue but only 45.4% of guided operating profit, so **the guide implies H2 operating profit of ¥69,935mn (calculated) at an 8.3% margin** — predicated on PT momentum, an AS recovery (including the March mould-cost recovery the company cites) and H2 revenue actually falling to c. ¥830bn on game-related and optical-device declines (company briefing). Source: guidance tracker verified against the FY3/26, Q1 FY3/27 and Q2 FY3/27 tanshin; Q2 briefing summary.
- **Strength — PT is a capacity-constrained profit engine, not a commodity line.** **PT was 16.9% of FY3/26 revenue but 59.9% of FY3/26 operating profit before corporate adjustments (segment profit ¥62,245mn, a 22.1% margin)**. In H1 FY3/27 PT revenue rose 22.0% YoY to ¥166.8bn and operating profit 36.1% to ¥40.1bn (briefing summary), on data-centre bearings, aircraft products and HDD pivot assemblies. Ball-bearing capacity is 404mn units/month only at maximum operating days and utilisation; October 2026 production was 361mn units and sales 374mn units (external 321mn). Full-year PT operating-profit guidance of ¥80,000mn (company guidance) equals the medium-term plan's FY3/29 target, pulled forward two years (company briefing). Source: PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 briefing summary, 6 November 2026.
- **Weakness — two of four segments are guided down, and the H2 bridge leans on AS.** FY3/27 segment operating-profit guidance (company guidance, Q2 briefing) is PT ¥80,000mn (+28.5% YoY) and MLS ¥36,000mn (+33.7%), but SE ¥25,000mn (−6.3%) and AS ¥16,500mn (−3.4%) — declines inside a semiconductor upcycle and after AS earned only ¥3.4bn in H1 (a 2.0% margin) on China/Europe auto weakness and European restructuring costs (c. ¥2.0bn booked in Q1 alone). **AS therefore needs c. ¥13.1bn of H2 operating profit (calculated), roughly 3.9x its H1 print**. SE also missed the August plan on H1 profit despite beating on sales. Source: Q2 FY3/27 briefing summary and Q&A summary, 6 November 2026; interview pack, 16 November 2026.
- **The 10-year record: revenue nearly tripled; operating profit only doubled — and twice halved.** Revenue rose from ¥638,926mn in FY3/17 (J-GAAP) to ¥1,664,387mn in FY3/26 (IFRS), a 11.2% CAGR (calculated), as Mitsumi (2017), U-Shin (2019) and ABLIC (2020) were consolidated. Operating profit rose from ¥49,015mn to ¥103,979mn over the same span but fell 27.6% in FY3/24 (to ¥73,536mn) on the post-COVID components correction, and the operating margin has not exceeded 9.0% in any of the ten years. ROE printed 12.1% in FY3/26 after 8.1–8.2% in FY3/24–25. The equity case is therefore a margin case: **management's own yardstick — an 'underlying' operating margin of 8.7% in FY3/27E rising to 10.1% by FY3/29 (company plan) — is the number to audit each quarter**. ## 1. Key indicators and financial history

MinebeaMitsumi reports with a March year-end. The history in this chapter and in Exhibit 1 is as originally reported in each year's own tanshin, compiled in the PL/segment sheet for 6479 (FY3/22–FY3/26 main block plus the supplementary history block; all template checks equal zero) and, for FY3/17–FY3/21, from the same own-year tanshin documents the sheet programme's history-deepening wave used. The basis changes once inside the decade: FY3/17 and FY3/18 are J-GAAP (決算短信〔日本基準〕), and the company adopted IFRS voluntarily from Q1 FY3/19, so FY3/19 onward is IFRS. The two J-GAAP years are never spliced into IFRS growth rates without a label: FY3/19 revenue growth of +0.4% is as printed in the FY3/19 tanshin against its IFRS-restated FY3/18 comparative of ¥881,413mn, not against the J-GAAP ¥879,139mn. Figures are ¥mn unless stated.

Revenue describes an acquisition-built decade. From ¥638,926mn in FY3/17 — a part-year for Mitsumi Electric, consolidated on 27 January 2017 — revenue jumped 37.6% in FY3/18 to ¥879,139mn on Mitsumi's first full year, reached ¥988,424mn by FY3/21 as U-Shin (consolidated from April 2019) and ABLIC (April 2020) were added, then broke through ¥1tn in FY3/22 (¥1,124,140mn) and grew every year since: ¥1,292,203mn (FY3/23), ¥1,402,127mn (FY3/24), ¥1,522,703mn (FY3/25) and ¥1,664,387mn (FY3/26) — a ten-year CAGR of 11.2% (calculated). Operating profit did not follow the same line: ¥49,015mn (FY3/17, 7.7% margin), ¥79,162mn (FY3/18, 9.0% — still the decade's best margin), ¥72,033mn (FY3/19), ¥58,647mn (FY3/20) and ¥51,166mn (FY3/21) as smartphone backlights and game-related demand faded; then ¥92,136mn (FY3/22) and ¥101,522mn (FY3/23) in the post-COVID components boom; a 27.6% fall to ¥73,536mn in FY3/24 (5.2% margin) when that boom corrected; and recovery to ¥94,482mn (FY3/25) and ¥103,979mn (FY3/26), a 6.2% margin in both years. Gross margin tells the mix story underneath: 18.8% in FY3/19, 17.0% in FY3/20–21, a cycle peak of 19.2% in FY3/22, then 17.1% / 16.5% / 17.8% / 17.6% across FY3/23–26 — the acquired, lower-margin revenue (semiconductors, access products) has structurally diluted the bearing-led margin of the old Minebea.

Below operating profit, the finance line is the decade's swing factor and must be read before the bottom line. Net finance results (finance income minus costs, as presented) were −¥712mn in FY3/19, −¥558mn in FY3/20, −¥1,639mn in FY3/21, −¥1,348mn in FY3/22, −¥5,402mn in FY3/23, +¥2,009mn in FY3/24, −¥11,873mn in FY3/25 and +¥29,800mn in FY3/26, when finance income of ¥40,908mn included a fair-value valuation gain on financial assets held (FY3/26 tanshin narrative). (For the J-GAAP years the tanshin's pre-tax line is not used in Exhibit 1; ordinary profit (経常利益) was ¥48,393mn in FY3/17 and ¥78,038mn in FY3/18, and FY3/17 also carried a ¥14,619mn negative-goodwill gain as extraordinary income and a ¥6,196mn convertible-bond redemption loss as an extraordinary loss on the Mitsumi integration — FY3/17 tanshin.) ## H1 FY3/27 results (cumulative, as reported, IFRS)

Revenue was ¥890,751mn (+14.4% YoY), operating profit ¥58,065mn (+30.8%; margin 6.5%, +0.9pt), profit before tax ¥71,805mn (+77.9%), net income attributable to owners ¥51,005mn (+78.4%) and basic EPS ¥127.01 — all first-half records, per the company. The quarterly split shows acceleration, not fade: Q1 standalone (briefing basis) printed revenue of c. ¥426.5bn and operating profit of ¥26.3bn (+50.9% YoY, a Q1 record), and Q2 standalone printed revenue of ¥464.2bn (+12.8% YoY) and operating profit of ¥31.8bn (+17.8%), both Q2 records, with a 6.8% Q2 margin. Two quality warnings belong with the headline growth. First, the gap between operating profit growth (+30.8%) and pre-tax growth (+77.9%) is financial: Q2 owners' profit of ¥29.7bn includes a c. ¥8.4bn gain from fair-value measurement of financial assets held (Toshiba shares, whose value the company notes is driven substantially by Kioxia's share price); excluding it, Q2 owners' profit was ¥21.3bn and EPS ¥53.03 against the reported ¥73.98 (briefing). **Management itself directs attention to the operating line for this reason**. Second, FX flattered the top line: the Q2 year-on-year FX impact was +¥33.6bn on revenue and +¥3.1bn on operating profit (briefing).

By segment (H1, company briefing summary, ¥bn rounded): PT revenue ¥166.8bn (+22.0% YoY) and operating profit ¥40.1bn (+36.1%) — data-centre bearings, aircraft products and HDD pivot assemblies all strong, Q2 PT margin 24.7%; MLS revenue ¥254.0bn (+18.3%) and operating profit ¥17.2bn (+48.1%) on HDD spindle motors and fan motors for solar and data centres; SE revenue ¥294.6bn (+10.2%) and operating profit ¥11.2bn (+16.2%) — ahead of the August plan on sales, behind it on profit (mechanical components and optical devices); AS revenue ¥172.8bn (+9.5%) and operating profit ¥3.4bn (−52.9%) on the China/Europe auto slowdown and European restructuring costs (c. ¥2.0bn in Q1, with a related site-sale gain now expected later in the year — Q&A summary). The four segments' operating profits sum to ¥71.9bn against consolidated operating profit of ¥58.1bn, so the unallocated corporate/adjustment drag in H1 was c. −¥13.8bn (calculated from briefing-rounded figures) — the bridge the interview pack asks management to disclose quarterly. The balance-sheet colour in the briefing: inventories ¥416.7bn at September 2026, down ¥16.2bn from June 2026. ## 3. Business description

MinebeaMitsumi is headquartered in Miyota, Nagano, and organises itself into four reportable segments — Precision Technologies (PT), Motor, Lighting & Sensing (MLS), Semiconductors & Electronics (SE) and Access Solutions (AS) — plus a small non-reportable Other (mainly in-house machinery). The segment definitions below follow the company's own tanshin segment notes. Until FY3/23 the four reportable segments were named Machined Components (機械加工品), Electronic Devices & Components (電子機器), MITSUMI (ミツミ) and U-Shin (ユーシン); the FY3/24 tanshin renamed them to the current names, stating the change is a renaming only with no impact on segment information, and the PL/segment sheet therefore carries the current names for all years, each year on its own documents' figures (register S3). Segment profit is operating profit. In FY3/26 the revenue mix was SE 35.5%, MLS 27.4%, AS 20.0%, PT 16.9% and Other 0.3%, but the profit mix was inverted: PT earned 59.9% of pre-corporate operating profit. That inversion is the business description in one line — a high-margin precision core carrying three larger, thinner businesses assembled by M&A.

## PT (Precision Technologies): the bearing franchise

PT's products are miniature and small ball bearings (the group's founding product), rod-end and spherical bearings for aircraft, HDD pivot assemblies (PMC), mechanical parts and aircraft fasteners. The demand exposure is wherever small motors and mechanisms must spin precisely: data-centre and AI-server hardware (fan motors, HDDs), automobiles, aircraft and, prospectively, humanoid robots. The H1 FY3/27 briefing colour is specific: ball-bearing revenue of ¥54.1bn in Q2 alone, October 2026 production of 361mn units and sales of 374mn units (external 321mn, internal 53mn), against maximum capacity of 404mn units/month that assumes maximum operating days and utilisation at every plant; management is raising effective capacity through changeover-time reduction rather than new lines. Aircraft-related revenue was ¥21.6bn in Q2 (+5.6% QoQ) as the two major airframers' production rates recover, and PMC revenue ¥9.2bn in Q2 (+9.2% QoQ) on nearline-HDD demand for AI servers. Financially PT is the group's annuity: sales of ¥177,470mn / ¥197,300mn / ¥211,406mn / ¥255,702mn / ¥281,151mn across FY3/22–26 with segment profit of ¥45,717mn / ¥42,951mn / ¥38,035mn / ¥55,696mn / ¥62,245mn — margins of 25.8% / 21.8% / 18.0% / 21.8% / 22.1%. **Even in the FY3/24 group downturn PT held an 18.0% margin**. The pricing stance disclosed in the Q2 Q&A is conservative: existing customers are supplied at conventional prices, volume gains are priced with a 'commensurate share' of cost, and no large average-price uplift is factored into results or the medium-term plan — upside from bearing pricing is therefore optionality, not plan. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 briefing summary and Q&A summary, 6 November 2026.

## MLS (Motor, Lighting & Sensing): the volume motor business

MLS combines motors (HDD spindle motors, stepping motors, DC and fan motors, automotive motors), electro devices (LED backlights and battery protection modules) and sensing devices (strain gauges, force/torque sensors, measuring instruments) — in the pre-FY3/24 structure this was the Electronic Devices & Components segment. Demand is broad — automotive, OA equipment, home appliances, solar and data centres — and the current cycle's engine is AI-adjacent: HDD spindle motors for nearline drives, fan motors for data centres and solar, and stepping motors (briefing). Q2 FY3/27 detail (briefing): revenue ¥129.5bn (+4.0% QoQ), of which motors ¥101.3bn, electro devices ¥17.6bn and sensing ¥10.4bn; operating profit ¥9.7bn, a 7.5% margin. Sensing carries the humanoid option: the company has begun sensor deliveries to two US robot manufacturers (c. 20 sensors per robot; current capacity c. 30,000 robots' worth per month on existing capex — Chairman's briefing). The five-year record shows why MLS is the swing segment on costs: sales of ¥371,023mn / ¥366,275mn / ¥369,388mn / ¥407,743mn / ¥456,517mn across FY3/22–26 with segment profit of ¥21,561mn / ¥922mn / ¥11,867mn / ¥22,984mn / ¥26,929mn — margins of 5.8% / 0.3% / 3.2% / 5.6% / 5.9%. The FY3/23 near-zero print (materials and energy costs against fixed prices) is the segment's documented vulnerability; the Q2 Q&A states material-cost rises (copper and others) are currently being passed to customers for general motors, while HDD-motor price rises are not in the plan. The Panasonic Industry motor-business acquisition (closing moved from 2 November 2026 to 1 March 2027; acquired sales c. ¥32.0bn in FY3/25) lands in this segment's perimeter from FY3/28. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet; Q2 FY3/27 briefing summary and Q&A summary.

## SE (Semiconductors & Electronics): Mitsumi's legacy, repriced by the cycle

SE is the former MITSUMI segment: analogue and power semiconductors (including ABLIC, consolidated April 2020, and the Shiga plant), optical devices (camera actuators), mechanical components (game-related mechanisms, switches, connectors), power-supply parts and smart products. It is the group's largest revenue block and its most cyclical: sales of ¥429,116mn / ¥530,464mn / ¥494,717mn / ¥527,646mn / ¥590,263mn across FY3/22–26 with segment profit of ¥41,846mn / ¥42,740mn / ¥35,450mn / ¥22,003mn / ¥26,669mn — margins compressing from 9.8% to 4.5% as game and smartphone-related volumes normalised and semiconductor investment (Shiga) weighed. The medium-term plan inside the segment is analogue/power: sales of c. ¥130bn in FY3/26 targeted to ¥170bn by FY3/29 (company plan, briefing materials), with 800V HVDC-related development for data-centre power cited at the Q2 briefing and single-month profitability at the Shiga plant expected in February–March 2027. H1 FY3/27 was the segment's warning pattern in miniature: revenue beat the August plan (+10.2% YoY to ¥294.6bn) while profit missed it (¥11.2bn), on mechanical components and optical devices; management guides H2 revenue down (game −c. ¥38bn, optical −c. ¥15bn group-wide, per the Chairman's briefing) and full-year SE operating profit down 6.3% to ¥25,000mn (company guidance). Pricing is selective by product — rises where volume allows, volume priority elsewhere (Q&A summary) — and no large price uplift is in the plan. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet; Q2 FY3/27 briefing summary and Q&A summary; interview pack, 16 November 2026.

## AS (Access Solutions) and the technology frame: 'integration' — combining, fusing, polishing

AS sells automotive access products — key sets, door latches, door handles, electronic steering locks — plus industrial-machinery and housing-equipment components. It was assembled from U-Shin (tender offer April 2019; a reportable segment from FY3/20) and Minebea Access Solutions, the former Honda Lock, acquired on 27 January 2023; the FY3/24 revenue step to ¥322,108mn from ¥194,699mn reflects the first full year of that consolidation on top of U-Shin, alongside the segment renaming (FY3/24 tanshin). The record since: sales of ¥145,577mn / ¥194,699mn / ¥322,108mn / ¥328,081mn / ¥332,249mn across FY3/22–26 with segment profit of ¥732mn / ¥22,302mn / ¥10,601mn / ¥15,924mn / ¥17,087mn — margins of 0.5% / 11.5% / 3.3% / 4.9% / 5.1%, the FY3/23 spike including the part-year Honda Lock contribution as reported in that year's own tanshin. AS is currently the problem segment: H1 FY3/27 operating profit fell 52.9% to ¥3.4bn on China and Europe auto weakness and European restructuring costs, partly offset by firm North America/Japan, two-wheel and industrial-machinery demand (briefing) — the diversification defence management itself makes. The full-year guide of ¥16,500mn (company guidance) depends on H2 delivery including the March mould-cost recovery.

The technology frame across all four segments is the company's 'integration' (統合) doctrine — combining its ultra-precision machining, motor, semiconductor, optical and sensing technologies into compound products no single-technology rival replicates: bearings plus motors plus sensors for robots, analogue semiconductors plus power devices for AI-server power, actuators plus semiconductors for cameras. The current expression is the 'five growth fields +1' (AI servers, humanoids, drones, LiDAR among the fields named in company materials) and the AI server-related sales plan of ¥148bn for FY3/27 rising to ¥230bn by FY3/29 (company plan). Production and capacity data beyond ball bearings (sites, utilisation, unit volumes by product) are not disclosed in the tanshin and are not quantified in this report. ## 4. Market and value chain

MinebeaMitsumi sits at the component layer of several value chains at once — precision mechanical parts (bearings, pivots), electromechanical parts (motors, actuators), semiconductors (analogue/power) and automotive access systems — selling to equipment and vehicle makers rather than end users, with pricing negotiated per programme and, as the Q2 Q&A documents, a deliberate policy of not extracting large price rises from existing customers even in shortage. Demand is therefore derived, and the current derivation is unusually concentrated: ==the AI-server build-out pulls bearings (server fan motors and HDDs), nearline HDD spindle motors and pivot assemblies, fan motors, and power semiconductors simultaneously, which is why four otherwise unrelated product lines inflected together in FY3/27==. The company's own market view, stated by the Chairman at the Q2 briefing, is that the AI-server market expands steadily with a further growth-angle increase around CY2030–31, that air cooling remains the majority of server cooling (advanced water cooling c. 10%, hybrid c. 20–30%, the rest air — company estimate in the briefing), and that even a short-term adjustment would not change the medium-term direction. No company-disclosed market-size or market-share figure for its categories is carried in the tanshin or the briefing summaries used here, and none is substituted: the verifiable demand evidence is the company's own reported segment growth, the bearing volume/capacity data point (374mn units sold in October 2026 against 404mn/month maximum capacity) and the AI server-related sales plan (¥148bn FY3/27 → ¥230bn FY3/29, company plan).

Entry barriers are process-based: miniature-bearing grinding and assembly at hundreds of millions of units per month with consistent precision, analogue-semiconductor process know-how (ABLIC), and automotive qualification cycles in access products — all reinforced by the 'integration' model, in which a customer buying a bearing can be sold the motor, sensor and semiconductor around it. The substitute and share risks are the mirror image: Chinese bearing and motor makers on price in standard products, customer dual-sourcing when capacity binds (the Q2 disclosure that bearing growth must come from utilisation, not new lines, cuts both ways), and platform shifts — SSD substitution for nearline HDDs over the long run, and water-cooling penetration reducing fan-motor content per server, a mix the company says it addresses with compatible products. The two sensitivities that follow for the equity: first, the profit pool's concentration — PT is 16.9% of revenue and c. 60% of pre-corporate profit, so a data-centre capex pause lands on the group's best margin, not its average one; second, with no order or backlog disclosure in the tanshin (未取得), the earliest observable of a pause is the quarterly PT print and the monthly bearing volume colour in briefings. ## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan — 'integration', the five growth fields +1, and the 10% underlying-margin target

MinebeaMitsumi's strategy is stated in its own vocabulary: 'integration' (統合 — combining, fusing and polishing its eight core technologies, the 'eight spears') applied to growth fields the company groups as the 'five growth fields +1', with AI servers the current centre of gravity. The numerical plan in the sources used here is the medium-term earnings and cash-allocation framework updated at the Q2 FY3/27 briefing (6 November 2026) — company plan figures, labelled as such, not J-Core Fundamental Research forecasts. **Operating profit plan: ¥128,000mn in FY3/27 (the guidance), ¥145.0bn in FY3/28 and ¥168.0bn in FY3/29 (briefing materials p.30)**. The margin yardstick is management's 'underlying' operating profit margin — operating margin excluding revenue from supplied components and the like, per the briefing — at 8.7% for FY3/27E, 9.3% for FY3/28E and 10.1% for FY3/29E, against the long-standing target of a 10% underlying margin by FY3/29 that the Chairman committed to at the briefing ('no matter what', in the published summary's wording).
