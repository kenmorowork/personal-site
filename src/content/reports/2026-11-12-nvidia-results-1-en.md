## Key points
- **Huang out, effective immediately.** Jensen Huang has resigned as President, CEO and director with immediate effect; the board accepted the resignation on Wed 11 Nov (US) and announced it on Thu 12 Nov. Huang, 63, co-founded NVIDIA in 1993 and led it for 33 years; **there is no designated successor**.
- **Kress interim CEO; Jones first chair.** CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while retaining the CFO role. **Harvey C. Jones has been appointed the company's first-ever board chair**, separating the chair and CEO roles for the first time.
- **The tape is pricing a leadership vacuum.** The stock fell 4.3% on Mon 9 Nov as the board investigation became public, a further 8.6% in Wednesday after-hours trade, and 12.9% in Thursday pre-market to US$151.38 — **roughly US$600bn of market value erased this week**.
- **Earnings in five days are the next catalyst.** Q3 FY27 results remain scheduled for Tue 17 Nov. Company guidance is revenue of US$108bn ±2%; consensus stands near US$111.4bn revenue and US$2.53 EPS. **Delivery against guidance, and any succession update on the call, will decide whether the de-rating stops here**.

## What happened — facts and timeline

NVIDIA announced on Thursday 12 November that Jensen Huang had resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer and director, effective immediately. The board had accepted the resignation the previous day. The departure follows a board investigation, opened on 8 Nov, and governance allegations reported in the US press; **the company's announcement does not state a reason for the resignation**.

CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO and keeps the CFO portfolio. Harvey C. Jones, a long-standing independent director, becomes the company's first board chair. The board will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO.

## Market impact and read-across

The pre-market fall of 12.9% to US$151.38 takes the shares below the bottom of their recent range and roughly 23% below the level of a month ago. **The move is, in our view, a key-man and governance de-rating rather than a revision to near-term fundamentals: no guidance change accompanied the announcement**.

Read-across runs in two directions. AI-accelerator peers and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index will trade on sympathy and on any sign of order or roadmap slippage; NVIDIA's supply chain — foundry, HBM and server assembly, including the Japan equipment and materials names — will be watched for comments at the 17 Nov print.

## Outlook for the next quarter — what we watch on 17 Nov

Three questions decide the next quarter. First, the print itself: revenue against the US$108bn ±2% guide and the first formal outlook under the interim CEO. Second, succession: any timetable, candidate framing, or further board change. Third, governance process: Huang has been asked to give a closed-door congressional briefing on 18 Nov, which keeps headline risk alive into the print.

Until at least the first two are answered, we keep the rating and price target under review. ==A clean print with credible succession language would, in our view, mark the de-rating as event-driven; a guidance cut or a contested succession would not==.
