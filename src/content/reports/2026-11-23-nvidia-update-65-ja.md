## Key points

- **Huang is back, effective immediately.** The board voted **unanimously** to reinstate Jensen Huang as President, Chief Executive Officer and as a member of the board, effective immediately (release dated Mon 23 Nov; announced 13:58 UTC / 22:58 JST). The move reverses the resignation the board accepted on 11 Nov and announced on 12 Nov.
- **The board's stated basis: candor, and Washington.** The board said it was satisfied with respect to Huang's **candor and ability to lead NVIDIA** after a series of discussions and meetings with him. It also received input from senior levels of the United States government and, based on that input, is confident the reinstatement poses **no national security risk**.
- **Kress returns to CFO only; Jones stays chair.** Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO from 12 Nov, continues as Chief Financial Officer. **Harvey C. Jones remains board chair** — the separation of chair and CEO put in place on 12 Nov is kept.
- **What stays open: the investigation runs on.** The independent committee's investigation into reporting that Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort **will proceed to conclusion**; Huang fully supports it and is cooperating. A National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors was formed on 17 Nov, the company is searching for a new Chief Compliance Officer, and it continues to cooperate with ongoing government investigations.
- **First market verdict: pre-market +8.40%.** NVDA closed Friday 20 Nov at **US$125.53, down 14.01% on the day and 48% below the 6 Oct high of US$243.37** (market cap ≈US$3.0tn). Friday after-hours trade recovered to US$130.01 (+3.57%); Monday pre-market stood at **US$136.08 (+8.40%)** — the first priced reaction to the reinstatement.

## What happened

NVIDIA announced on Monday 23 November that Jensen Huang has been reinstated as President and Chief Executive Officer and as a member of the board, effective immediately. The board's vote was unanimous.

The sequence is short and complete: Huang resigned with immediate effect (board acceptance 11 Nov, announcement 12 Nov); CFO Colette Kress served as interim CEO, delivering the Q3 FY27 print on 17 Nov — revenue of US$115.8bn against guidance of US$108.0bn ±2%, with Q4 guided to US$130.0bn ±3%; and on 23 Nov the board restored the founder-CEO, twelve days after his departure. Harvey C. Jones, who became the company's first board chair on 12 Nov, remains chair; Kress returns to the CFO role alone.

## Why the board acted

The release gives three grounds. First, process: the decision followed a series of discussions and meetings with Huang, after which the board was satisfied with respect to his candor and his ability to lead the company. Second, Washington: in reaching its decision the board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and on that basis is confident the reinstatement poses no national security risk. Third, judgement: in the chair's words, the board believes 'Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA', and that the reinstatement is in the best interests of stockholders, employees, partners and customers.

**Our reading (a view, not a fact in the release): the government input is the load-bearing element**. The resignation was entangled with reporting about a foreign intelligence effort targeting Huang; a board could not reverse itself on that file without an external all-clear, and the release is drafted to record that one was obtained.

## What remains open

Today's release closes the succession question. It does not close three other files.

First, the investigation: the committee of independent directors formed after the foreign-intelligence reporting, assisted by independent counsel, **will proceed to conclusion**, with Huang's stated full support and cooperation. Its findings — and any personnel or control consequences — are still ahead. Second, the compliance build-out: the National Security and Export Control Committee (independent directors, formed 17 Nov) is engaging specialist counsel and advisors, and the search for a new Chief Compliance Officer, reporting to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee, has begun. Third, the company states it will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.

Separately — and not addressed by this release at all — the **reported SEC examination into potential accounting issues**, which Reuters reporting brought to the market in the past week and which coincided with the steepest leg of the sell-off, remains an open headline risk. Nothing in the reinstatement speaks to it.

## Market reaction

The stock's starting point matters. NVDA closed Friday 20 Nov at US$125.53, **down 14.01% on the day and 48% below the 6 Oct 2026 high of US$243.37**, with a market capitalisation of roughly US$3.0tn (Finnhub). The November decline was, in the framework this desk has used throughout, a multiple compression on governance and positioning rather than a revision to estimates — Q3 estimates were beaten on 17 Nov while the shares kept falling.

The first priced reaction to the reinstatement is positive but partial: after-hours trade on Friday had already recovered to US$130.01 (+3.57%), and Monday pre-market stood at US$136.08, +8.40% versus Friday's close (Finnhub). Even a hold at that level leaves the shares roughly 44% below the October high. **Our view: the reinstatement removes the governance trigger of the rout, not its other channels** — the SEC accounting-examination reporting and the AI-capex / corporate-credit transmission channel are unchanged by it. Tokyo was closed on Monday 23 Nov (Labour Thanksgiving Day), so the Japanese read-across — the AI data-centre supply chain in our coverage — prices the news at Tuesday's reopen.

Four watchpoints, in the order they arrive.

1) **Tonight's US close (Mon 23 Nov) versus the US$130–136 zone**: whether the regular session holds the pre-market gain or fades it is the market's real verdict on how much of the governance discount has actually been lifted. 2) **SEC follow-ups**: any further reporting or company comment on the reported accounting examination is now the lead overhang. 3) **Dell Technologies' fiscal Q3, 24 Nov (US)**: the next demand-side read on AI server momentum for the wider complex. 4) **The Tokyo reopen, Tue 24 Nov**: how the AIDC chain in our coverage retraces, against Nikkei futures that stood at ¥54,600 on Saturday morning.
