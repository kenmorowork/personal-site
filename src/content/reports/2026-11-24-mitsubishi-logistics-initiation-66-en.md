## Key points

- **Unrealised real-estate gains** Rental real estate carried at ¥136,161mn was appraised at ¥434,520mn at FY3/2026 — an unrealised gain of ¥298,359mn, or about ¥867 per share (J-Core calculations). Gross NAV per share is about ¥1,970 before tax on realisation, 37% above the ¥1,240.5 share price, and the company itself puts its P/B including this gain at about 0.8x.
- **Normalise FY3/26 before using it** FY3/2026 net income of ¥54,773mn includes a ¥67,281mn gain on sales of investment securities and a ¥5,414mn Cavalier Logistics impairment. Operating profit fell 21.6% to ¥15,928mn and business profit was ¥18,575mn — those are the run-rate anchors, not the reported EPS of ¥155.84 or ROE of 14.5%.
- **A ¥120.11bn forwarding acquisition** Mitsubishi Logistics will acquire all shares of Hankyu Hanshin Express for ¥120.11bn, closing expected in Q4 FY3/2027 (announced 30 October 2026). FY3/2027 operating profit guidance was cut to ¥15,500mn on advisory and PMI costs, while revenue guidance was raised to ¥290,000mn — the deal costs arrive before the earnings do.
- **Real estate earns half the segment profit** Real estate was 13.0% of FY3/2026 external revenue but 47.9% of segment operating profit, at a 33.0% margin, against logistics at 5.3%. The FY3/2026 profit fall was mainly condominium sales dropping from ¥15,603mn to ¥4,129mn — rental revenue was flat at ¥32,121mn.
- **The plan needs delivery, not drift** Management Plan [2025-2030] targets business profit of about ¥63,000mn, net income of about ¥41,000mn and ROE of at least 10% by FY3/2031, funded by ¥590,000mn of investment, about ¥100,000mn of policy-share sales and at least ¥40,000mn of buybacks, with DOE of at least 4%. From FY3/2026 operating profit of ¥15,928mn, that is an execution story.

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Mitsubishi Logistics reports under Japanese GAAP with a March year-end and two reportable segments — Logistics and Real estate. The ten-year history in Figure 1 is consolidated, with segment and sub-segment detail in Section 5. Three comparability warnings govern everything that follows. First, a 1-for-5 stock split took effect on 1 November 2024, and all per-share figures in this report are on the post-split basis. Second, the revenue-recognition standard applied from FY3/2022 reduced that year's reported revenue by about ¥6,290mn against the old basis, and earlier years were not restated. Third, from FY3/2026 the company reports a new headline metric, business profit (事業利益 = operating profit + equity-method profit/loss + asset-recycling business profit/loss), and segment profit is now shown on both the operating-profit and business-profit bases.

The decade divides into three phases. Revenue grew from ¥208,718mn in FY3/2017 to ¥229,060mn in FY3/2020 while operating profit stayed in a narrow ¥12,200–12,748mn band — a warehousing and rental business growing slowly on price and volume. The international freight-rate spike then lifted revenue to ¥300,594mn and operating profit to ¥23,030mn in FY3/2023, before freight normalised and revenue fell back to ¥254,507mn in FY3/2024. FY3/2025 recovered to ¥284,069mn of revenue and ¥20,310mn of operating profit, helped by condominium sales inside the real-estate segment. FY3/2026 revenue fell 3.7% to ¥273,446mn as condominium sales dropped away, and operating profit fell 21.6% to ¥15,928mn.

Reported net income does not belong in the same series. Net income was ¥39,160mn in FY3/2021 on asset-transfer gains, ¥31,864mn in FY3/2025 including a ¥29,999mn gain on sales of investment securities, and ¥54,773mn in FY3/2026 including a ¥67,281mn securities-sale gain, partly offset by a ¥5,414mn impairment on Cavalier Logistics goodwill in the United States. ROE of 14.5% in FY3/2026 is therefore not a run-rate figure, and neither is the reported EPS of ¥155.84. Section 1 continues below with the normalisation we apply throughout this report. ## Reported profit is not run-rate profit

Our normalisation is simple and uses only disclosed items. Operating profit is the cleanest starting point: ¥15,928mn in FY3/2026, down from ¥20,310mn, with the decline split between a ¥1,169mn fall in logistics segment profit (to ¥12,693mn), a ¥2,004mn fall in real-estate segment profit (to ¥11,693mn, as condominium sales revenue fell from ¥15,603mn to ¥4,129mn on the incl.-internal basis) and a ¥1,209mn widening of corporate and elimination costs (to −¥8,458mn). Business profit of ¥18,575mn adds equity-method profit of ¥2,637mn — itself a swing from a ¥5,597mn equity-method loss in FY3/2025 that included an ¥8,818mn write-off related to ITL Corporation — and ¥8mn of asset-recycling profit.

Below operating profit, the FY3/2026 accounts contain ¥67,281mn of gains on sales of investment securities in special profit and ¥5,414mn of impairment in special losses. Stripping both items out does not produce a precise normalised net income, because the tax effect of the securities sales is not separately disclosed; we therefore anchor normalised earnings power on operating profit and business profit, and we flag every forecast year in which the company's own numbers still assume securities gains. On that basis the earnings question for this stock is not the FY3/2026 headline but whether operating profit can rebuild from ¥15,928mn toward the mid-term plan's business-profit target of about ¥63,000mn by FY3/2031 — a target that explicitly requires non-linear growth, including M&A.

## 2. Latest update — H1 FY3/27 results, revised guidance and the Hankyu Hanshin Express acquisition

H1 FY3/2027 (April–September 2026) revenue was ¥144,854mn, operating profit ¥8,240mn, business profit ¥9,752mn and net income ¥9,539mn, down 71.0% year on year only because H1 FY3/2026 contained large securities-sale gains. Against the revised full-year forecast, H1 progress was 49.9% on revenue, 53.2% on operating profit and 44.2% on net income (J-Core calculation from reported figures).

On 30 October 2026 the company revised FY3/2027 guidance and announced a transformational acquisition on the same day. Revenue guidance was raised from ¥280,000mn to ¥290,000mn (+6.1% year on year) on stronger logistics volumes, but operating profit guidance was cut from ¥17,500mn to ¥15,500mn (−2.7% year on year), mainly for M&A advisory and PMI costs related to the acquisition of Hankyu Hanshin Express; segment guidance summaries put the logistics segment's M&A-related costs at about ¥2,000mn. Ordinary profit guidance is ¥19,600mn and net income guidance ¥21,600mn, with DPS unchanged at ¥44. The revised net income forecast still assumes about ¥14,000mn of gains on planned sales of about ¥20,000mn of policy shareholdings (secondary summary of the company's Q2 materials) — forecast profit remains dependent on securities gains, a point we return to in Risks.

The acquisition itself: Mitsubishi Logistics will acquire all shares of Hankyu Hanshin Express for ¥120.11bn, with closing expected in Q4 FY2026 (the fiscal year ending March 2027), subject to regulatory approvals. The consideration equals about 27% of Mitsubishi Logistics' market capitalisation at the 24 November 2026 close (J-Core calculation). It is the largest single step so far toward the mid-term plan's targets of doubling overseas revenue versus FY3/2025 and lifting the logistics share of net income to 65% by FY3/2031.

## 3. Business description

Mitsubishi Logistics, founded in 1887 and headquartered in Nihonbashi, Tokyo, is Japan's largest general warehouse operator by revenue among the TSE warehousing majors, with 50 consolidated subsidiaries and 13 equity-method affiliates and 4,991 consolidated employees at FY3/2026. The group reports two segments. Logistics (FY3/2026 external revenue ¥238,013mn) covers warehousing (storage and in/out handling), land transport, international transport and forwarding — including Cavalier Logistics in the United States — port transport, and related IT and other services. Real estate (external revenue ¥35,432mn) covers office and commercial building leasing and management, condominium sales, and the newer asset-recycling business, which acquires and develops property, operates it, and sells it, including into a real-estate fund the company plans to establish.

The asset base explains the business model. Named rental assets in the filings include the Nihonbashi Dia Building (the head office), Yokohama Dia Building, Shibaura Dia Building and facilities in the Kobe Harborland area; land on the balance sheet was ¥131,180mn at FY3/2026. Logistics facilities and rental buildings sit in the same prime urban locations, so the two segments share one scarce input — well-located urban land held for decades at historical cost. That is the source of both the real-estate profit stream and the unrealised gain quantified in Section 6.

## Two segments, two very different economics

In FY3/2026 logistics produced 87.0% of external revenue but only 52.1% of segment operating profit before corporate costs, at a 5.3% margin. Real estate produced 13.0% of revenue and 47.9% of segment profit, at a 33.0% margin on the incl.-internal revenue base (J-Core calculations from the reported segment note). On the business-profit basis the split is similar: logistics ¥15,116mn and real estate ¥11,917mn in FY3/2026, against consolidated business profit of ¥18,575mn after ¥8,458mn of corporate and elimination costs on the operating-profit reconciliation.

Inside logistics, warehousing is the largest disclosed sub-segment and the steadiest: ¥75,099mn in FY3/2026, up 3.7%. Land transport was ¥55,492mn (+0.7%), international transport and forwarding ¥77,273mn (−6.4% as freight rates normalised), port transport ¥26,184mn (+13.9%) and other ¥4,578mn. Inside real estate, rental revenue was flat at ¥32,121mn, while other revenue — mainly condominium sales — fell 73.5% to ¥4,129mn, which is the single largest reason consolidated revenue and real-estate profit fell in FY3/2026. The earnings mix therefore has a stable rental core, a mid-single-digit-margin logistics engine, and two volatile overlays: international freight rates and condominium sales timing.

## 4. Market and value chain

Japan logistics is a low-single-digit volume industry becoming a mid-single-digit yen-revenue industry through price, mix and outsourcing. Yano Research Institute (press release, 5 August 2026) puts the 15-sector logistics market at ¥24.794tn in FY2024 (+5.7%), and forecasts ¥25.360tn in FY2025 (+2.3%) and ¥26.361tn in FY2026 (+3.9%) — about +2–4% per year, with Yano itself noting that a full recovery in freight volume has yet to be achieved and that growth is driven primarily by transportation unit prices and exchange rates. Third-party estimates for 3PL, the structural winner inside the total, cluster around +4–5.5% per year (IMARC Group: Japan 3PL CAGR 4.3% for 2025–33; Expert Market Research: 5.4% for 2025–34 — different definitions, not averaged). MLIT warehouse statistics show the physical side: ordinary-warehouse storage balances rising about +2% year on year with throughput broadly flat. The 2024 driver-overtime cap, itemised waiting and handling charges, and a projected warehouse-worker shortfall of about 55,100 by FY2028 (cabinet-decided sectoral policy, January 2026) are converting labour scarcity into pricing power and automation capex.

The value chain runs from shippers through warehousing and distribution processing, port handling, land transport and international forwarding to the consignee, with real-estate leasing and asset recycling monetising the land the chain sits on. Mitsubishi Logistics is one of the few operators present at every stage, and the Hankyu Hanshin Express acquisition deepens the forwarding stage, historically its thinnest relative to warehousing.

Against peers, Mitsubishi Logistics is the revenue leader of the traditional warehousing majors but not the sector's largest company by market value: Kamigumi (9364), a port operator, is larger. Mitsui-Soko Holdings (9302) earned a higher FY3/2026 operating margin (7.4%) on a 3PL- and air-freight-led model and trades at 1.73x book, while Sumitomo Warehouse (9303) trades at 0.93x book with a large securities portfolio. Mitsubishi Logistics sits between them at 1.12x book — despite carrying the largest rental-real-estate unrealised gain of the three relative to equity (Section 6). ## 5. Segments and regions

Figure 3 shows the two-segment history on the operating-profit basis. Segment disclosure before FY3/2022 was not obtained on a consistent basis in our data set and is not shown, rather than reconstructed. Logistics operating profit peaked at about ¥18,800mn in FY3/2023 on the freight spike (secondary cross-check, rounded) and has since normalised to ¥12,693mn; real-estate operating profit was ¥10,100–13,697mn across FY3/2022–FY3/2025 before the condominium-driven fall to ¥11,693mn in FY3/2026. Corporate and elimination costs are material and rising — ¥7,249mn in FY3/2025 and ¥8,458mn in FY3/2026 on the operating-profit reconciliation — and any forecast that ignores them overstates segment-sum profit by roughly half of consolidated operating profit.

## 6. Balance sheet and the real-estate asset value

The balance sheet is conservative: total assets ¥639,786mn, equity ratio 59.3%, cash and deposits ¥63,032mn and interest-bearing debt ¥117,355mn at FY3/2026 (company definition including lease obligations). Operating cash flow fell to ¥6,531mn in FY3/2026 despite ¥80,071mn of pre-tax profit, on ¥22,021mn of tax paid and a ¥9,458mn increase in real estate held for sale in the asset-recycling business — a reminder that the securities gains funding the plan are taxed in cash.

The asset-value case rests on one disclosed note. Rental real estate was carried at ¥136,161mn at March 2026 against a fair value of ¥434,520mn, based mainly on external appraisals. The unrealised gain of ¥298,359mn (J-Core calculation) equals 78.6% of equity of ¥379,539mn, or about ¥867 per share on the 344,040,271 shares ex-treasury (J-Core calculations). Adding the unrealised gain to BPS of ¥1,103.18 gives a gross net asset value of about ¥1,970 per share before tax on realisation, against a share price of ¥1,240.5 — a 37% discount. The company itself stated on 29 June 2026 that its P/B including rental-property unrealised gains is still below 1.0x, at about 0.8x. The mid-term plan's asset-recycling business and real-estate fund (AUM targets ¥30,000mn by FY3/2028 and ¥80,000mn by FY3/2031) are the mechanism management has chosen to surface this value gradually rather than hold it inert. ## 7. J-Core forecasts

Figure 9 sets out our estimates. For FY3/2027 we adopt the company's revised guidance of 30 October 2026 in full — revenue ¥290,000mn, operating profit ¥15,500mn, net income ¥21,600mn, EPS ¥64.0 and DPS ¥44 — because H1 progress rates (49.9% of revenue, 53.2% of operating profit) support it and because the guidance already carries the Hankyu Hanshin Express advisory and PMI costs. Two cautions attach to that adoption: the net income line still assumes about ¥14,000mn of policy-share sale gains (secondary summary of company materials), and the acquisition is expected to close only in Q4, so FY3/2027 includes little of its earnings.

For FY3/2028 our estimates are revenue ¥312,000mn (+7.6%), operating profit ¥20,000mn, net income ¥16,800mn, EPS ¥50 and DPS ¥46 — all J-Core estimates. The bridge from FY3/2027 guidance is explicit. Revenue: a full year of Hankyu Hanshin Express consolidation and underlying logistics growth of about 4% on price revisions and volumes, partly offset by condominium sales normalising after FY3/2026's low base. Operating profit: the ¥15,500mn guidance base, plus about ¥2,000mn as the one-off M&A and PMI costs in FY3/2027 guidance do not repeat, plus about ¥2,500mn from the full-year acquisition contribution and logistics price and volume growth. Net income falls despite higher operating profit because our FY3/2028 estimate assumes no gains on sales of investment securities, against about ¥14,000mn assumed in the company's FY3/2027 forecast; on a like-for-like operating basis, underlying net income rises. EPS uses the share count implied by the company's guidance EPS. These estimates sit far below the mid-term plan's FY3/2031 business-profit target of about ¥63,000mn: the plan requires further M&A, asset-recycling profits and overseas growth that we do not underwrite until they are announced.

## 8.

We value Mitsubishi Logistics on its asset base, cross-checked on earnings, because reported earnings are distorted by securities gains and the balance sheet carries a disclosed, appraised asset value well above book. Gross NAV per share — BPS plus the rental-real-estate unrealised gain per share, before tax on realisation — is about ¥1,970 at FY3/2026 and about ¥1,990 on our FY3/2028 BPS estimate of ¥1,127 with the unrealised gain held flat (J-Core calculations).

Hankyu Hanshin Express closes on schedule and integrates without material synergy slippage; PMI costs end with FY3/2027; operating profit reaches our ¥20,000mn FY3/2028 estimate; asset recycling begins to surface real-estate value.

## 9. Risks and management strategy

Securities-sale dependence. Reported profit has been carried by gains on sales of investment securities — ¥29,999mn in FY3/2025 and ¥67,281mn in FY3/2026 — and the revised FY3/2027 forecast still assumes about ¥14,000mn of such gains. The mid-term plan funds about ¥100,000mn of its ¥590,000mn investment programme from policy-share sales and targets policy shareholdings below 20% of net assets. The unwind is therefore both a funding source and an earnings cliff: when the portfolio is sold down, the gains stop, and reported net income converges to the operating level — the transition our FY3/2028 estimate already makes. Execution risk runs the other way as well: sale timing depends on market conditions and counterparty relationships, and a delayed unwind delays the investment programme it funds.

Acquisition and integration risk. The ¥120.11bn Hankyu Hanshin Express acquisition is large relative to the acquirer (about 27% of market capitalisation, J-Core calculation) and is the second major overseas-forwarding commitment after Cavalier Logistics, whose goodwill was impaired by ¥5,414mn in FY3/2026. The FY3/2027 guidance cut shows PMI costs arriving before any synergy.

Operating risks. Logistics margins are thin (5.3% in FY3/2026) against wage inflation, a structural warehouse-labour shortage and the pass-through risk the sector has historically been slow on; international forwarding revenue fell 6.4% in FY3/2026 on freight normalisation and remains rate-sensitive. Real-estate profit depends on rental conditions in a small number of prime assets and on condominium timing. Corporate costs of ¥8,458mn are large relative to operating profit and rising.

Management strategy, as stated in Management Plan [2025-2030], answers these risks directly: business profit of about ¥63,000mn and ROE of at least 10% by FY3/2031, net income mix shifting to 65% logistics / 30% real estate / 5% new business, overseas revenue at least doubling, ¥590,000mn of investment (¥475,000mn growth), DOE of at least 4% with continuous dividend increases, buybacks of at least ¥40,000mn, net assets controlled at about ¥400,000mn and an equity ratio of about 45% by FY3/2031. The strategy is coherent; the FY3/2026 starting point — operating profit ¥15,928mn and normalised ROE well below the reported 14.5% — shows how much of it depends on delivery rather than on the current earnings base.
