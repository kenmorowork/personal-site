TDK is a global electronic-components group — passive components (MLCCs, inductors), sensors, magnetic application products (HDD heads and suspensions) and, largest of all, rechargeable batteries through Amperex (ATL) — with 92.7% of sales outside Japan and 55.0% in China (customer location). In FY3/2026 net sales were ¥2,504,820mn, operating profit ¥272,415mn (10.9%), profit before tax ¥276,810mn and owners' net income ¥195,663mn (diluted EPS ¥103.0 calculated; Finnhub's annual EPS ¥103.09 agrees). FY3/2027 guidance was raised on 30 October 2026 to sales ¥3,050,000mn / OP ¥365,000mn / owners' NI ¥280,000mn (basic EPS ¥147.50). Strengths: (1) the non-battery AI businesses are inflecting hard — **H1 FY3/2027 OP rose 41.0% to ¥208.2bn (13.3% margin) with Passive OP up 2.7×, Magnetic OP up ~2.8× on HDD head volumes +38% and suspensions +34%**, and AI-ecosystem sales up ~1.7× YoY; (2) the raised guide already exceeds every final-year target of the mid-term plan ending this year (revenue ¥2,500bn, OP margin ≥11%, ROE ≥10%, ROIC ≥8% — guide: ¥3,050bn, 12.0%, ROIC forecast raised to 9.7%); (3) Energy (batteries) remains a high-return anchor — FY3/2026 OP ¥246,682mn at 18.0%, about three-quarters of segment profit before corporate costs (calculated); (4) the balance sheet is net cash (¥299,144mn calculated at FY3/2026-end) with operating cash flow of ¥507,672mn in FY3/2026; (5) pricing discipline is documented — H1 selling-price erosion was held to −¥2.1bn by supply-demand pricing (company bridge), against the component industry's usual deflation. Weaknesses: (1) **the swing factor is a single programme cycle — nearline HDD demand (assumption raised to 79m units)** drives Magnetic, the ¥30bn capex raise, and much of the OP bridge, while suspensions already guide down ~6% QoQ in Q3 on a model change; (2) the largest segment is volume-exposed to smartphones — Energy OP grew only 9.1% in H1 on +33.9% sales (FX-flattered) because small-battery volumes fell, and the company cut its FY smartphone production assumption to <1.1bn units (~15% below April's implied level) on memory prices; (3) this year's AI Passive growth (>3× expected to data centres) is mix and allocation within existing capacity — MLCC capacity rises only ~20% next year (company), capping compounding; (4) cash conversion broke in H1 — FCF was −¥23.0bn (withholding-tax payment and working capital) and the FY FCF forecast was cut to −¥50bn after M&A (Linergy Power) and the capex raise to ¥400bn; inventories jumped to ¥585,448mn at FY3/2026-end (4.1 months of cost of sales, calculated); (5) disclosure is speech-text only — TDK publishes no Q&A for interim briefings, orders/backlog are not disclosed, and R&D and employees do not appear in the tanshin (register S8); (6) the stock has de-rated ~49% from its June 2026 high (¥4,315) to ¥2,201.5 despite the raise — the market is discounting HDD-cycle duration. Rating and price target are Under Review placeholders (see Basis of preparation).

## Key points

- **The guide has outrun the plan** FY3/2027 guidance: initial (28 April 2026) sales ¥2,580,000mn / OP ¥295,000mn / NI ¥225,000mn → Rev.1 (30 October 2026) sales ¥3,050,000mn / OP ¥365,000mn / PBT ¥370,000mn / NI ¥280,000mn / basic EPS ¥147.50, dividend raised to ¥25 + ¥25. Company-stated reason: AI data-centre component demand exceeded April assumptions and H1 beat the initial outlook. **The mid-term plan's final-year targets (revenue ¥2,500bn, OP margin ≥11%) are exceeded by ¥550bn and ~1pt respectively (calculated)** — the May 2027 next-plan briefing is now the valuation event.
- **Magnetic is the swing factor** H1 FY3/2027: Magnetic Application sales ¥176.9bn, OP ¥33.1bn (18.7% calculated) — ~2.8× YoY — on HDD head volumes +38% and suspensions +34%. Capex was raised ¥30bn to ¥400bn, committed to head/suspension capacity, against a nearline demand assumption of 79m units. But Q3 already guides suspensions ~−6% QoQ on a model change: this is programme business, and the FY3/2026 full-year Magnetic OP was only ¥26,951mn — **the H1 run-rate annualises to 2.5× that**.
- **Batteries: margin without volume** Energy Application is 55% of sales (¥1,370,304mn FY3/2026) at an 18.0% OP margin — but **H1 OP grew just 9.1% on +33.9% sales (FX contributed ¥134.4bn of group revenue growth), because small-battery volumes fell** with smartphone production, now assumed at <1.1bn units for the FY (~15% below April's implied level, company). H1 Energy OP also includes a ¥2.6bn one-time gain on the EV power-supply R&D business transfer. Mix and pass-through pricing must keep offsetting volume in H2, when Q3 small-battery volumes guide ~−10% QoQ.
- **AI Passive growth is allocation, not capacity** Passive Components OP rose 2.7× in H1 to ¥39.6bn (11.1% calculated), and AI data-centre Passive sales are now expected up >3× this year (vs ~2× planned in April) toward a ~10×-by-FY2031 ambition. The constraint: **MLCC capacity rises only ~20% next fiscal year** — this year's growth is mix and allocation within existing capacity (company). Aluminium-electrolytic capacity is planned to double yearly to ~7× FY2026 by FY2029, and ~¥40bn goes to thin-film inductors for vertical power delivery.
- **Net cash, but a −¥50bn FCF year guided** FY3/2026 operating cash flow was ¥507,672mn and the group ended the year with net cash of ¥299,144mn (calculated). H1 FY3/2027 FCF was nevertheless −¥23.0bn (a Q1 withholding-tax payment and working-capital build), and **the FY FCF forecast was cut to −¥50bn after the capex raise and the Linergy Power acquisition**. Inventories rose 43% in FY3/2026 to ¥585,448mn (4.1 months of cost of sales, calculated) — the build's composition (HDD vs battery vs passives) is not disclosed.

## 3. Business description

TDK Corporation (founded 1935 as Tokyo Denki Kagaku Kogyo; headquartered in Nihonbashi, Tokyo) is one of the world's largest electronic-components makers, built on ferrite and magnetic materials. President & CEO is Noboru Saito (since April 2022); CFO is Tetsuji Yamanishi. It reports four segments plus Other (segment profit as defined by the company — sales less cost of sales, SG&A and other operating items, excluding head-office costs; register S3). A 5-for-1 stock split took effect on 1 October 2024.

## Energy Application Products

Rechargeable batteries (Amperex Technology / ATL — small lithium-ion cells for smartphones and ICT devices, medium-sized cells, EV-related through joint ventures) and power supplies. FY3/2026 sales ¥1,370,304mn (55% of group), OP ¥246,682mn (18.0%) — the group's profit anchor. The mix story (new products, material-cost pass-through pricing) has so far offset smartphone volume decline; H1 FY3/2027 OP grew only 9.1% despite +33.9% sales.

Energy's H1 arithmetic is the group's central margin question. Segment sales rose 33.9% to ¥867.7bn but operating profit rose only 9.1% to ¥150.1bn — an incremental margin of about 4% on the added sales (calculated) — and the ¥150.1bn includes a ¥2.6bn one-time gain on the transfer of the EV power-supply R&D business. The volume side is explicit: the company's full-year smartphone production assumption was cut to below 1.1bn units, about 15% below the level implied by the April plan, and Q3 guidance has small-battery volumes falling a further about 10% quarter on quarter (segment sales −2% to −5% ex-FX). What has carried the segment is mix and material-cost pass-through pricing on new products; on the full-year history that mix story lifted Energy's margin from 12.8% in FY3/2022 (¥123,212mn) to 18.0% in FY3/2026 (¥246,682mn), so the H1 print is the first half in the window where volume, not mix, set the segment's direction.

## Passive Components

MLCCs, aluminium electrolytic and film capacitors, inductors, transformers, EMC components. FY3/2026 sales ¥593,201mn, OP ¥41,831mn (7.1%) — a depressed margin against ¥77,251mn (15.3%) in FY3/2022, reflecting the post-shortage correction; H1 FY3/2027 OP recovered to ¥39.6bn (11.1%) on AI data-centre demand (ceramic capacitors and inductors for power delivery), with AI-DC sales expected up >3× this year.

The AI data-centre leg is quantified in the company's own capacity commitments. Passive sales to AI data centres are now expected to grow more than 3× this fiscal year, against about 2× planned in April, within an ambition of about 10× by FY2031 — but MLCC capacity rises only about 20% next fiscal year, so this year's growth is mix and allocation within existing capacity rather than new plant. The dated commitments are: aluminium-electrolytic capacity doubling yearly to about 7× the FY2026 level by FY2029; about ¥40bn for thin-film inductors for vertical power delivery; and, in Sensors, about ¥100bn to double TMR sensor capacity by FY2031. FIELDNOTES' electronic-components report (16 November 2026) records the same pattern across the peer set — Taiyo Yuden running MLCC utilisation at about 95% in Q2 with a book-to-bill of 1.83 and its AI-server MLCC capacity expansion pulled forward two years, and Murata committing about ¥1tn to MLCCs over five years — which frames TDK's allocation-led growth as an industry-wide capacity race rather than a company-specific claim.

## Magnetic and Sensor Application Products

Magnetic Application: HDD heads and suspensions (the nearline AI-storage play), magnets and power products — FY3/2026 ¥262,903mn / ¥26,951mn (10.3%), after a ¥56,392mn segment loss in FY3/2023 (HDD downturn and restructuring); H1 FY3/2027 OP ¥33.1bn already exceeds the FY3/2026 full year. Sensors (TMR/Hall sensors, MEMS microphones and motion sensors): FY3/2026 ¥224,623mn / ¥20,748mn (9.2%) — the segment lost money throughout FY3/2018–21 on the history block (−¥19,381mn to −¥25,024mn a year) before the turnaround; ~¥100bn is planned to double TMR capacity by FY2031.

Magnetic's swing is programme-concentrated, and the company disclosed the concentration itself. HDD head volumes rose 38% and suspension volumes 34% year on year in H1, yet the Q3 guide already has suspensions falling about 6% quarter on quarter on a model change (heads about +5%) — programme-level volatility inside a strong nearline cycle whose full-year demand assumption was raised to 79mn units. The ¥30bn capex raise, to ¥400bn for the year, is committed to head and suspension capacity against that demand, and the full-year free-cash-flow forecast was cut to −¥50bn after M&A and strategic investments. Sensors, meanwhile, printed a 14.3% H1 margin (¥18.3bn, about 1.5× year on year) after losing money in every history-block year from FY3/2018 to FY3/2021 (−¥19,381mn to −¥25,024mn a year); the Q3 guide of −4% to −7% ex-FX is the first test of whether that margin is scale economics or ICT seasonality.

## 4. Market and value chain

TDK sits mid-chain in nearly every electronics value chain: materials and components into smartphones (Apple-chain exposure via ATL and components), AI servers and storage (MLCCs, inductors, HDD heads/suspensions), autos and industrial equipment. Competitive sets: MLCCs against Murata, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Taiyo Yuden; HDD heads against Seagate/Western Digital captive operations (TDK is the merchant head supplier); batteries against ATL's Chinese peers and Samsung SDI/LG in small cells; sensors against Alps Alpine, Honeywell and Bosch Sensortec. The portfolio question is explicit in the company's own framework: 15 component business units are in an 'improvement' category under portfolio management, with direction for the remaining monitored units to be decided by end-FY3/2027 (pack). ==The long-term target state — ROE ≥15%, ROIC ≥12%, OP margin ≥15% — requires the non-battery mix to keep rising==.
