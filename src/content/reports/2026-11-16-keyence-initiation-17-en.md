The highest-margin large-cap in Japanese industrials, disclosed on its own terms: Keyence Corporation (6861, TSE Prime) develops and sells factory-automation sensors, machine-vision systems, measuring instruments, laser markers, microscopes and barcode readers through a direct-sales, fabless model: products are planned in-house, production is outsourced, and its own sales engineers sell directly to factories, typically shipping the same day. The financial record compiled in the PL/segment sheet (J-GAAP tanshin, JPX archive) is without peer in this coverage: revenue of ¥1,169,289mn in FY3/26 (+10.4% YoY), **an operating margin of 51.0% — the fifth consecutive year above 50%** — a gross margin of 83.0%, ROE of 13.5%, an equity ratio of 94.6% and zero interest-bearing debt. H1 FY3/27 (to 20 September 2026) accelerated sharply: **revenue ¥735,399mn (+34.9% YoY) and operating profit ¥408,262mn (+50.0%), a 55.5% operating margin**. Strengths: (1) a structural, not cyclical, margin architecture — value-added product planning, direct distribution that captures the distributor margin and feeds customer problems back into development, and an asset-light fabless cost base; (2) compounding through cycles — revenue has risen every year in the five-year record, from ¥755,174mn in FY3/22, a 11.5% CAGR, with overseas sales reaching 66.6% of the total in FY3/26; (3) a balance sheet that removes financial risk from the equity story (net cash, no borrowings, operating cash flow of ¥430,680mn in FY3/26). Weaknesses: (1) disclosure minimalism that is itself an analytical and governance issue — Keyence is a single segment by its own statement, omits the segment note in every tanshin, discloses no product split, no regional profit, no order intake or backlog, no R&D figure and no headcount in its earnings releases; (2) no earnings guidance and no mid-term plan, as a matter of stated policy, so there is no company anchor against which to measure delivery — monitoring reduces to quarterly prints, the overseas ratio and the margin structure; (3) valuation leaves no room for the cycle — at ¥74,580 (13 November 2026) the shares trade at 40.6x FY3/26 EPS and 5.2x book value, and the current demand wave is tied to the same AI/data-centre and semiconductor capex that is lifting the whole IME complex. This report contains no J-Core Fundamental Research forecasts and no company guidance exists to quote; third-party consensus, where mentioned, is labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Keyence reports under Japanese GAAP with a 20 March year-end (the PL/segment sheet calendar shows the template's 31 March convention; the company's fiscal periods run to 20 March). The ten-year record below is as reported in the company's tanshin filed in the JPX disclosure archive, compiled in the PL/segment sheet for 6861 (FY3/17–FY3/26 actuals plus 20 quarters; all template checks equal zero). Figures are ¥mn unless stated.

The preceding five years contain the one structural break in the ten-year record: FY3/17 is a nine-month transitional year (21 June 2016 – 20 March 2017, following the change of year-end from 20 June to 20 March), and its reported revenue of ¥316,347mn and operating profit of ¥169,750mn (53.7% margin) are for nine months and are not annualised. On the full twelve-month basis that follows, revenue rose from ¥526,847mn in FY3/18 to ¥587,095mn in FY3/19 (+11.4%), then fell two consecutive years — ¥551,843mn in FY3/20 (−6.0%) and ¥538,134mn in FY3/21 (−2.5%) — before the recovery that produced the FY3/22 figure above. Operating profit peaked in FY3/19 at ¥317,868mn and troughed in FY3/21 at ¥276,758mn; **the operating margin stayed within a 50.3–55.6% band across the four full years** (FY3/19: 54.1%; FY3/20: 50.3%; FY3/21: 51.4%), and gross margin within 81.8–82.3%. The overseas sales ratio rose from 49.1% in the nine-month FY3/17 to 53.3% in FY3/18 and 56.0% by FY3/21. Per-share figures cross a 2-for-1 share split: weighted shares are as reported at about 121.3mn in FY3/17–19 and about 242.5mn from FY3/20, and EPS on the sheet's share basis was ¥1,736.7 in FY3/18, ¥1,864.9 in FY3/19, ¥816.9 in FY3/20 and ¥813.5 in FY3/21 — the step down between FY3/19 and FY3/20 is the split, not earnings.

Revenue rose every year: ¥755,174mn in FY3/22, ¥922,422mn in FY3/23 (+22.1% YoY), ¥967,288mn in FY3/24 (+4.9%), ¥1,059,145mn in FY3/25 (+9.5%) and ¥1,169,289mn in FY3/26 (+10.4%) — a five-year CAGR of 11.5% with no down year through the 2023–24 factory-automation inventory correction, which hit less direct, distribution-led peers far harder. Operating profit was ¥418,045mn, ¥498,914mn, ¥495,014mn, ¥549,775mn and ¥595,759mn, at margins of 55.4%, 54.1%, 51.2%, 51.9% and 51.0%. The margin structure underneath is the story: gross margin held in an 81.8–83.8% band (83.0% in FY3/26) while the SG&A ratio rose from 26.9% of sales in FY3/22 to 32.1% in FY3/26 — the deliberate cost of expanding the direct sales force in Japan and overseas. In other words, the ~4pt of operating-margin compression over five years is a chosen investment in the distribution asset, not price or cost erosion at the gross line.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was ¥303,360mn in FY3/22, ¥362,963mn in FY3/23, ¥369,642mn in FY3/24, ¥398,656mn in FY3/25 and ¥445,185mn in FY3/26; EPS was ¥1,250.83, ¥1,496.59, ¥1,524.13, ¥1,643.77 and ¥1,835.63 on a stable weighted share count of about 242.5mn (Keyence has no dilutive securities). ROE as reported in the tanshin headers was 13.5% in both FY3/25 and FY3/26 — high in absolute terms, and mechanically suppressed by the equity base discussed below. The effective tax rate was 28.8–30.0% across the period; there were no extraordinary items in any of the five years, and non-operating items are small except for FY3/26 non-operating income of ¥40,491mn (which lifted ordinary profit to ¥635,756mn, +13.3% YoY, faster than operating profit growth of +8.4%).

The balance sheet is the most conservative in this coverage. At 20 March 2026 total assets were ¥3,670,655mn and net assets ¥3,471,472mn (equity ratio 94.6%; book value per share ¥14,313.86). Interest-bearing debt is zero, as presented in every period of the sheet. Cash and equivalents were ¥451,269mn at FY3/26 year-end (FY3/22: ¥396,165mn; FY3/23: ¥344,002mn; FY3/24: ¥406,065mn; FY3/25: ¥451,715mn) — the FY3/26 cash balance was flat YoY despite record profit because investing outflow of ¥312,387mn (largely securities and time-deposit placement, per the FY3/26 tanshin cash-flow statement) and financing outflow of ¥113,722mn (dividends) absorbed the ¥430,680mn of operating cash flow. Receivables were ¥379,590mn and inventory ¥85,273mn at FY3/26 year-end; the sheet's calculated inventory measure is about 5.2 months of cost of sales. Depreciation and amortisation was ¥17,227mn in FY3/26, giving an EBITDA proxy of ¥612,986mn — depreciation is only 1.5% of sales, the clearest single marker of the fabless model. ## 2. Latest update — Q2 FY3/27 results

Keyence reported Q2 FY3/27 (H1, 21 March–20 September 2026) results on 29 October 2026. This is the strongest half in the company's history on the figures compiled from the release, and — characteristically — it arrived with no forecast against which to judge it.

## H1 FY3/27 results (cumulative, as reported)

Revenue was ¥735,399mn (+34.9% YoY), **operating profit ¥408,262mn (+50.0%)**, ordinary profit ¥424,348mn (+48.9%) and net income attributable to owners of the parent ¥298,675mn (+49.4%), against a prior-year half of ¥545,300mn / ¥272,179mn / ¥285,000mn / ¥199,959mn. ## 3. Business description

Keyence is headquartered in Higashiyodogawa-ku, Osaka, and develops, manufactures (through outsourced production) and sells electronic application equipment for factory automation: sensors, measuring instruments, machine-vision systems, laser markers, microscopes and code readers. Because the company reports a single segment and discloses no product-level sales, this chapter is built from the company's product and corporate materials (accessed November 2026); no product revenue mix is presented, because none is disclosed.

## Product portfolio

The catalogue spans six families. (1) Sensors — photoelectric, fibre-optic, laser, proximity, pressure and flow sensors, the volume core of factory automation and the original business. (2) Machine-vision systems — cameras, controllers and AI-assisted inspection software used for presence, measurement and defect inspection on production lines; the family most directly exposed to semiconductor, electronics and EV-battery inspection demand, competing with Cognex and Basler. (3) Measuring instruments — optical comparators, 3D measurement, micrometers and displacement sensors for quality-assurance departments as well as lines. (4) Laser markers — for traceability marking on parts and packaging. (5) Digital microscopes — the VHX series, sold into R&D and failure-analysis use as much as production. (6) Barcode and code readers — including AI-equipped readers for logistics and line traceability. The common design philosophy across families is what the company calls added value: products are specified to solve a measured customer problem (a detection that previously failed, an inspection that previously needed a skilled operator) and priced against that value rather than against a component cost-plus, which is the micro-foundation of the 83% gross margin. Source: Keyence product materials (keyence.co.jp / keyence.com), accessed November 2026.

## The direct-sales model: the sales force is the R&D sensor network

Keyence sells direct. Its sales engineers visit customer factories, identify problems on the line, and demonstrate products on the spot; the problems they collect are the primary input to product planning, and new products are developed against the most common, most valuable problems. There are no distributors, so **the margin a peer pays away to distribution is retained** — one leg of the gross-margin structure — and no channel inventory sits between Keyence and end-demand, which is part of why its revenue held up better than distribution-led peers' in the FY3/24 correction (group revenue +4.9% in a year when much of Japanese FA was destocking). The model is labour-intensive by design: the SG&A ratio has risen from 26.9% (FY3/22) to 32.1% (FY3/26) of sales as the company adds sales engineers in Japan and, faster, overseas, where overseas sales reached 66.6% of revenue in FY3/26. Headcount is not disclosed in the tanshin, so the productivity of that investment (sales per sales engineer) cannot be tracked from the earnings releases — a real gap, flagged in Chapter 5. Source: company corporate materials, accessed November 2026; PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive).

## Fabless production and same-day shipping

Production is outsourced: Keyence plans and designs products in-house and commissions manufacture to partner factories, holding finished-goods stock itself so that orders ship the same day. The financial fingerprints are all over the sheet: **depreciation of ¥17,227mn on revenue of ¥1,169,289mn in FY3/26 (1.5%)**; a balance sheet with no borrowings and a 94.6% equity ratio; and inventory of ¥85,273mn — roughly 5.2 months of cost of sales on the sheet's calculation — which is the deliberate working-capital cost of the same-day-shipping promise (stock is held in finished products, close to the customer, rather than in a factory pipeline). The trade-offs are equally structural: Keyence carries stock-obsolescence risk on a catalogue it keeps broad for availability, depends on partner-factory capacity and quality it does not own, and cannot flex a production cost base it does not have — its cost flexibility in a downturn is the SG&A line, i.e. people. None of these quantities (partner capacity, utilisation, unit volumes) is disclosed. Source: company corporate materials, accessed November 2026; PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive).

## Why the margin is structural — and what would break it

The ~51–55% operating margin is the output of three stacked choices, each visible in the accounts: value-based product planning (gross margin 82–84%, versus roughly 40–60% for diversified FA peers — peer margins are third-party general knowledge, not company disclosure, and are stated as context only); direct distribution (no distributor margin, but a heavy owned sales cost inside SG&A); and fabless production (minimal depreciation and no factory fixed-cost absorption problem). The five-year record shows the stack absorbing a demand correction (FY3/24 revenue +4.9%, operating margin still 51.2%) and an SG&A build-out without breaking. What would break it, in order of observability: **a gross-margin break below ~80%** (would signal price competition or mix deterioration — visible in any quarterly print); SG&A growth persistently above revenue growth without an overseas-revenue payoff (the FY3/22–26 pattern is the benign version of this); and a demand air-pocket in vision/inspection tied to semiconductor and electronics capex, which the company gives investors no order data to anticipate. Source: PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); analysis is J-Core Fundamental Research's framing of the reported figures.

## 4. Market and value chain

Keyence operates in factory automation (FA) sensing, inspection and measurement — the 'eyes and nerves' layer of the production line, upstream of the controllers and actuators sold by the diversified FA majors. Demand is a derived function of manufacturing capex and, within it, of the shift from labour-based to automated inspection: every increment of unattended production requires sensing and vision content, which is why the category has grown faster than machinery capex itself over long periods. No company-disclosed market-size figure exists for Keyence's exact category set, and this report does not substitute a third-party market estimate as fact; the demand backdrop visible in reported data is the same AI/data-centre, semiconductor and electronics capex wave lifting the IME complex in 2026, corroborated for Keyence itself by the H1 FY3/27 print (+34.9% YoY) rather than by any order disclosure.

The value chain runs from components (image sensors, lasers, optics, processors) through product planning/assembly — outsourced, in Keyence's case — to the customer interface. Keyence's position is unusual: it occupies the planning, branding, stocking and selling layers and rents the manufacturing layer, the mirror image of a traditional manufacturer. Competition is family-by-family rather than group-against-group: Omron (sensors and vision, distribution-led, the closest domestic overlap), Cognex (machine vision, US), Panasonic and Mitsubishi Electric (broad FA portfolios where sensors/vision are one division among many), Basler (vision components, Germany), and specialists such as Sick and Banner Engineering in sensing. Entry barriers are not patents alone: they are the installed problem-knowledge of the direct sales force, the breadth of a catalogue stocked for same-day delivery, and the switching cost once a sensor or vision recipe is written into a customer's line. Substitutes are principally the customer's own labour (manual inspection) and, increasingly, general-purpose AI cameras and software — the same technology Keyence itself embeds in its newer vision and code-reader products. The structural contest for the next cycle is whether value migrates to the sensing hardware with embedded know-how (Keyence's model) or to platform software and commodity cameras (the Cognex/Basler flank); **the reported gross margin — 85.7% in H1 FY3/27 — is the scoreboard**, and so far it shows no migration. Sources: company product materials, accessed November 2026; competitor positioning from company materials (general); PL/segment sheet.

## 7. Strategy, disclosure policy and what to monitor

There is no mid-term plan chapter to write, and the absence is the strategy disclosure. Keyence publishes no mid-term management plan and no earnings forecast; its stated approach, visible in the tanshin's lack of any forecast section (PL/segment sheet register S7), is that management's task is to maximise added value and long-term profit rather than to manage to a published numerical plan. The observable strategy must therefore be read off behaviour rather than documents: (1) relentless replication of the direct-sales model overseas — overseas sales +15.0% CAGR over FY3/22–26 to 66.6% of revenue, with the SG&A ratio's rise from 26.9% to 32.1% as the funding line, and a new president, Tetsuya Nakano, whose prior role was head of the overseas business strengthening function (Chapter 8); (2) product planning concentrated on problems that recur across industries (inspection automation, traceability, labour-saving measurement), keeping the catalogue broad and the gross margin above 80%; (3) capital policy as the one published forward commitment — the dividend was raised from ¥200 (FY3/22) to ¥550 (FY3/26), a 30.0% payout in FY3/26, with ¥550 forecast again for FY3/27 (pre-split), while buybacks remain negligible and the cash balance is held, not returned, beyond the dividend.

The monitoring set that substitutes for a plan, in the order the data arrives: quarterly revenue growth and gross margin (the first and only demand signal, given no order disclosure); the SG&A ratio and its relationship to overseas growth (annual regional split); the inventory balance against cost of sales (the same-day-shipping stock commitment, ~5.2 months at FY3/26); and the dividend, the sole management-issued forward number. The kill conditions for the structural-margin thesis are equally concrete: **a gross margin print below ~80%**, or two consecutive halves of SG&A growth exceeding revenue growth while the overseas ratio stalls. All figures in this chapter are as reported; the monitoring framework is J-Core Fundamental Research's, and no company targets are implied. Source: PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); FY3/26 tanshin, 24 April 2026.
