An SMT mounter pure-play at a demand inflection: FUJI Corporation (6134, TSE Prime / NSE Premier) is one of the world's leading suppliers of surface-mount technology (SMT) electronic-component placement machines — the 'mounters' that populate printed circuit boards — sold under the Robot Solution segment (93.4% of FY3/26 revenue), alongside a small, currently loss-making Machine Tool segment that is the company's original business. The initiation case rests on the scale and speed of the current upcycle: **FY3/26 revenue rose 41.8% YoY to a record ¥180,642mn** and order intake reached ¥207,429mn, and in H1 FY3/27 (April–September 2026) revenue rose a further 60.7% YoY to ¥127,838mn with operating profit up 250.6% to ¥33,631mn, driven by AI-server and semiconductor-related demand in Asia and North America. Full-year FY3/27 company guidance is **revenue of ¥254,000mn and operating profit of ¥65,500mn — figures that already exceed, by a wide margin, the final-year targets of Midterm Business Plan 2026** (revenue ¥180,000mn, operating profit ¥33,000mn). Strengths: (1) purity and position — Robot Solution earned a 19.9% segment margin in FY3/26 and FUJI competes in the top tier of global placement equipment with its modular NXTR platform and flexible AIMEX series; (2) a debt-free, cash-rich balance sheet (no interest-bearing debt recorded in any of FY3/22–FY3/26; equity ratio 83.5% at end-March 2026; cash and equivalents ¥53,159mn) that funds a shareholder-return policy of a ¥80 dividend floor and a payout ratio of 50% or more, plus a ¥10.0bn buyback executed in November 2026; (3) order disclosure in every tanshin — total and per-segment intake and period-end backlog — which makes the cycle unusually visible. Weaknesses: (1) extreme cyclicality and concentration — revenue fell 17.1% in FY3/24 when China demand rolled over, China alone was 47.7% of revenue in FY3/22, and Asia ex-China jumped to 42.7% in FY3/26 on the Thailand-centred AI-server build-out, so the mix can swing violently by region and by end-market; (2) earnings quality below the operating line in FY3/26 — extraordinary losses of ¥9,960mn against extraordinary income of ¥3,310mn cut profit before tax to ¥24,641mn, well below ordinary profit of ¥31,291mn, so net income to parent rose only to ¥15,733mn despite record operating profit; (3) the Machine Tool segment has been loss-making in four of the last five years and shrank further in H1 FY3/27 (sales −45.3% YoY), and group operating cash flow fell to ¥9,181mn in FY3/26 as receivables and inventory absorbed the growth. All historical figures are as reported by the company (J-GAAP) via the PL/segment sheet built from tanshin in the JPX disclosure archive; the only forward figures are company guidance and company plan targets, labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

FUJI reports under Japanese GAAP with a March year-end. The ten-year record below is as reported in the company's tanshin (earnings releases) filed in the JPX disclosure archive, compiled in the PL/segment sheet for 6134 (FY3/22–FY3/26 actuals plus 20 quarters; all template checks equal zero). Figures are ¥mn unless stated. Note on programme classification: this report is filed under the sector label 'Machine Tools' per the Research Report Editor programme convention; economically, FUJI is primarily an SMT placement-equipment maker, and the Machine Tool segment is its smaller, legacy business (Chapter 3).

The preceding five years, FY3/17–FY3/21, are held in the sheet's supplementary History block, each year as originally reported in its own annual tanshin. Revenue rose from ¥86,397mn in FY3/17 to ¥120,032mn in FY3/18 (+38.9% YoY) and ¥140,967mn in FY3/20, before easing to ¥136,161mn in FY3/21 (−3.4%). Operating profit rose from ¥9,794mn (11.3% margin) in FY3/17 to ¥23,106mn (17.9%) in FY3/19, dipped to ¥19,571mn (13.9%) in FY3/20 and recovered to ¥21,904mn (16.1%) in FY3/21; **Robot Solution supplied the bulk of segment profit in every year** (¥14,545mn in FY3/17, rising to ¥26,307mn in FY3/21), while Machine Tool was loss-making in three of the five years. Order intake rose from ¥94,024mn in FY3/17 to ¥150,021mn in FY3/21 and the period-end backlog nearly doubled across the five years, from ¥23,978mn to ¥46,010mn; the era balance sheets also carried interest-bearing debt — ¥10,070mn at end-FY3/17, including ¥10,039mn of bonds — unlike the debt-free FY3/22–FY3/26 window.

Revenue was ¥148,128mn in FY3/22 and ¥153,326mn in FY3/23 (+3.5% YoY), fell to ¥127,059mn in FY3/24 (−17.1%) as the China-led post-COVID mounter wave normalised, was flat at ¥127,387mn in FY3/25 (+0.3%), then surged to a record ¥180,642mn in FY3/26 (+41.8%) on AI-server and semiconductor-related capex. Operating profit traced the same cycle with wider amplitude: ¥28,472mn (19.2% margin) in FY3/22, ¥27,108mn (17.7%) in FY3/23, ¥13,421mn (10.6%) in FY3/24, ¥13,781mn (10.8%) in FY3/25 and a record ¥29,282mn (16.2%) in FY3/26. Gross margin compressed from 39.5% in FY3/22–23 to 36.4–36.7% in FY3/24–26 and has not recovered despite the volume surge — mix and cost absorption, not price, are doing the work — while the SG&A ratio spiked to 25.8% in the FY3/24–25 trough and fell back to 20.5% in FY3/26 on operating leverage.

Below the operating line, FY3/26 is the year that needs care: ordinary profit was ¥31,291mn, but extraordinary losses of ¥9,960mn against extraordinary income of ¥3,310mn reduced profit before tax to ¥24,641mn, and net income attributable to owners of the parent was ¥15,733mn — below FY3/22's ¥21,188mn despite record sales and operating profit. EPS was ¥219.70 in FY3/22, ¥212.05 in FY3/23, ¥110.59 in FY3/24, ¥119.64 in FY3/25 and ¥178.79 in FY3/26 (calculated in the sheet from reported net income and weighted shares; the share count has fallen steadily — weighted shares 96.4mn in FY3/22 to 88.0mn in FY3/26 — on buybacks and cancellations). Depreciation and amortisation was ¥9,508mn in FY3/26, giving an EBITDA proxy (operating profit + D&A) of ¥38,790mn.

The balance sheet is exceptionally conservative. The sheet records zero interest-bearing debt at every year-end FY3/22–FY3/26; cash and equivalents were ¥53,159mn at 31 March 2026 (FY3/22: ¥59,538mn); total assets were ¥278,356mn and net assets ¥232,454mn at March 2026, an equity ratio of 83.5% (company earnings summary). The cost of growth shows in working capital: receivables rose from ¥34,798mn (FY3/25) to ¥65,341mn (FY3/26) and inventory from ¥53,190mn to ¥64,103mn (6.1 months of cost of sales on the sheet's calculation, improved from 8.1 months in FY3/25), and operating cash flow fell to ¥9,181mn in FY3/26 from ¥23,413mn in FY3/25 and ¥30,187mn in FY3/24. Orders frame the cycle: total intake was ¥163,473mn in FY3/22, ¥142,429mn in FY3/23, ¥116,209mn in FY3/24, ¥123,888mn in FY3/25 and ¥207,429mn in FY3/26 (book-to-bill 1.15x); the period-end backlog was ¥63,592mn, ¥52,695mn, ¥41,845mn, ¥38,346mn and ¥65,133mn respectively. ## 3. Business description

FUJI Corporation is headquartered at 19 Chausuyama, Yamamachi, Chiryu, Aichi Prefecture, Japan, and is listed on the TSE Prime and NSE Premier markets (code 6134). It designs, manufactures and sells SMT electronic-component mounting robots (mounters) and machine tools worldwide; overseas revenue was 90.8% of the FY3/26 total on the customer-location decomposition in Chapter 5, which makes FUJI one of the most export-exposed names in Japanese capital goods. Capital is ¥5,878mn and consolidated headcount was 3,132 (non-consolidated 1,771) at 31 March 2026 (company profile, fuji.co.jp — headcount is not disclosed in the tanshin).

## Robot Solution: SMT mounters (NXTR / AIMEX) and the smart-factory line

The Robot Solution segment （ロボットソリューション事業） is the manufacture and sale of electronic-component mounting robots and related automation. The flagship is the NXTR modular placement platform — the successor generation to the long-running NXT series — positioned for high-volume, high-density production of the kind AI-server and communications boards require: modular heads and feeders that can be reconfigured for component size and throughput, and line-level automation around the mounter. The AIMEX series covers the flexible, high-mix end — machines built to switch quickly between products for automotive, industrial and smaller-lot production. Around the mounters FUJI sells the wider SMT line and its FUJI Smart Factory concept: printers, feeders and material handling, automated kitting and parts supply, and production software that links planning, placement and inspection — the March 2025 partnership with Arch Systems to integrate AI guidance into SMT lines sits in this layer (third-party competitive review, accessed November 2026). The commercial logic is content and lock-in: the mounter is the highest-value, most specification-sensitive machine on the SMT line, placement quality and changeover time drive the customer's line economics, and an installed base pulls parts, feeders, service and software. Segment economics bear this out — **a 23.8% segment margin in FY3/22, still 14.3% in the FY3/25 trough, and 19.9% in FY3/26** (Chapter 5). Source: company product and segment descriptions (fuji.co.jp, accessed November 2026); company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive).

## Machine Tool: the founding business, now subscale

The Machine Tool segment （マシンツール事業） — lathes and other machine tools, including the company's original single-function lathe lineage — is the business FUJI was founded on in 1959, but it is now 5.4% of group revenue (¥9,705mn in FY3/26) and was loss-making in FY3/22 (−¥852mn), FY3/23 (−¥644mn), FY3/24 (−¥786mn) and FY3/26 (−¥107mn), earning a profit only in FY3/25 (+¥740mn, a 6.7% margin). Demand is automotive-led and currently weak: H1 FY3/27 sales fell 45.3% YoY to ¥2,848mn with a ¥136mn operating loss, and full-year FY3/27 company guidance is for ¥9,000mn of Machine Tool sales, below FY3/26. At this scale the segment neither funds nor materially drags the group, but it absorbs management attention and fixed cost, and its order intake fell to ¥8,023mn in FY3/26 from ¥11,796mn in FY3/23. The third category, Other (non-reportable — control and electronic equipment and image processing), contributed ¥2,199mn of sales and ¥85mn of profit in FY3/26. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); company earnings summary, November 2026.

## Technology and production

FUJI's technology position in placement rests on three elements visible in its disclosure and product materials: modular machine architecture (placement heads, feeders and nozzles that let one platform span high-speed chip placement to large/odd-form components), placement accuracy and quality systems for miniaturised components (01005-class chips and dense AI-server boards), and line automation/software (material supply automation and the FUJI Smart Factory platform) aimed at unmanned or lightly manned lines — the direction of travel as EMS customers face labour shortages in Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia and China, the same geographies driving current orders. Production is centred in Aichi (Chiryu headquarters plant and Okazaki), where core modules are machined in-house, with sales and service subsidiaries across Asia, North America and Europe (FUJI EUROPE CORPORATION GmbH in Kelsterbach, Germany, among them). Unit production volumes, capacity and utilisation are not disclosed in the tanshin and are therefore not quantified in this report; the FY3/27 capital-expenditure figure of ¥11,200mn cited in Chapter 2 comes from the earnings presentation as summarised by a third party, not from the tanshin. Source: company materials (fuji.co.jp), accessed November 2026; PL/segment sheet register S8 (not-disclosed items).

## 4. Market and value chain

SMT placement machines sit in the middle of the electronics-manufacturing value chain: component and board makers supply inputs, EMS providers and in-house OEM plants (AI servers, smartphones, automotive electronics, industrial controls) buy lines of printers, mounters, reflow ovens and inspection systems, and **the mounter is typically the rate-limiting, highest-value step**. Demand is therefore a derived, high-beta function of electronics capex — currently dominated by AI data-centre servers and semiconductor-related investment, which is precisely the demand the company cites for H1 FY3/27 (AI-server and semiconductor-related equipment in Asia; telecom-infrastructure and AI-server equipment in North America). Third-party market research puts the global SMT pick-and-place market at roughly US$2.8bn in 2025, growing to roughly US$4.3bn by 2034, with published CAGR estimates ranging widely (about 4.6–9.5% depending on segment definition) — treat the sizing as indicative only (third-party compilations via web search, November 2026; no primary market study was available for this report).

Competition is concentrated at the quality end. The global top tier in placement comprises ASMPT (SIPLACE platform, Hong Kong/Singapore-listed), Panasonic Connect (NPM series, within Panasonic Holdings), Yamaha Motor's surface-mounter business (YRM/YSM series), JUKI, Hanwha Semicon (DECAN series) and FUJI; third-party rankings consistently place FUJI in the top two in placement equipment alongside ASMPT, and the top three suppliers together are estimated to hold over half the placement market (third-party competitive reviews, November 2026 — estimates, not company-disclosed). Entry barriers are real: placement accuracy at speed is a mechatronics and software problem accumulated over decades, global service coverage is a prerequisite for EMS customers running lines 24/7, and qualification cycles at large EMS and OEM accounts are long — which cuts both ways, protecting incumbents' installed bases while making share gains slow. Substitutes are limited at the high end (there is no alternative way to place thousands of components per board at volume), so competition expresses itself as speed/accuracy/automation races and price pressure in mid-range machines, where Chinese and Korean makers are most active.

For FUJI specifically, three sensitivities follow from its own disclosure. First, regional concentration: in FY3/26 **Asia ex-China was 42.7% of revenue and China 27.4% — over 70% of revenue in greater Asia** — and the FY3/22 print (China 47.7% of revenue) shows how a single market's capex pause transmits: group revenue fell 17.1% in FY3/24. Second, end-market concentration: the current wave is AI-server/semiconductor capex, a concentrated, policy- and hyperscaler-budget-sensitive demand pool; order intake by segment, published every quarter, will show any turn first (Robot Solution intake was ¥197,151mn of the ¥207,429mn FY3/26 total). Third, the mid-range price tier: FUJI's premium positioning is defensible in AI-server lines but exposed in cost-sensitive consumer and automotive volume, where Machine Tool's automotive weakness in H1 FY3/27 is a reminder of where the soft demand currently sits. ## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan

The operative strategy document is Midterm Business Plan 2026 (announced 10 May 2024; plan period FY3/25–FY3/27), formulated under a management philosophy of growing existing businesses, developing new businesses and increasing corporate value toward becoming 'the No. 1 brand in the industry', with business strategy advanced through digital transformation (DX), flagship-machine market awareness, and health and productivity in the workplace (company plan materials, May 2024).

Plan targets for the final year, FY3/27, set against the FY3/24 base: net sales ¥180,000mn (FY3/24 actual ¥127,059mn), operating profit ¥33,000mn (FY3/24 actual ¥13,421mn), ROE of 10% (FY3/24: 4.6%) and PBR of 1.1x or more (1.1x at end-FY3/24), with capital allocation managed with awareness of the cost of capital and the share price. The shareholder-return policy in the plan — annual dividends of at least ¥80 per share and a payout ratio of 50% or more — is restated in the Q2 FY3/27 tanshin. All figures in this paragraph are company plan targets, not J-Core Fundamental Research estimates. Source: 'Notice regarding the Formulation of Midterm Business Plan 2026' (FUJI Corporation, 10 May 2024); Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 5 November 2026.

Plan versus reality is now the central strategy fact: FY3/26 revenue of ¥180,642mn already reached the plan's FY3/27 sales target a year early, and ==FY3/27 company guidance (revenue ¥254,000mn, operating profit ¥65,500mn) stands at 141% of the plan's sales target and 198% of its operating-profit target==. The plan was written at the bottom of the mounter cycle (May 2024) and the AI-server capex wave has made its numerical frame obsolete within eighteen months; what remains operative is the return policy and the ROE/PBR capital-efficiency framing. Readers should therefore treat the plan targets as a floor the cycle has already carried the company through, and the next mid-term plan — not yet published at the date of this report — as the document that will reset the frame. The open tensions the next plan must address are visible in this report's data: a subscale, loss-making Machine Tool segment; a ¥80-floor / 50%-payout return policy whose arithmetic does not currently reconcile with the ¥190 dividend forecast on guidance EPS (Chapter 2); and a working-capital model that consumed cash in the fastest growth year on record (Chapter 6).
