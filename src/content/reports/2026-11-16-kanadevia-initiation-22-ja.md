Waste-to-energy scale, backlog visibility — and an execution record that keeps taxing both: Kanadevia Corporation (7004, TSE Prime; renamed from Hitachi Zosen in October 2024) is a plant-engineering group whose centre of gravity has moved decisively to the environment business: waste-to-energy (WtE) plants in Japan and, through Swiss subsidiary Kanadevia Inova, across Europe, the Middle East and beyond. The environment segment was ¥505,230mn of the record ¥645,222mn consolidated revenue in FY3/26 (78.3%), and Inova alone contributed ¥278.4bn — 55.1% of the segment — after compounding at a 25.6% CAGR from ¥111.7bn in FY3/22 (Shared Research report, 5 November 2026). The order book is the strongest part of the story: order intake reached ¥897.7bn in FY3/26, **the backlog stood at ¥2,284.7bn at March 2026 and ¥2,460.4bn at September 2026 — about 3.8x FY3/27 guided sales** — with roughly 64% of the March 2026 backlog in recurring operation & maintenance (O&M) work rather than one-off EPC (Shared Research, 5 November 2026). Strengths: (1) genuine global WtE leadership — a 59.9% share of EMEA WtE plant orders in 2021–25 on Shared Research's compilation, more than 1,500 facilities delivered in 44 countries and regions on the company's own count; (2) a business model shifting from EPC toward long-duration O&M, DBO and concession contracts (Japanese DBO operating terms now run into the 2050s), which lengthens revenue visibility; (3) an active portfolio clean-up — presses, vacuum valves and the solid-state battery business sold, marine engines deconsolidated, bridges to be exited — that is funding the pivot with disposal gains. Weaknesses: (1) profitability has not followed scale — FY3/26 operating profit halved to ¥12,192mn (−54.8% YoY, a 1.9% margin) on Inova technical problems and quality-misconduct costs, and [red]FY3/27 guidance was cut again at Q2 (operating profit ¥25,500mn → ¥23,000mn, 5 November 2026)[/red] on Inova order delays and extra project costs, with the Abu Dhabi WtE project running about three months late; (2) governance and quality are a live, costed issue, not a footnote — unqualified welding over 12 years and marine-engine data falsification over more than 20 years produced quality-misconduct special losses of ¥3,567mn in FY3/25 and ¥2,711mn in FY3/26; (3) the balance sheet is thin for a contractor of this backlog — the equity ratio fell to 27.4% at March 2026 as total assets swelled to ¥718,640mn and interest-bearing debt rose, and both non-environment segments (Machinery & Infrastructure, Carbon Neutral Solutions) were loss-making in FY3/26. All historical figures are as reported by the company (J-GAAP); the only forward figures are company guidance, labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## 1. Key indicators and financial history

Kanadevia reports under Japanese GAAP with a March year-end. No PL/segment sheet exists for this company in the J-Core Fundamental Research programme, so the ten-year record below is compiled from the company's annual securities reports as extracted in EDINET XBRL (via the-shashi's 10-period summary, accessed 16 November 2026) and cross-checked against the Shared Research report of 5 November 2026 and the company's own results materials. Figures are ¥mn unless stated. This is the Shared Research standard history length (FY3/17–FY3/26, ten years); FY3/16 was not verified from an accessible filing and is therefore not presented (see Basis of preparation).

Revenue grew from ¥399,331mn in FY3/17 to a record ¥645,222mn in FY3/26 — a 5.5% nine-year CAGR — but the path was two distinct regimes. From FY3/17 to FY3/21 revenue was essentially flat (¥376,437mn–¥408,592mn) and profitability thin: operating profit ranged from ¥5,907mn (FY3/18, a 1.6% margin) to ¥15,396mn (FY3/21), and net income to parent never exceeded ¥5,864mn. From FY3/22 the Inova-led environment expansion changed the scale: revenue rose ¥441,797mn → ¥492,692mn → ¥555,844mn → ¥610,523mn → ¥645,222mn, and **operating profit climbed to a peak of ¥26,946mn in FY3/25 (4.4% margin)** with net income to parent of ¥22,103mn and ROE of 12.6%. FY3/26 then broke the profit trend while extending the revenue record: operating profit fell to ¥12,192mn (−54.8%), ordinary profit to ¥13,621mn (−44.0%) and net income to parent to ¥11,137mn (−49.6%), because gross profit actually declined (¥109,066mn vs ¥114,184mn) on a less profitable project mix and Inova technical problems, while SG&A rose a further 11.0% to ¥96,874mn.

Per-share and returns data tell the same story. EPS was ¥34.79 in FY3/17, troughed at ¥12.88 in FY3/18, and rose to ¥131.33 in FY3/25 before halving to ¥66.20 in FY3/26. The annual dividend was held at ¥12 for six years (FY3/17–FY3/22), then stepped up to ¥18, ¥23 and ¥25 in FY3/24–FY3/26; the FY3/27 company forecast is ¥38. ROE was 5.1% in FY3/17, 12.6% in both FY3/24 and FY3/25, and 5.8% in FY3/26.

The balance sheet expanded much faster than equity. Total assets rose from ¥393,474mn (FY3/17) to ¥718,640mn (FY3/26, +17.9% in the final year alone), driven by receivables and contract assets (¥261,165mn at March 2026), goodwill (¥31,105mn, up from ¥1,486mn in FY3/22, reflecting the Inova-group acquisition programme) and other intangibles. Net assets rose only to ¥203,400mn, so the equity ratio slipped from 31.1% (FY3/25) to 27.4% (FY3/26) and the D/E ratio (as compiled from the securities reports) rose from 0.56x to 0.83x. Operating cash flow is volatile by construction — ¥478mn in FY3/24 and ¥24,769mn in FY3/25 against ¥11,647mn in FY3/26 — while investing outflow was heavy in FY3/25–FY3/26 (¥56,573mn and ¥48,035mn) on acquisitions and capex of ¥27,493mn and ¥25,207mn. ## 2. Latest update — Q2 FY3/27 results and guidance

Kanadevia reported Q2 FY3/27 (H1, April–September 2026) results on 5 November 2026, with the results briefing on 6 November 2026. This chapter summarises the release; segment and order detail is in Chapter 5.

## H1 FY3/27 results (cumulative, as reported)

Revenue was ¥298,533mn (+11.5% YoY), the operating loss was ¥3,528mn (versus a loss of ¥7,781mn in H1 FY3/26), the ordinary loss was ¥1,095mn (versus ¥8,329mn) and net income attributable to owners of the parent was ¥5,047mn (versus a loss of ¥5,508mn); H1 EPS was ¥30.0. The first half is seasonally loss-making at the operating line for this group — deliveries and acceptances concentrate in H2, above all Q4 — so **the year-on-year narrowing, not the loss itself, is the signal**. Gross profit rose to ¥49.4bn (16.5% margin, +1.9pt YoY) on higher environment sales and the absence of prior-year one-off factors, partly absorbed by higher SG&A (¥53.0bn, including personnel costs) and by the Abu Dhabi WtE delay (about three months, attributed by the company to the situation in the Middle East; related costs are stated to be in the full-year forecast).

Below the operating line, the half was transformed by portfolio disposals: extraordinary gains of ¥12.0bn comprised the ¥4.5bn gain on the sale of the special-valves subsidiary (VTEX, to Kitz, June 2026) and the ¥7.5bn gain on the transfer of the all-solid-state battery business (to Suzuki, effective 1 July 2026). Extraordinary losses included ¥1.5bn of quality-misconduct-related expenses and ¥1.0bn of business-restructuring expenses. Net income therefore rose ¥10.6bn YoY despite the operating loss. The balance sheet at 30 September 2026 showed total assets of ¥694.7bn (down from ¥718.6bn at March 2026 on receivable collections), interest-bearing debt reduced by about ¥40.0bn in the half, and an equity ratio of 28.9% (from 27.4%). H1 operating cash flow was ¥53.3bn on working-capital improvement (receivable collections of ¥51.6bn), investing cash flow was modestly positive on disposal proceeds (business transfers ¥7.5bn, subsidiary share sales ¥7.6bn) against capex of ¥11.2bn, and financing outflow of ¥40.1bn was mainly repayment of short-term borrowings. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 5 November 2026 (figures as compiled by Kabutan/TDnet XBRL); company Q2 FY3/27 results materials and briefing transcript, 5–6 November 2026.

## Guidance: operating profit cut at Inova; orders held at ¥840bn

Full-year FY3/27 guidance, as revised on 5 November 2026, is order intake ¥840,000mn, revenue ¥640,000mn (−0.8% YoY), operating profit ¥23,000mn (+88.6%), ordinary profit ¥22,000mn, net income to parent ¥21,000mn and EPS ¥124.8, with an annual dividend forecast of ¥38 (from ¥25 in FY3/26) — company guidance, not a J-Core Fundamental Research forecast. The only headline change from the August 2026 forecast was operating profit, cut by ¥2,500mn (from ¥25,500mn) for delayed order timing on certain Inova projects and additional costs on some Inova projects; within the unchanged revenue total, Environment EPC sales guidance was trimmed ¥7.0bn and O&M raised ¥7.0bn. Segment operating-profit guidance is Environment ¥24.2bn, Machinery & Infrastructure −¥0.6bn, Carbon Neutral Solutions −¥0.8bn and Other +¥0.2bn — i.e., **the plan assumes the group earns its profit entirely in Environment while the other two core segments remain loss-making for a second year**. FX assumptions for the forecast are ¥190/CHF, ¥150/USD and ¥100/AUD (company materials); the Swiss franc is the key exposure through Inova (the H1 average was ¥198.9/CHF versus ¥180.2 a year earlier).

Against guidance, H1 progress was 49.6% on orders and 46.7% on revenue, with the operating line still negative — normal for the group's H2-weighted completion pattern (in FY3/26, Q4 alone contributed ¥220.5bn of revenue and ¥16.9bn of operating profit on the company's quarterly split), but it leaves the full-year profit dependent on H2 project execution at Inova, the same unit that caused both the FY3/26 profit halving and this cut. The company states that costs known at Q1 are factored into the forecast and that no further Abu Dhabi-type delays are currently anticipated (briefing transcript, 6 November 2026). Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin and results materials, 5 November 2026.

## 3. Business description

Kanadevia is an Osaka-based plant-engineering and machinery group, founded in 1881 as Osaka Iron Works and renamed from Hitachi Zosen Corporation to Kanadevia Corporation on 1 October 2024 — the shipbuilding business that gave the old name its meaning was separated in 2002, and the last marine-engine link was deconsolidated in March 2026. The group reports three core segments plus Other: Environment (¥505,230mn of FY3/26 external sales, 78.3%), Machinery & Infrastructure (¥68,468mn, 10.6%), Carbon Neutral Solutions (¥69,238mn, 10.7%) and Other (¥2,284mn). Consolidated headcount was 12,666 at March 2026 (securities report compilation), of whom about 8,989 were in Environment. The organising idea across the group is the plant life cycle: engineer and build the facility (EPC), then operate, maintain and retrofit it for decades (O&M) — a model the company is extending into DBO (design-build-operate) contracts and, at Inova, into concessions and asset ownership.

## Environment — waste-to-energy, water and the Inova group

Environment is the group. Domestically it designs, builds and operates municipal waste-treatment (WtE) plants, sludge and water-treatment facilities (including through Australian desalination/water subsidiary Osmoflo), and related environmental plants, sold to municipalities typically as EPC contracts followed by long-term O&M, or as integrated DBO contracts. Overseas, the segment is Kanadevia Inova (Zurich), acquired in 2010 as Hitachi Zosen Inova, which supplies WtE and biogas (biomethane) plants across EMEA and beyond and is moving from pure EPC toward O&M, services and project ownership.

The segment's internal split (company materials) is EPC versus O&M. In FY3/26 EPC sales were ¥256.0bn with operating profit of ¥2.5bn (a 1.0% margin — the locus of the Inova problems), while **O&M sales were ¥249.2bn with operating profit of ¥14.2bn (5.7%)**. Inova group sales within those lines were EPC ¥179.1bn and O&M ¥99.3bn, totalling ¥278.4bn, or 55.1% of segment sales; the ex-Inova (mainly domestic) business contributed EPC ¥76.9bn and O&M ¥149.9bn. Shared Research describes Inova's target architecture as a '40/40/20' model to FY3/29 — broadly 40% EPC, 40% O&M/services and 20% asset/concession-type business — intended to dilute EPC cyclicality. H1 FY3/27 showed the direction: Inova sales rose to ¥162.5bn in the half (EPC ¥95.0bn, O&M ¥67.5bn), Inova acquired Dutch biomethane assets (September 2026), and the Casablanca WtE concession (33.5 years including a 30-year long-term service agreement; about 1.5mn t/year; about 126MW; commercial operation scheduled June 2030) was presented as the template for asset-type growth. The offsetting fact is execution: Inova's H1 operating profit was only about ¥2.2bn combined (EPC ¥1.6bn, O&M ¥0.6bn) on those ¥162.5bn of sales, held back by procurement and labour cost inflation and the Abu Dhabi delay. Sources: company Q2 FY3/27 results materials, 5 November 2026; Shared Research report on 7004, 5 November 2026.

## Machinery & Infrastructure — presses, process equipment, bridges and water gates

This segment combines press machines, precision/process machinery and infrastructure (bridges, hydraulic water gates, shield machines and steel structures). It is the segment in fastest retreat. Sales fell from ¥130,816mn in FY3/21 (on the pre-2021 segment basis) to ¥68,468mn in FY3/26, and segment profit swung from +¥1,016mn in FY3/25 to a loss of ¥2,416mn in FY3/26, hit by bridge-related quality-misconduct costs. The portfolio actions are explicit: the press business (subsidiary H&F) was sold to Amada with effect from April 2025; the vacuum/special-valve subsidiary VTEX was sold to Kitz in June 2026 (¥4.5bn consolidated gain in H1 FY3/27); a food-sorting business was divested in September 2026; and bridge operations at the Mukaishima Works are to cease — the works closes and the bridge business is to be exited by 2031 (Shared Research). What remains is weighted to process equipment, precision machinery and water gates/infrastructure, where H1 FY3/27 sales were ¥25.5bn and the operating loss narrowed to ¥1.9bn as bridge quality costs faded. The investment question for this segment is not growth but whether the retained core can return to even a low-single-digit margin once exit costs end; FY3/27 segment guidance is still a ¥0.6bn loss. Sources: company results materials, FY3/26 and Q2 FY3/27; securities-report segment compilation (the-shashi); Shared Research, 5 November 2026.

## Carbon Neutral Solutions — hydrogen, methanation, offshore wind and nuclear-related equipment

Carbon Neutral Solutions (renamed from the decarbonisation/green-innovation lines) houses process equipment for energy and chemicals, hydrogen production (electrolysers) and Power-to-Gas/methanation, offshore wind foundation and installation work, and nuclear-related equipment (notably spent-fuel storage and transport casks through US subsidiary NAC International). Sales were ¥69,238mn in FY3/26 with a segment loss of ¥2,467mn, after the marine-engine business — previously in this segment — was deconsolidated in March 2026 when part of the shares in the marine-engine subsidiary were transferred to Imabari Shipbuilding, reducing the holding from 65% to 40% (now an equity-method affiliate). The deconsolidation mechanically shrinks the segment: H1 FY3/27 sales were only ¥13.5bn (marine-engine sales fell to zero) with a loss of ¥1.8bn, yet order intake in the half was strong at ¥56.8bn — including a large nuclear-related order (spent-fuel dry storage for Taiwan Power Company's Third Nuclear Power Plant, completion scheduled August 2033) and a green-hydrogen partnership in Oman/Middle East with Bahwan Engineering. FY3/27 segment guidance is sales of ¥38.0bn and an operating loss of ¥0.8bn. The segment is best read as an option portfolio — hydrogen/methanation and offshore wind are pre-scale, nuclear casks are a real niche — carried at a small loss while Environment funds it. Sources: company Q2 FY3/27 results materials, 5 November 2026; securities-report segment compilation.

## The EPC / O&M model and why the mix matters

Two economic models coexist in the group. EPC is competitive-bid, percentage-of-completion project work: large, lumpy, working-capital-hungry and exposed to cost overruns, delay damages and warranty provisions — the FY3/26 warranty provision was ¥19,733mn and the loss-on-construction provision ¥8,047mn (securities report). O&M is contracted operation, maintenance, retrofit and after-sales service on the installed base: smaller tickets, multi-year to multi-decade durations, and — on FY3/26 numbers — **roughly five times the operating margin of EPC within Environment (5.7% vs 1.0%)**. The strategic programme of the past five years has been to grow the second model at the expense of the first: O&M order intake was ¥387.7bn of the ¥726.0bn Environment total in FY3/26, Japanese DBO wins in H1 FY3/27 carry operating terms to 2051–52, and about 64% of the March 2026 backlog was recurring business (Shared Research). The limit of the model is that EPC remains the entry ticket — O&M and DBO annuities are won by first winning and successfully building the plant — so Inova's EPC execution problems cannot be diversified away; they sit upstream of the annuity stream. Source: company results materials; annual securities report via EDINET XBRL compilation; Shared Research, 5 November 2026.

## 4. Market and value chain

The value chain runs from municipal and utility customers (in Japan, local governments procuring under competitive tender, increasingly as DBO/PFI packages that bundle 15–25 years of operation; overseas, municipalities, utilities and project developers, increasingly via concessions) through plant engineering contractors — who design, procure boilers, grates, turbines and flue-gas treatment, and manage construction — to the decades-long O&M tail. Kanadevia's claimed installed base is more than 1,500 facilities delivered in 44 countries and regions (company statement in the February 2026 integration filing). Demand drivers are structural rather than cyclical in the near term: ageing Japanese incineration capacity requiring rebuilds and retrofits, waste-volume growth and landfill diversion in EMEA and emerging markets, and biomethane/CO2-capture adjacencies; the constraints are equally structural — engineer scarcity, procurement and labour cost inflation, and tender price discipline.

Competitive position, on Shared Research's compilation (5 November 2026): Inova held a 59.9% share of EMEA WtE orders in 2021–25 — a dominant position against European rivals — while domestically Kanadevia was #4 in EPC with a 12.8% share and #3 in O&M with 11.8%, in a fragmented home market contested by Takuma, JFE Engineering, Ebara Environmental Plant, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Environmental & Chemical Engineering and others. The domestic leader on profitability is Takuma (6013): **on a fraction of Kanadevia's revenue it earns several times the margin** (Exhibit 4) — FY3/26 sales of ¥165,620mn, operating profit of ¥15,409mn (9.3% margin) and record orders of ¥333,026mn (Takuma supplementary materials, 14 May 2026) versus Kanadevia's group operating profit of ¥12,192mn on ¥645,222mn of sales. The contrast is the market chapter in one line: Kanadevia has bought global scale and backlog through Inova; Takuma shows what a domestically focused, O&M-weighted WtE book earns. The cancelled integration with Nippon Steel Engineering (announced February 2026 as an absorption merger with Kanadevia as surviving company, effective April 2027; cancelled September 2026) would have combined the two groups' environmental engineering into a domestic leader — its cancellation leaves the fragmented domestic structure, and Kanadevia's sub-scale domestic share, unchanged, and pushes strategy back to the next medium-term plan due in spring 2027. Sources: Shared Research, 5 November 2026; Takuma FY2025 supplementary materials, 14 May 2026; company integration filing, 5 February 2026 (EDINET).

## Inova in detail: growth bought, margin deferred

Inova's trajectory is the single most important sub-story in the group. Revenue: ¥111.7bn (FY3/22) → ¥278.4bn (FY3/26), a 25.6% CAGR (Shared Research), reaching 55.1% of Environment segment sales and 43.1% of group revenue. The growth was assembled by acquisition as well as organically — group goodwill rose from ¥1,486mn (FY3/22) to ¥31,105mn (FY3/26) and other intangibles from ¥9,964mn to ¥31,834mn over the same period (securities reports), and Environment segment capex jumped to ¥17,625mn in FY3/26. Profitability has not kept pace: within FY3/26, Inova EPC earned ¥4.6bn on ¥179.1bn of sales for the full year on the company's half/full-year split, and in H1 FY3/27 Inova's combined operating profit was about ¥2.2bn on ¥162.5bn of sales (1.4%), as procurement and labour inflation and the Abu Dhabi delay absorbed volume. The FY3/27 guidance cut was attributed entirely to Inova (order delays and additional project costs, −¥2.5bn). Management's stated direction — vertical integration, an asset-management platform, biomethane expansion (Dutch assets acquired September 2026) and the Casablanca concession — is designed to move Inova toward the 40/40/20 mix; **the numbers to monitor are Inova EPC margin, the O&M share of Inova sales (35.7% in FY3/26), and whether guidance survives H2 without a second cut**. Sources: company results materials FY3/26 and Q2 FY3/27; Shared Research, 5 November 2026.

## 7. Mid-term plan — Forward 25 scorecard and the wait for the next plan

The operative plan through FY3/26 was the medium-term management plan 'Forward 25'. Shared Research's target-achievement table (5 November 2026) records a split verdict: order intake reached 149.6% of the plan target (actual ¥897.7bn implies a target of about ¥600bn), while operating profit reached only 43.5% of target (actual ¥12,192mn implies a target of about ¥28.0bn) and ROE printed 5.8% against a 9.5% target. In short, **the plan's demand thesis was beaten by half as much again and its profit thesis missed by more than half** — the cleanest available summary of the group's problem. (The achievement ratios are Shared Research's; the implied targets are calculated from them and are labelled as such.)

There is currently no successor plan in force. The February 2026 agreement to integrate with Nippon Steel Engineering — which would have reset strategy around a combined environmental-engineering champion — was cancelled in September 2026, and the company states that the next medium-term management plan will be announced in spring 2027, with 'initiatives for earnings recovery and sustainable growth' to be detailed there (Q2 FY3/27 materials). The challenges management itself lists for that plan are stabilising overseas Environment earnings, strengthening the domestic Environment business, improving capital efficiency and portfolio reallocation, and redesigning global group governance under the new Group CxO structure — alongside the quality-misconduct recurrence-prevention programme (Group Quality Policy established 1 July 2026; town-hall rollout September 2026). Until spring 2027, FY3/27 guidance (Chapter 2) and the disposal programme are the only forward anchors; this report accordingly presents no plan targets beyond company guidance. Sources: Shared Research, 5 November 2026; company Q2 FY3/27 results materials, 5 November 2026.
