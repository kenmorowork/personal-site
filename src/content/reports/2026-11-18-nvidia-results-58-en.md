Under review: We keep our rating and price target for NVIDIA under review, as set on 12 Nov following the resignation of founder-CEO Jensen Huang. The Q3 FY27 print re-anchors the story on fundamentals — **a 7% revenue beat against the company's own guide and a Q4 outlook above consensus** — but the succession question is untouched by it, and the first formal outlook under interim CEO Colette Kress carries a gross-margin step-down that the market will interrogate.

## Key points
- **Beat on every line.** **Q3 FY27 revenue was US$115.8bn (+103% YoY, +20% QoQ) against company guidance of US$108.0bn ±2%** and Bloomberg consensus of ~US$109.1bn. GAAP gross margin of 74.8% beat the 74.0% ±50bp guide; non-GAAP EPS of US$2.68 compares with US$2.47 expected; GAAP net income was US$65.3bn.
- **Data Center did the work — and the mix broadened.** Data Center revenue reached US$107.6bn (+110% YoY), 93% of total sales. Within it, Hyperscale was US$60.1bn (+71% YoY) while **AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise reached US$47.5bn (+195% YoY) — the non-hyperscaler book is now growing nearly three times as fast**. Edge Computing contributed US$8.2bn (+42%).
- **Q4 guided up on revenue, down on margin.** Q4 FY27 revenue is guided to US$130.0bn ±3% versus ~US$125.3bn LSEG consensus, helped by an additional week in the quarter. **GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin are guided to 71.0–72.0%, a step-down of roughly 3 points from Q3's 74.8%** as Vera Rubin systems ramp. The outlook again assumes zero Data Center compute revenue from China; Q3 itself included US$2.4bn of Hopper shipments to China.
- **Rubin is shipping; commitments jumped.** Vera Rubin NVL72 systems began shipping in the quarter, which management called the fastest product ramp in the company's history. **Supply commitments rose to US$390bn from US$279bn in Q2**, mainly multi-year memory procurement; total future commitments stood at US$518bn at quarter end.
- **First print under Kress; succession still open.** This was the first earnings release under interim CEO Colette Kress, who also remains CFO. **The release refers to an ongoing search and names no candidate**. Shares rose briefly by more than 4% in after-hours trade.

## Results versus guidance and consensus

NVIDIA reported Q3 FY27 (quarter ended 25 Oct 2026) after the US close on Tue 17 Nov. **Revenue of US$115.8bn came in 7.2% above the midpoint of the company's own guidance and about 6% above Bloomberg consensus**; the beat was broad, with gross margin and EPS also ahead (Figure 1). Sequential growth of 20% on a US$96.2bn base quarter is, in absolute terms, an increment of roughly US$19.6bn in one quarter.

The quality of the beat matters more than its size. Gross margin expanded to 74.8% GAAP (74.9% non-GAAP) against a 74.0% ±50bp guide, which management attributed to Blackwell Ultra mix. Nothing in the release suggests the beat was bought with price or pulled forward from Q4: the Q4 guide itself was raised above consensus.

## Data Center: hyperscale is no longer the only engine

Data Center revenue of US$107.6bn (+110% YoY, +21% QoQ) crossed US$100bn for the first time. The disclosure split is the story within the story: **Hyperscale revenue of US$60.1bn grew 71% YoY, while AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise revenue of US$47.5bn grew 195% YoY**. Sovereign, neocloud and enterprise demand is compounding faster than the hyperscaler book, which widens the customer base that the Vera Rubin ramp sells into.

Vera Rubin NVL72 systems began shipping after production ramped, alongside continued Blackwell Ultra growth and initial standalone Vera CPU deployments. Supply commitments of US$390bn (from US$279bn) — primarily multi-year memory — and US$518bn of total future commitments describe a company locking in HBM and capacity well into the Rubin cycle. For the supply chain, that commitment stack is the closest thing to forward order visibility NVIDIA publishes.

## Q4 guidance: growth with a margin step-down

Q4 FY27 revenue guidance of US$130.0bn ±3% sits about 4% above LSEG consensus of ~US$125.3bn and implies +12% QoQ — with the caveat that Q4 contains an additional (14th) week, so the underlying weekly run-rate growth is lower than the headline.

The offset is margin: GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin are guided to 71.0–72.0%, roughly 3 points below Q3's print. Rack-scale systems carry more pass-through content (memory, networking, assembly) per revenue dollar than board-level product, so a Rubin-led mix mechanically dilutes percentage margin even as gross profit dollars grow — at the guide midpoint, Q4 gross profit would be ~US$93bn versus US$86.6bn in Q3. ==Whether 71–72% is a ramp trough or the new Rubin-era level is the single most important question on the call==.

China remains excluded: the outlook assumes no Data Center compute revenue from China, even though Q3 included US$2.4bn of Hopper shipments there. Any licensed China compute revenue is therefore pure upside to the guide — and, after the 17 Nov BIS interim final rule closed the cloud-access loophole, also the least predictable line in the model.

## Governance: a clean first print does not settle succession

The print was delivered under interim CEO Colette Kress, five days after Jensen Huang's resignation took effect. Operationally, nothing slipped: the guide was beaten, the Rubin ramp started on schedule, and the outlook was raised. That supports the view that near-term execution is institutional rather than personal.

What the print does not do is name a permanent CEO, set a timetable, or close the governance file — a closed-door congressional briefing connected to the circumstances of the departure was scheduled for 18 Nov (US). **Until a permanent appointment is made, a key-man discount can re-attach to the shares on any headline, regardless of fundamentals**. We therefore keep the rating under review.

## Read-across to the AIDC complex

For the Industrials, Materials and Energy complex this desk covers, the print is confirmatory rather than new information — but it is strong confirmation. A US$130bn revenue quarter guided with zero China compute, US$518bn of total commitments and a rack-scale platform ramping at record speed all point the same way: **data-centre power, cooling and grid equipment order books (transformers, switchgear, UPS, CDU/liquid cooling) face demand visibility extending well beyond 2026**, and HBM procurement locked in multi-year supports the memory and semiconductor-equipment chain.

The margin guide cuts the other way for one part of the chain: if rack-scale mix dilutes NVIDIA's own percentage margin, value is shifting toward the system level — assembly, power delivery and cooling content per rack — rather than accruing solely to the accelerator. That is consistent with what server and power-equipment makers in our coverage have been reporting.

## What we monitor next

Three things. First, the permanent CEO appointment — any name, timetable or further board change re-prices the governance discount immediately. Second, Q4 gross margin delivery against the 71–72% guide, and the first FY28 margin framing: trough or new level decides how the Rubin cycle is valued. Third, China: Hopper shipments under licence, and any licensing practice under the BIS cloud rule, are unmodelled upside with binary timing.

On the demand side, **hyperscaler capex guides in January will either validate the US$518bn commitment stack or mark its peak**. We will revisit the rating once succession is resolved.
