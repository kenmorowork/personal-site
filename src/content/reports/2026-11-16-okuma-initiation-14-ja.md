Single-source machine tools at a cyclical inflection: Okuma Corporation (6103, TSE Prime) is a comprehensive machine-tool builder that designs and manufactures both the machine and its CNC control (the proprietary OSP) in-house — its long-standing 'single source' model. The investment case at initiation rests on an order cycle that has rarely been stronger: **order intake in H1 FY3/27 (April–September 2026) was a record ¥161,440mn (+44.4% YoY)** and the order backlog reached ¥144,716mn at end-September 2026, while full-year FY3/27 company guidance (unchanged at Q2, 4 November 2026) calls for revenue of ¥260,000mn and operating profit of ¥26,000mn, a 10.0% operating margin. Strengths: (1) a differentiated product core in multitasking machines (MULTUS series) and five-axis machining centres, the process-integration categories customers are buying for labour-saving and automation; (2) disclosure quality unusual in the sector — order intake, backlog and product sales are published in every quarterly tanshin; (3) a fortress balance sheet (equity ratio 69.6% at end-September 2026) and a new shareholder-return policy of a payout ratio of 35% or more under Mid-term Management Plan 2028. Weaknesses: (1) profitability has not kept pace with scale — **the operating margin fell from 11.1% in FY3/24 to 6.6% in FY3/26 and ROE was 5.3% in FY3/26**, below the level the previous mid-term plan targeted, as personnel investment, parts costs and US tariffs absorbed volume gains; (2) the geographic segment structure (entity location) shows Japan carrying group profit while Europe earned a segment margin of only 2.0% in FY3/26; (3) the business remains a classic capital-goods cycle exposure — Japan Machine Tool Builders' Association (JMTBA) orders are at all-time highs in late 2026, and Okuma's own history (revenue −9.3% and operating profit −42.2% in FY3/25) shows how quickly the cycle can reverse. All historical figures in this report are as reported by the company (J-GAAP) via the PL/segment sheet built from tanshin in the JPX disclosure archive; the only forward figures are company guidance, labelled as such.

## Key points

- **Record orders, record backlog.** H1 FY3/27 order intake was **¥161,440mn (+44.4% YoY)**, which the company describes as a record, and the backlog rose to ¥144,716mn at 30 September 2026 (+40.5% YoY) — a book-to-bill of 1.37x for the half. Full-year FY3/27 order guidance was raised to ¥276,000mn on 5 August 2026 (from ¥250,000mn). Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 4 November 2026; company revision notice, 5 August 2026.
- **Guidance implies a sharp margin step-up.** FY3/27 company guidance — revenue ¥260,000mn (+10.2% YoY), **operating profit ¥26,000mn (+67.7%)**, ordinary profit ¥26,500mn, net income to parent ¥20,000mn, EPS ¥343.81 — was raised at Q1 and left unchanged at Q2. H1 delivered 45.4% of guided revenue and 46.3% of guided operating profit, so delivery depends on converting the record backlog in H2. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 4 November 2026.
- **Strength — the single-source model and mix.** Okuma develops the machine and the OSP CNC control together, and its mix is weighted to process-integration machines: in FY3/26 **machining centres were 56.1% of product sales and multitasking machines 25.8%**, against 14.5% for NC lathes. Its Thermo-Friendly Concept and 'Green-Smart Machine' positioning target precision stability and energy saving, the two specifications aerospace, defence, data-centre/energy and semiconductor-equipment customers are currently paying for. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 tanshin qualitative section.
- **Weakness — margins and ROE lag the story.** **Gross margin compressed from 32.7% (FY3/24) to 29.4% (FY3/26)** and the operating margin from 11.1% to 6.6%; ROE was 5.3% in FY3/26 versus the 8%+ floor targeted for FY3/29 under Mid-term Management Plan 2028 and 13–15% under the 2030 Vision. Europe's segment margin was 2.0% in FY3/26. The plan's own bridge requires price increases, rationalisation and volume to outrun a planned ¥15.5bn increase in human-capital investment by FY3/29. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Mid-term Management Plan 2028 (company, 18 May 2026).
- **Disclosure is a genuine differentiator — with defined gaps.** Every tanshin carries order intake and period-end backlog by product (NC lathes / machining centres / multitasking machines / NC grinders / other) plus product sales, and geographic segment profit — visibility few global peers match quarterly. Not disclosed in the tanshin: unit volumes, capacity and utilisation, R&D expenditure, headcount and a customer-location regional sales split in the segment note (an overseas-sales note is provided instead). Source: PL/segment sheet register for Okuma (6103), 2026-11-16.

## 2. Latest update — Q2 FY3/27 results and guidance

Okuma reported Q2 FY3/27 (H1, April–September 2026) results on 4 November 2026. This chapter summarises the release; segment and order detail is in Chapter 5.

## H1 FY3/27 results (cumulative, as reported)

Revenue was ¥118,131mn (+12.3% YoY), **operating profit ¥12,045mn (+98.8%)**, ordinary profit ¥12,766mn (+105.5%) and net income attributable to owners of the parent ¥11,646mn (+155.8%); H1 EPS was ¥198.61. The profit growth rate far exceeded revenue growth because the prior-year half carried the tariff and cost burden with a 5.8% operating margin, whereas H1 FY3/27 printed 10.2%. Standalone Q1 FY3/27 (April–June 2026) had already shown the turn: revenue ¥51,903mn (+16.3% YoY), operating profit ¥4,399mn (+175.1%), net income to parent ¥4,984mn; Q2 standalone therefore contributed revenue of about ¥66,228mn and operating profit of about ¥7,646mn (derived as H1 minus Q1, both as reported).

The company states that consolidated order intake of ¥161,440mn (+44.4% YoY) ==was a record for a half-year==, with demand strengthening in the second half of the period, led by the US and Japan. By end-market, management cites aerospace, space/satellite and defence, data-centre and energy, and semiconductor production equipment as the active areas, with small and mid-sized customers also resuming capex; Europe was described as firm overall despite German automotive weakness, and China as steady in semiconductor equipment and general machinery after the prior year's large projects rolled off. Okuma exhibited five-axis machining centres, multitasking machines and automation systems at IMTS 2026 in Chicago (14–19 September 2026). On the supply side, the Dream Site Engineered Solutions facility at the Konan plant (Konan, Aichi) began operation in January 2026 and is being used both for automated-line concentrated production and as assembly space; Okuma America Corporation (Charlotte, NC) completed a Global Repair Center for spindle and control repair to expand the aftermarket business.

H1 operating cash flow was ¥20,913mn (prior-year H1: ¥8,319mn); investing outflow was ¥2,685mn, helped by ¥4,592mn of investment-securities sales; financing outflow was ¥2,333mn, including ¥5,002mn of share buybacks and ¥2,963mn of dividends, partly funded by ¥5,000mn of new long-term borrowing. Treasury shares rose to 9,338,037 of 67,510,308 issued shares. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin, 4 November 2026 (JPX archive, TDnet 140120261103563384 as recorded in the PL/segment sheet register).

## Guidance: raised at Q1, unchanged at Q2

Full-year FY3/27 guidance, as confirmed unchanged in the Q2 release of 4 November 2026, is revenue ¥260,000mn (+10.2% YoY), operating profit ¥26,000mn (+67.7%), ordinary profit ¥26,500mn (+61.8%), net income to parent ¥20,000mn (+59.3%) and EPS ¥343.81 — company guidance, not a J-Core Fundamental Research forecast. ## 3. Business description

Okuma is a comprehensive (full-line) machine-tool manufacturer headquartered in Oguchi, Aichi Prefecture, Japan. It designs, manufactures and sells CNC machine tools and related systems and services worldwide; overseas sales were 73.3% of revenue in FY3/26 (company integrated-report materials) and 71.5% in H1 FY3/27 (overseas-sales note to the Q2 FY3/27 tanshin: US 29.4% of consolidated revenue, Europe 15.4%, China 15.4%, other Americas 3.9%, other Asia-Pacific 7.4%).

## Product portfolio

The disclosed product split (orders and sales note in every tanshin) has five categories. Machining centres — including vertical, horizontal and five-axis machines — are the largest: **FY3/26 sales of ¥132,309mn (56.1% of product sales)**. Multitasking machines (the MULTUS series, which combines turning and milling in one setup) contributed ¥60,763mn (25.8%). NC lathes contributed ¥34,304mn (14.5%), NC grinders ¥2,424mn (1.0%) and 'other' (including automation systems and services elements classified in the note) ¥6,087mn (2.6%). The strategic weight sits in the first two categories: five-axis machining centres and multitasking machines are the 'process-integration' machines that consolidate multiple setups into one, which is the core of the company's labour-saving and automation proposition and of its IMTS 2026 exhibit. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive), FY3/26 product sales.

## The single-source model: machine and OSP control developed together

Okuma's defining structural choice is that **it develops its CNC control — the OSP (Okuma Sampling Path) system — in-house**, together with the machine, its servo motors and encoders. The company markets this as being the customer's 'single source' for the complete machining system: one supplier is responsible for machine, control and increasingly the automation cell around them. The commercial consequences are threefold. First, differentiation: features that require deep machine–control integration (thermal compensation, collision avoidance, machining-condition optimisation) can be engineered as one system rather than assembled from a third-party control. Second, switching costs and aftermarket attach: OSP familiarity, service and parts tie the installed base to Okuma, supporting the service business the company is expanding (for example the Charlotte Global Repair Center for spindles and controls, completed in 2026). Third, cost and risk: Okuma carries the full R&D and support burden of a controls platform that competitors such as Mazak (Mazatrol, also in-house) share but that many builders avoid by buying FANUC or Siemens controls. R&D expenditure is not disclosed in the tanshin (it appears in the annual securities report), so the intensity of this commitment cannot be tracked quarterly from the earnings releases alone. Source: company descriptions (Okuma corporate materials, accessed November 2026); Q2 FY3/27 tanshin.

## Thermo-Friendly Concept, Green-Smart Machine and automation

Two named technologies anchor the product strategy. The Thermo-Friendly Concept is Okuma's thermal-deformation control architecture: machine structures designed to accept predictable thermal growth, combined with control-based compensation, so that dimensional accuracy is maintained through warm-up and ambient change without lengthy warm-up cycles — a direct productivity and quality claim for high-precision users. Under Mid-term Management Plan 2028 the company positions its machines as 'Green-Smart Machines': machines that autonomously reconcile high-precision, high-efficiency production with reduced energy consumption, contributing to customer decarbonisation. Around the machine, Okuma sells automation (loaders, robots, pallet systems and its ARMROID/AROUD concepts), AI and 'intelligence' functions, and what it calls 'monozukuri services' — production-support services spanning the machine life cycle. These elements matter financially because they raise content per installation and service attach in a market where the binding customer constraint is increasingly skilled labour, not machine availability. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin qualitative section; Mid-term Management Plan 2028 (company, 18 May 2026).

## Production system: Dream Site smart factories

Production is concentrated in Aichi and Gifu, Japan — the headquarters plant at Oguchi, the Kani plant (Kani, Gifu) and the Konan plant (Konan, Aichi) — organised under the company's 'Dream Site' smart-factory concept: highly automated, digitally connected plants intended to run mixed-model production of machines, parts and controls with short lead times. The newest step is Dream Site Engineered Solutions at Konan, in operation since January 2026, which concentrates automated-line production and adds assembly space to meet the current demand increase. Overseas, Okuma Machine Tools Inc. has manufactured in Charlotte, North Carolina since the late 1980s, and the group sells and services through subsidiaries including Okuma America Corporation (US), Okuma Europe GmbH and Okuma Deutschland (Germany), and Okuma Machine Tool (Shanghai) in China. Unit production volumes, capacity and utilisation are not disclosed in the tanshin and are therefore not quantified in this report. Source: Q2 FY3/27 tanshin; company history materials (Okuma Europe / Okuma Corporation, accessed November 2026).

## 4. Market and value chain

Machine tools are the 'machines that make machines': demand is a derived, high-beta function of manufacturing capex across automotive, aerospace, energy, semiconductor equipment, construction and general job-shop machining. The cycle is currently at an extreme. JMTBA orders for October 2026 were ¥204.4bn (+42.5% YoY), the second-highest month on record after September 2026's all-time high of ¥223.2bn; **cumulative January–October 2026 orders of ¥1,873.7bn (+43.2% YoY) already exceeded the previous full-year record** of ¥1,815.8bn set in 2018, with two months still to run (JMTBA flash report, 11 November 2026). Foreign orders were ¥152.1bn in October (about 74% of the total) and domestic orders ¥52.3bn (+46.5% YoY). The JMTBA attributes the current wave not to automotive — historically the largest end-market — but to AI/data-centre investment, semiconductor equipment, energy and aerospace, a mix that favours high-precision five-axis and multitasking machines of the type Okuma emphasises; the association's chairman has simultaneously cautioned that a pause in data-centre and semiconductor demand is the risk to watch (JMTBA commentary, October 2026).

The value chain runs from components (castings, ball screws, spindles, servo motors, CNC controls) through machine builders to distributors/dealers and end-users, with an aftermarket tail of service, parts and rebuilds. Okuma is unusually vertically integrated at the high-value end of that chain: it makes its own controls, motors and encoders, and casts and machines key components in-house, which supports both its single-source differentiation and its exposure to fixed-cost absorption when volumes fall. Competition is global and concentrated among a handful of full-line builders: DMG Mori (6141) — the largest Japanese builder, with a strong European base and a services/automation push; Yamazaki Mazak (unlisted) — the closest analogue, with in-house Mazatrol controls and global plants including the US and UK; Makino Milling Machine (6135) in high-precision machining centres and EDM; and FANUC (6954) as both the dominant third-party CNC supplier and a competitor in compact machines (Robodrill) and automation. Regional Chinese builders compete at the volume end. Okuma's disclosed scale — FY3/26 revenue ¥235,888mn — places it below DMG Mori and Mazak but among the global leaders in multitasking machines, where MULTUS is a reference product.

For Okuma specifically, three market sensitivities follow from its disclosure. First, the US is the single largest demand pool: 29.4% of H1 FY3/27 revenue by customer location (overseas-sales note), and the Americas segment (entity location) was 29.0% of FY3/26 segment sales — so US capex, the dollar and US tariff policy transmit directly; management cited tariff cost effects during FY3/26. Second, China was 15.4% of H1 FY3/27 revenue by customer location and is contested by both local builders and export-control risk. Third, the demand mix driving the 2026 record — data centres, semiconductors, aerospace/defence — is capex-concentrated and policy-sensitive; Okuma's backlog gives near-term visibility, but order intake, published every quarter by product, is the variable that will show any turn first. Sources: JMTBA flash statistics via press reports, 11 November 2026; company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY3/27 tanshin overseas-sales note.

## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan

Mid-term Management Plan 2028 (announced 18 May 2026; plan period FY3/27–FY3/29) is the operative strategy document, set under a 2030 Vision. The review of the predecessor plan is candid in the company's own materials: under Mid-term Plan 2025, FY3/26 revenue reached a record but missed the plan target, and ==operating margin (6.6%), ROE (5.3%) and ROIC (4.3%) all fell short of target== (company securities-report summary of the plan review, FY3/26).

Plan 2028 targets, for FY3/29 (the plan's final year, 'FY2028' in the company's naming): **revenue ¥270,000mn, operating margin of 11% or more** (the plan's profit bridge builds to operating profit of about ¥30,000mn), and ROE of 8% or more, on FX assumptions of USD/JPY 150 and EUR/JPY 180. ROE is designated the single most important management indicator. The 2030 Vision beyond the plan: revenue ¥300,000mn, operating margin of 15% or more and ROE of 13–15% by FY3/31. The strategy has two pillars — 'customer-driven value creation' (market-driven management; AI and automation solutions; five-axis and multitasking process-integration machines; Green-Smart Machines and 'monozukuri services' globally) and 'reform of the business foundation' (value-chain strengthening and human-capital investment). The FY3/29 operating-profit bridge published with the plan is instructive about the bet: volume growth +¥17.2bn, price improvement +¥4.6bn, rationalisation +¥5.4bn and FX +¥0.8bn are expected to outweigh cost increases, a ¥10.4bn rise in expenses, ¥3.4bn of additional depreciation and a deliberate ¥15.5bn expansion of human-capital investment (all ¥bn, company plan materials, as summarised by EDINET DB).

Financial strategy (company plan materials): cumulative FY3/27–29 operating cash flow of ¥90,000mn or more is allocated to investment of ¥42,000mn or more (growth ¥23,500mn, renewal ¥11,000mn, ESG ¥7,500mn — including AI-based machine autonomy development and a new plant in China) and shareholder returns of ¥22,000mn or more. The dividend policy is a payout ratio of 35% or more, with total returns benchmarked to the Mid-term Plan 2025 period level; balance-sheet targets include inventory of 5.0 months and cross-shareholdings below 10% of net assets by FY3/29. The FY3/27 dividend increase to ¥120 (from ¥100), announced with the August 2026 forecast revision, is the first application of that policy. All figures in this chapter are company plan targets and policies, not J-Core Fundamental Research estimates. Source: Mid-term Management Plan 2028 (Okuma, 18 May 2026; Japanese release 18 May, English 28 May 2026); company revision notice, 5 August 2026.

## 8. Other information

History. Okuma was founded in January 1898 in Nagoya by Eiichi Okuma as Okuma Menki Shokai, a maker of noodle-making machines. Machine-tool manufacture began in 1904 (not 1937, as sometimes summarised); the company was renamed Okuma Iron Works in 1916 and incorporated as Okuma Iron Works Ltd. in 1918, and by 1937 its machine-tool production value was the largest in Japan. Post-war milestones relevant to today's structure include the Oguchi headquarters plant (1969), US production at Charlotte through Okuma Machine Tools Inc. (1987), Okuma Europe GmbH (1988), the renaming to Okuma Corporation (1991), Okuma America Corporation (1995), and the 2006 four-company merger that created the current group. The founding maxim — 'create what does not exist' — remains the company's stated spirit.
