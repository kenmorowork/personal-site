Prices told a different story: **Nvidia lost its founder-CEO mid-week and US producer prices rose at the fastest annual pace since November 2022**, unwinding the early-week semiconductor rotation by Friday.

## Key points
- **Demand was confirmed in volumes.** **TSMC's October revenue was a record NT$586.89bn (+59.7% YoY)**; Hon Hai's Q3 net profit was a record NT$77.5bn (+34%) with AI servers at 54% of sales; Applied Materials' Q4 revenue was a record (+53% YoY) and Cisco's Q1 revenue a record (+24%) with FY27 guidance raised.
- **Nvidia was the week's price event.** Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and director with immediate effect (announced 12 Nov); CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. **The shares opened -13.1% on 12 Nov, recovered to close -4.34%, then fell a further 2.0% on 13 Nov to $162.85**, below a $4tn market cap.
- **Power and machinery breadth was the second story.** **Siemens' Q4 orders rose 58% to a record €34.77bn; Hitachi Energy India's orders rose 85.9% with a record backlog of ₹333.35bn** and data centres its No. 2 order source; Japan's machine-tool orders hit an annual record in ten months; India approved its first 1,150 kV transmission project.
- **Macro pushed the other way.** **US October CPI came in hot (+0.4% MoM vs +0.3% consensus) and PPI rose +6.5% YoY, the largest since November 2022**. Friday's US close unwound the rotation: Nasdaq -0.91%, SOX -3.4%, AMD -8.8%.
- **Capex and supply chains are still tightening.** Nebius raised 2026 capex guidance to about $27bn; **CoreWeave's backlog reached $132.5bn; SMIC's utilisation was 97.3%**; Samsung pulled its HBM packaging plant forward a year to June 2028; China's domestic AI accelerators' combined share passed Nvidia's for the first time (Huawei Ascend 28%).

## Part 1 — Market performance

## The week in numbers — rotation, then unwind

The week had two halves. Into Wednesday, semiconductor and AI names were bought: on 11 Nov (US) the SOX rose 5.66% with all 30 constituents up, and CoreWeave gained 24.71% after its print. Tokyo did not follow on 12 Nov — the Nikkei fell 0.70% to 60,819.36 on afternoon selling in chip and AI names (Advantest the largest drag) while TOPIX rose 0.20%, showing the selling was concentrated rather than broad.

**By Friday the trade had reversed. The US close on 13 Nov was Nasdaq -0.91%, SOX -3.4%, AMD -8.8% and Nvidia -2.0%**. Tokyo steadied the same day (Nikkei +0.53% to 61,142.35, TOPIX +0.11%). ## Part 2 — Coverage, capex and statistics

## Semiconductors and AIDC — the prints

TSMC's October revenue of NT$586.89bn (+59.7% YoY) was its largest month on record, taking January–October revenue to NT$4,485.62bn (+43.3%). Applied Materials posted record Q4 revenue (+53% YoY) and Cisco record Q1 revenue (+24%) with FY27 guidance raised. In Japan, Japan Micronics' nine-month operating profit rose 138.9% YoY with guidance and the dividend raised, and Eblen's H1 operating profit rose 106% — evidence that HBM-related investment is reaching measurement and control equipment.

On the demand side, **CoreWeave's Q3 revenue was $3.66bn (+168% YoY) with backlog of $132.5bn, but a net loss of $709M and interest expense of $846M**; Nebius' Q3 revenue was $980.8M (+571%) while 2026 capex guidance rose to about $27bn and some third-party capacity slipped into H1 2027. Anthropic's Q3 compute and infrastructure spend was $9.9bn, 58% of its revenue. The constraint set — funding cost and delivery capacity, not end demand — is unchanged. ## Power, machinery and factory automation

Siemens' Q4 orders of €34.77bn (+58%) were a record, led by smart infrastructure including data-centre electrical equipment, and its medium-term growth target was raised. Hitachi Energy India's Q2 (Jul–Sep) orders rose 85.9% to ₹41.21bn on revenue of ₹29.97bn (+63.6%), with backlog at a record ₹333.35bn — roughly 11x one quarter's revenue. GE Vernova won 765 kV transformer orders (70+ units) from India's POWERGRID, and India approved its first 1,150 kV project (₹68,816 crore, about six years).

Japan's October machine-tool orders were ¥204.4bn (+42.5% YoY), breaking the annual record in ten months. Amada raised its FY net profit forecast to ¥40bn (+31%) on AI data-centre sheet-metal demand; SMC raised its full-year recurring profit forecast by 36.0% with a buyback of up to ¥50bn; THK raised its net profit forecast to ¥58.8bn; Takasago Thermal raised its operating profit forecast 20% to ¥60bn with an orders forecast of ¥620bn for the first time. On 14 Nov, Infineon announced supply of the first 900V GaN bidirectional switch to Enphase for solid-state transformers — the power-semiconductor layer of the same AIDC power chain.

## Part 3 — Catalysts and the week ahead

## Catalyst attribution — why it moved, why it happened

Figure 6 lists the events that actually drove the week's performance, with the mechanism in each case. Two patterns matter. First, ==the largest single-stock move (Nvidia) was a governance event, not a demand event== — the announcement came because a board investigation into the founder's conduct left no alternative, and the price fell on succession uncertainty (no designated No. 2, a CFO as interim CEO) rather than on any change to orders or guidance. Second, the sector-level moves were expectation trades: hot CPI and PPI prints moved the discount rate, and the same names that had been bought on prints early in the week were sold on macro by Friday.
