The motion-control core inside everyone else's automation: Yaskawa Electric Corporation (6506, TSE Prime and Fukuoka) is the Kitakyushu-based factory-automation group built on 'electric motors and their applications': it claims the leading global position in AC servo motors and controllers, sells inverters (AC drives) worldwide, and is one of the 'big four' industrial-robot makers with its MOTOMAN brand, organised in three reportable segments — Motion Control, Robotics and System Engineering — plus a small Other (logistics). The initiation question is whether a demand supercycle now visible in orders can be converted into the profit recovery the new mid-term plan promises. The demand evidence is strong: **quarterly order intake hit a record ¥185.4bn in Q2 FY2/27 (+38% YoY**, company briefing materials, October 2026), driven by data-centre and semiconductor capex, and full-year FY2/27 guidance was revised on 9 October 2026 to revenue of ¥600,000mn (+10.7% YoY). Strengths: (1) a component franchise with genuine technology depth — Yaskawa coined 'mechatronics' in 1969, makes the servos, controllers and robots as one system under its i³-Mechatronics concept, and supplies motion components to the wider industry, not only to its own robots; (2) breadth of the current upcycle — H1 FY2/27 Motion Control revenue rose 21.7% YoY with growth in every region, and overseas revenue reached 75.2% of the total (briefing materials); (3) a solid balance sheet (equity ratio attributable to owners 59.5% at February 2026) and a rising dividend (¥68 in FY2/26 to a forecast ¥72 in FY2/27). Weaknesses: (1) profitability has fallen for three consecutive years — the operating margin compressed from 12.3% in FY2/23 to 8.7% in FY2/26 and ROE to 7.7%, as volume fell and then costs, wages and depreciation rose faster than added value; (2) earnings quality needs care — FY2/25 profit before tax of ¥78,454mn included a one-off gain of ¥26,777mn on the sale and revaluation of associate investments, so the reported FY2/25 profit growth was not operating; (3) execution is currently impaired by the group's own systems change — a new core (ERP) system disrupted production and shipments in H1 FY2/27, Robotics profit fell 43.3% YoY in the half, and full-year operating-profit guidance was cut to ¥57,500mn even as revenue guidance was raised. Against that record, the new mid-term plan 'Dash 35' (announced 22 May 2026) **targets revenue of ¥650,000mn and a record operating profit of ¥100,000mn (15.4% margin) by FY2/30 — more than double FY2/26 operating profit**. All historical figures in this report are as reported by the company (IFRS) via the PL/segment sheet built from tanshin in the JPX disclosure archive; the only forward figures are company guidance and company plan targets, labelled as such. No rating or price target is assigned at initiation (Under Review).

## Key points

- **Record orders, led by Motion Control.** Consolidated order intake in Q2 FY2/27 (June–August 2026) was ¥185.4bn, which the company describes as **a record-high quarterly intake, +38% YoY and +13% QoQ**, following ¥163.8bn in Q1 (+29% YoY); **Motion Control orders rose 56% YoY in Q2** on data-centre and semiconductor demand. Orders are disclosed in briefing materials, not in the tanshin. Source: FY2026 1H financial results briefing materials (company, October 2026).
- **Guidance: revenue up, operating profit down — read the revision correctly.** At Q2 FY2/27 (9 October 2026) the company raised FY2/27 revenue guidance from ¥580,000mn to ¥600,000mn but [red]cut operating-profit guidance from ¥60,000mn to ¥57,500mn[/red], while lifting profit before tax to ¥65,500mn and net income to owners to ¥47,500mn (basic EPS ¥183.11). H1 delivered 47.6% of guided revenue but only 38.4% of guided operating profit, so the year depends on H2 normalisation of production and shipments after the ERP disruption. Source: Q2 FY2/27 tanshin, 9 October 2026; company briefing materials, October 2026.
- **Strength — the servo core is compounding again.** Motion Control was 43.5% of FY2/26 revenue and is the growth engine of the current cycle: H1 FY2/27 **segment revenue was ¥137,286mn (+21.7% YoY) and segment profit ¥15,973mn (+32.8%), an 11.6% margin**, with AC servo and controller sales up in every region on semiconductor, electronic-components and machine-tool demand, and drives lifted by data-centre, semiconductor-equipment and oil & gas sales. Source: Q2 FY2/27 tanshin segment note, 9 October 2026.
- **Weakness — three years of margin erosion, and FY2/25 flattered by a one-off.** Group operating profit fell from ¥68,301mn (FY2/23) to ¥47,307mn (FY2/26); the SG&A ratio rose from 23.8% to 26.9% of revenue over the same period. FY2/25 is the trap year for screens: operating profit fell 24.3% to ¥50,156mn, yet profit before tax rose 13.6% to ¥78,454mn because of **a ¥26,777mn gain on the sale and revaluation of associate investments** recorded in Q3 FY2/25 below operating profit. Underlying FY2/25 earnings declined. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive).
- **The plan asks for a step-change: Dash 35 targets ¥100bn of operating profit by FY2/30.** The long-term plan 'Vision 2035' and first mid-term plan 'Dash 35' (FY2/27–FY2/30, announced 22 May 2026) target FY2/30 revenue of ¥650,000mn, **operating profit of ¥100,000mn (15.4% margin, a record high)**, ROE of 12% or more, ROIC of 11% or more and a dividend payout ratio of 40% or more, with physical AI — centred on the adaptive AI robot MOTOMAN NEXT — as the growth frontier. These are company plan targets, not J-Core Fundamental Research estimates. Source: company release 'New Long-term Business Plan Vision 2035 and New Mid-term Business Plan Dash 35', 22 May 2026.

Yaskawa reported Q2 FY2/27 (H1, 1 March–31 August 2026) results on 9 October 2026. ## H1 FY2/27 results (cumulative, as reported)

Revenue was ¥285,598mn (+9.8% YoY), operating profit ¥22,058mn (−5.5%), profit before tax ¥25,963mn (+3.0%) and net income attributable to owners of the parent ¥18,988mn (+4.1%); H1 basic EPS was ¥73.21. The split between the halves of the half is informative: Q1 FY2/27 standalone (March–May 2026) printed revenue of ¥138,982mn (+10.6% YoY) and operating profit of ¥8,486mn (−19.2%), so Q2 standalone (June–August 2026, derived as H1 minus Q1, both as reported) contributed revenue of about ¥146,616mn and operating profit of about ¥13,572mn — a sequential recovery as production and shipments normalised. The profit decline on higher revenue has three company-stated causes: the new core (ERP) system introduced to strengthen the management base disrupted production and shipments; indirect expenses increased; and temporary expenses for business structural reform in Europe were recorded. Profit before tax rose despite lower operating profit because equity-method investment income increased (¥1,992mn recognised in H1 per the cash-flow statement, against ¥165mn a year earlier) alongside net financial income. The company's H1 operating-profit bridge (briefing materials) quantifies the tug-of-war: foreign exchange +¥4.6bn and volume +¥3.6bn against added value −¥0.6bn (including the ERP production impact), total expenses −¥6.3bn (wages and depreciation) and 'other' −¥2.7bn (European restructuring expenses and the absence of prior-year other income).

Demand, by contrast, was the strongest in the company's history on the order measure it discloses in briefing materials: consolidated orders were ¥163.8bn in Q1 FY2/27 (+29% YoY) and **a record ¥185.4bn in Q2 (+38% YoY, +13% QoQ)**, with Motion Control orders +48% YoY in Q1 and +56% in Q2, and **Q2 AC servo orders +76% YoY**. Management attributes the intake to AI-related investment — data centres and semiconductors — with general-industry automation demand also firm; by region in Q2, orders rose in every location, led by the Americas (+60% YoY) and Asia ex-China (+53%). Average exchange rates in H1 were USD/JPY 159.82, EUR/JPY 184.91, CNY/JPY 23.48 and KRW/JPY 0.108, all weaker-yen than a year earlier (Q2 FY2/27 tanshin).

The balance sheet at 31 August 2026: total assets ¥842,833mn (+¥30,467mn vs February 2026), total equity ¥514,590mn, owners' equity ratio 59.8%. H1 operating cash flow was ¥45,628mn (prior-year H1: ¥15,804mn) on improved receivables collection; investing outflow was ¥29,642mn, including ¥27,286mn of tangible and intangible asset purchases and ¥3,736mn for an acquisition involving a change in consolidation scope; financing outflow was ¥12,776mn, including a ¥10,000mn bond redemption, ¥15,007mn of new long-term borrowing and ¥8,861mn of dividends. Cash and equivalents ended the half at ¥66,083mn; free cash flow (operating plus investing) was ¥15,985mn per the company. Two capacity milestones were completed in the half: full-scale operation of Robot Factory No. 5 in Kitakyushu, which integrates AC servo and robot production and uses the AI robot MOTOMAN NEXT in a decentralised cell system, and of the System Engineering factory at the Minamiyukuhashi plant (briefing materials). Source: Q2 FY2/27 tanshin, 9 October 2026 (JPX archive); FY2026 1H briefing materials (company, October 2026).

## 3. Business description

Yaskawa Electric is headquartered in Kitakyushu, Fukuoka Prefecture, and listed in Tokyo and Fukuoka. Since its founding in 1915 the group has defined its domain as 'electric motors and their applications', and today organises itself around three core technologies — motion control, power conversion and robotics — delivered through three reportable segments plus Other. The segment definitions below are the company's own (Q2 FY2/27 tanshin, segment note): Motion Control develops, manufactures, sells and services AC servo motors, control devices and inverters; Robotics develops, manufactures, sells and services industrial robots and related products; System Engineering develops, manufactures, sells and services industrial automation drives and social (infrastructure) systems. Segment profit is on an operating-profit basis. In FY2/26 the mix was Motion Control 43.5% of revenue, Robotics 45.6%, System Engineering 7.1% and Other 3.7% (external revenue basis, PL/segment sheet).

## Motion Control: AC servos, controllers and drives

Motion Control has two businesses. The AC servo motor and controller business sells the servo systems — motors, amplifiers (the Σ (Sigma) series is the flagship line) and machine controllers such as the new MPX1310 series — that position machine tools, semiconductor and electronic-components equipment, and robots themselves; Yaskawa states it holds the leading global share in AC servo motors and controllers, and cumulative AC servo shipments reached 20 million units in 2020 (company materials via PitchBook company profile, accessed November 2026). The drives (inverter) business sells AC drives including the GA700 series, PV (solar) inverters — the Enewell-SOL line — and the U1000 matrix converter, whose harmonic-suppression characteristics are currently winning data-centre HVAC and server-cooling orders in the US (briefing materials). Financially this is the group's highest-quality segment at scale: revenue of ¥227,260mn / ¥252,126mn / ¥260,035mn / ¥238,752mn / ¥236,053mn across FY2/22–26 with segment profit of ¥38,161mn / ¥36,193mn / ¥38,198mn / ¥23,005mn / ¥24,384mn — margins of 16.8% / 14.4% / 14.7% / 9.6% / 10.3%. The FY2/25 margin halving, on an 8.2% revenue decline, is the clearest operating-leverage evidence in the group, and **the FY2/27 guidance margin of 13.6% is the single most important number in the recovery case**. One comparability point is essential: from Q1 FY2/25, solar power conditioners were moved from System Engineering into Motion Control as part of a reorganisation of the System Engineering business, and prior-year figures in the company's releases were restated onto the new basis — the PL/segment sheet captures segment history on the basis disclosed in each period's documents, so long series should be read with that break in mind (see Chapter 5). Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY2/27 tanshin segment note; FY2/25 tanshin qualitative section.

## Robotics: MOTOMAN, from arc welding to AI robots

Robotics sells industrial robots under the MOTOMAN brand — arc- and spot-welding robots (for example the MOTOMAN-AR series), painting robots, handling robots, semiconductor wafer-transfer robots, biomedical robots, the HC-series collaborative robots and, most recently, the MOTOMAN NEXT series of adaptive AI robots, controlled by the YRM controller line. The lineage is the company's founding claim in robotics: the MOTOMAN-L10 of 1977 was Japan's first all-electric industrial robot, and cumulative MOTOMAN shipments reached 500,000 units in February 2021 (company release). Applications remain anchored in automotive (welding and handling systems) but the growth mix has shifted to semiconductor robots (strong in China in H1 FY2/27), general industry, food, pharmaceuticals/cosmetics and 3C, and now to previously hard-to-automate tasks addressed by MOTOMAN NEXT — mixed-case palletising and food-topping automation are the two customer deployments the company showcased in its H1 FY2/27 materials. Revenue was ¥178,670mn / ¥223,829mn / ¥234,680mn / ¥237,413mn / ¥247,012mn across FY2/22–26 — Robotics became the largest segment by revenue in FY2/25 — but profit moved the other way: ¥17,248mn / ¥26,126mn / ¥25,149mn / ¥23,751mn / ¥20,418mn, margins of 9.7% / 11.7% / 10.7% / 10.0% / 8.3%. The company attributed the FY2/25 decline to lower plant utilisation and advance investment to strengthen systems capability, and H1 FY2/27 added Japan ERP disruption and European restructuring costs (segment profit −43.3% YoY in the half). **Robotics is therefore the segment where volume recovery has not yet become profit recovery** — and where the Dash 35 physical-AI strategy must prove itself. Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); company materials, accessed November 2026.

## System Engineering and Other

System Engineering is the project business: electrical systems for steel plants, port-crane systems, water supply and sewage systems, and industrial automation drives, sold largely as engineered projects with lumpy revenue recognition. It is small — revenue of ¥52,265mn / ¥51,111mn / ¥55,455mn / ¥38,352mn / ¥38,744mn across FY2/22–26 — and its history carries the segment-basis break described above: the FY2/25 revenue fall of 30.8% is partly the removal of solar power conditioners to Motion Control (restated comparisons in the company's own releases show a smaller underlying decline) and partly genuinely weak project flow. Profitability has, unusually, improved as the segment shrank: segment profit of ¥2,126mn / ¥2,574mn / ¥5,637mn / ¥4,605mn / ¥4,989mn, margins of 4.1% / 5.0% / 10.2% / 12.0% / 12.9%, and H1 FY2/27 continued the pattern (revenue +11.2% YoY on port-crane sales; profit +79.5% on project-margin improvement). FY2/27 guidance of ¥5,600mn of segment profit would be a five-year high. Other — principally logistics services — contributed revenue of ¥20,311mn and profit of ¥1,988mn in FY2/26. Corporate and elimination adjustments were −¥4,472mn in FY2/26 (principally basic-research and unallocated corporate costs, per the segment note). Source: company results via PL/segment sheet (tanshin, JPX archive); Q2 FY2/27 tanshin.

## Technology frame: mechatronics, i³-Mechatronics and physical AI; production

Yaskawa's strategic vocabulary is unusually consistent over decades, and it maps directly onto the product architecture. 'Mechatronics' — mechanism plus electronics — was coined inside the company by engineer Tetsuro Mori in 1969 (trademark registered 1972) for the idea of controlling mechanical devices precisely with electric power. The current solution concept, i³-Mechatronics, adds digital data management to that base: the three 'i's are integrated, intelligent and innovative, and the commercial claim is that because Yaskawa makes the servo, the controller and the robot, it can collect and use production data across the cell to solve customer problems — quality, traceability, labour shortage — rather than selling components alone. Vision 2035 (May 2026) extends this to 'i³-Singularity' — i³-Mechatronics combined with AI — and defines physical AI as realising use cases in areas where automation was traditionally difficult by integrating Yaskawa products with AI, embodied in the MOTOMAN NEXT AI robot; the company is also developing actuators aimed at humanoid-robot applications and ran field deployment of a MOTOMAN NEXT strawberry sorting and packaging solution in H2 FY2/27 planning (briefing materials). Production is centred on Kitakyushu (the Yukuhashi and, from 2026, Robot Factory No. 5 and the Minamiyukuhashi System Engineering factory) with manufacturing and sales bases worldwide; the group cites business hubs in 29 countries and production bases in 12 countries (company profile materials). Unit production volumes, capacity and utilisation are not disclosed in the tanshin and are therefore not quantified in this report. Source: company history and plan materials (Yaskawa Global Site; Vision 2035 / Dash 35 release, 22 May 2026); FY2026 1H briefing materials.

## 4. Market and value chain

Yaskawa sells into the factory-automation value chain at its highest-value layer: the components that make machines move precisely (servos, controllers, drives) and the robots that use those same components, sold both to end-users and, for components, to other equipment makers. Demand is a derived function of manufacturing capex, and the current cycle is being pulled by AI-related investment: the company reports data-centre and semiconductor capex as the driver of its record order intake, with servo demand from machine tools and electronic-components equipment and drive demand from data-centre cooling in the same wave (Q2 FY2/27 tanshin; briefing materials). Third-party market sizing is consistent with a mid-single-digit structural growth market rather than a boom market: Global Market Insights estimates the AC servo motors and drives market at USD 16.3bn in 2025, growing at a 6.4% CAGR to USD 30.8bn by 2035, with **Yaskawa the market leader at over 9% share in 2025** and the top five suppliers (ABB, Siemens, Yaskawa, Panasonic Industry, Mitsubishi Electric) holding 33% combined (GMI industry analysis, accessed November 2026 — a third-party estimate, not company data). In industrial robots, industry commentary groups Yaskawa with FANUC, ABB and KUKA as the 'big four' (PitchBook company profile, November 2026); one 2026 industry analysis puts FANUC at roughly 17% of global robot shipments and Yaskawa at roughly 8%, with the leading group together above 40% (Japonity, May 2026 — third-party estimate).

Competition differs by segment. In Motion Control the rivals are Mitsubishi Electric, Siemens, ABB, Panasonic Industry and FANUC (in controllers and compact automation), plus Chinese servo makers competing on price in China — the market where Yaskawa is pushing its Σ-Mx strategic product (briefing materials). In Robotics the rivals are FANUC (the scale and margin benchmark, with its own CNC ecosystem), ABB Robotics, KUKA and Mitsubishi Electric, with collaborative-robot specialists and Chinese makers pressing from below; Yaskawa's differentiation claim is the depth of its own motion technology inside the robot — its servos and control are developed in-house rather than bought in. Entry barriers are the accumulated servo-control know-how, installed-base trust in uptime-critical lines, global service networks and application engineering; the substitute risk is less a rival product than a technology shift — integrated drives, direct-drive and software-defined motion, and now AI-native robot platforms (including humanoids) that could re-draw which layer of the stack captures value. That is precisely the shift Dash 35's physical-AI programme, and its strategic-investment envelope for external AI technology, is designed to defend against. Two sensitivities follow for the equity: first, China is both a top-three revenue location (23.4% of H1 FY2/27 revenue, briefing materials) and the most contested market; second, the demand now driving orders — data centres and semiconductors — is concentrated, capex-lumpy AI investment, and order intake by segment, published quarterly in briefing materials, is the earliest observable if it pauses. Sources: company tanshin and briefing materials, October 2026; GMI and other third-party estimates as cited and dated in the text.

## 7. Strategy and mid-term plan — Vision 2035 and Dash 35

On 22 May 2026 Yaskawa announced a new long-term business plan, 'Vision 2035' (FY2/27–FY2/36 in the company's FY2026–FY2035 naming), and its first four-year mid-term plan, 'Dash 35' (FY2/27–FY2/30), succeeding the 'Challenge 25 Plus' plan that ran to FY2/26. The starting point is candid in the numbers if not in a formal scorecard in the sources used here: FY2/26, the predecessor plan's final year, closed with revenue of ¥542,122mn and an operating margin of 8.7%, well below the profitability the group had printed in FY2/23 (12.3%) — the new plan is framed as a profitability restoration first and a growth plan second.

Dash 35 targets, for FY2/30 (company plan targets, not J-Core Fundamental Research estimates): revenue ¥650,000mn (from ¥542,100mn in FY2/26, a 4.6% four-year CAGR), operating profit ¥100,000mn — a record high, 2.1x FY2/26 — for an operating margin of 15.4% (from 8.7%), ROE of 12% or more (from 7.7%), ROIC of 11% or more (from 6.9%) and a dividend payout ratio of 40% or more (FY2/26 actual: 50.0% on depressed earnings; FY2/27 forecast: 39.3%). Plan FX assumptions for FY2/30 are USD/JPY 145, EUR/JPY 170, CNY/JPY 20.50 and KRW/JPY 0.105. Vision 2035 beyond the plan sets **an operating margin of 20% or more and a payout ratio of 40% or more by FY2/36** as its financial targets. The five Dash 35 policies are: (1) develop the physical-AI market — expand the adaptive AI robot MOTOMAN NEXT, build physical-AI markets including humanoid robots through key components such as advanced actuators, and open previously un-automatable areas with partners; (2) expand implementation of i³-Mechatronics solutions, leveraging regional strengths; (3) world-leading new product development from core technology, operating data and AI; (4) expand new mechatronics applications — domestic agriculture automation and medical/laboratory automation with partners; and (5) evolve YDX (the group's digital transformation, of which the new ERP system is part) and i³-Singularity. Secondary reporting on the plan cites total investment of about ¥250bn over the four years, of which about ¥120bn is strategic investment for acquiring physical-AI technology, alliances and M&A (press summary of company plan materials, October 2026 — flagged as secondary; the primary release summary used here confirms the targets above but does not restate the investment envelope). The FY2/27 guidance in Chapter 2 is the plan's first year: it already requires the operating margin to recover to 9.6%, and the segment guidance requires Motion Control to deliver ¥39,400mn of the ¥57,500mn group operating profit. The arithmetic of Dash 35 is therefore back-loaded and Motion-Control-led: roughly ¥42,500mn of the ¥52,700mn operating-profit increase targeted by FY2/30 must come after FY2/27. Source: company release 'New Long-term Business Plan Vision 2035 and New Mid-term Business Plan Dash 35', 22 May 2026; FY2026 1H briefing materials (company, October 2026).

## 8. Other information

History. Yaskawa was founded on 16 July 1915 in Kitakyushu by Keiichiro (Daigoro) Yasukawa, growing out of the Yasukawa family's mining interests as a maker of electrical equipment for coal mining; its first order product, a three-phase induction motor, was delivered in 1917, and the shares were first listed in 1949. The milestones that built the current group: the word 'mechatronics' coined in-house in 1969; Japan's first all-electric industrial robot, the MOTOMAN-L10, in 1977; the Sigma series AC servo line launched in 1992, still the flagship servo family; cumulative MOTOMAN shipments of 500,000 units in February 2021; and the i³-Mechatronics solution concept, now extended toward physical AI under Vision 2035. The head office remains at 2-1 Kurosakishiroishi, Yahatanishi-ku, Kitakyushu, Fukuoka. Source: company history materials (Yaskawa Global Site, accessed November 2026); company releases.
